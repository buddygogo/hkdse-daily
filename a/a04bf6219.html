According to a recent report, kindergarten teachers across Hong Kong are increasingly required to work late into the night hosting festive galas and open days without any additional pay, as preschools compete fiercely to attract prospective pupils amid a deepening enrolment crisis. It is widely argued that this unpaid overtimeextra work hours for which no additional wages are paid無償超時工作 has quietly become an industry norm, driven largely by Hong Kong's plunging birth ratea rapidly and sharply falling number of births急劇下降的出生率, which has left many kindergartens scrambling to fill their classrooms amid an admission cruncha sudden, severe shortage of new student enrolments收生荒.

Teachers interviewed described single-handedly building elaborate decorations and moving heavy furniture with little janitorial support, often remaining on campus past nine or ten at night without overtime pay or compensatory leave. Critics contend that such practices amount to exploitation disguised as dedicationunfair treatment of workers presented instead as admirable commitment被包裝成敬業精神的剝削, placing an unsustainable burden on an already under-resourced professionan occupation lacking sufficient funding, staff or support資源不足的行業. Nevertheless, school operators maintain that hosting eye-catching promotional events has become an unavoidable necessity in a shrinking market where every enrolment counts.

By the same token, labour advocates argue that the sector's working conditions cannot be divorced from the wider demographic pressures reshaping Hong Kong's education landscape. It is often observed that when institutions are forced to compete for a shrinking pool ofstruggle against rivals over a decreasing number of爭奪日益減少的…… pupils, front-line staff frequently absorb the resulting pressure. Whether the Education Bureau will introduce statutory safeguardslegally required protections法定保障 for early-years educators, or leave the matter to market forces, remains to be seen — a question with real consequences for the wellbeing of those shaping Hong Kong's youngest learners.

中文翻譯

根據一份最新報告,香港幼稚園教師愈來愈經常被要求通宵工作,籌辦節慶聯歡及開放日,卻沒有任何額外薪酬,原因是幼稚園在收生危機日益嚴峻下,爭相吸引準學生入讀。輿論普遍認為,這種無償超時工作已悄然成為行業常態,主因是香港急劇下降的出生率,令不少幼稚園在收生荒中疲於奔命,苦苦填滿課室。

受訪教師形容,她們要獨自搭建精巧裝飾、搬動沉重傢俱,卻幾乎沒有工友協助,往往要留在校園直至晚上九、十時,卻沒有超時津貼或補假。批評者認為,這種做法等同於被包裝成敬業精神的剝削,為本已資源不足的行業加上難以承受的重擔。然而,辦學團體堅稱,在萎縮的市場中,舉辦吸引眼球的宣傳活動已成為不可避免的生存之道。

同樣地,勞工權益倡導者認為,這個行業的工作狀況不能脫離更廣泛的人口壓力去理解。有人指出,當機構被迫爭奪日益減少的學生時,前線員工往往要承受隨之而來的壓力。教育局最終會否為幼教工作者引入法定保障,還是任由市場力量主導,仍有待觀察——這個問題對塑造香港最年幼學習者的人,有著實質的影響。
