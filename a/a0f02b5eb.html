The Development Bureau has come under renewed scrutiny after confirming a last-minute U-turna reversal of an earlier decision or policy政策上的大轉變 on plans for the new Pak Shek Kok railway station, with public housing quietly dropped from the site in favour of private flats. Permanent Secretary for Development Doris Ho Pui-ling told reporters on Friday that the station, part of the East Rail Line extension works and earmarkedset aside for a particular purpose指定用途的；預留的 to open as early as 2033, would instead be financed by allowing the project's developer to build and sell private housing on the surrounding land.

It is widely argued that such reversals undermine public trust in long-term planning, yet officials insisted the decision was pragmatic rather than political. Ho explained that the area around the proposed station currently lacks the requisiterequired; necessary for a particular purpose必需的；所需的 community facilities — schools, clinics and transport links — to support a sizeable public housing estate, and that building purely private flats would better align withto be consistent or in agreement with與……一致；配合 existing development in the neighbourhood. Nevertheless, lawmakers have pressed the bureau to explain why the original mixed-use blueprint, which had promised a share of subsidised flats, was not honoured.

By the same token, the government was keen to allayto reduce fear, worry or doubt減輕；緩解（憂慮） public anxiety over the city's overall housing supply. Officials stressed that the revised proposal would not jeopardiseto put something at risk of being harmed or destroyed危害；使陷入危險 the administration's ten-year target of delivering 420,000 flats, including 294,000 public units, by 2036, since sufficient land has reportedly already been securedobtained or guaranteed, especially through effort獲得；確保 elsewhere to meet that quota.
