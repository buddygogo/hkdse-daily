A dispute over a multimillion-dollar lottery prize has landed a Hong Kong man in police custody, after he allegedly refused to share a Mark Six jackpot worth approximately HK$30 million with fourteen of his colleagues. According to police, the group, made up of staff members from the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department, had reportedly pooled their moneycombined funds from several people for a shared purpose集合各人的金錢作共同用途 to purchase the winning ticket together, with the 36-year-old man responsible for buying the ticket and holding the bank account into which the prize was ultimately paid.

Nevertheless, once the ticket proved to be a winner, the man allegedly refused to distribute the winnings evenly among the group, despite a meeting having been convened in an attempt to resolve the disputea disagreement or conflict between parties爭議或衝突 amicably. A 53-year-old colleague subsequently reported the matter to police, prompting an investigation on suspicion of theft. It is widely argued that this case serves as a cautionary tale about the importance of establishing clear, written agreements before entering into any form of shared financial venture, however informal it may seem. By the same token, the incident highlights a recurring legal grey area in Hong Kong: verbal arrangements between friends or colleagues, though seemingly built on mutual trust, often carry little weight without documented proof, leaving aggrieved parties with limited recourse besides pursuing costly and time-consuming legal action. Critics contend that greater public awareness of such risks could help prevent similar disputes from arising in the future.
