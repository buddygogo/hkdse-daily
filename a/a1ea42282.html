English

A fire broke out on Monday on the roof of the third incineration module at I·PARK1, Hong Kong's first waste-to-energy incineratora plant that burns rubbish to generate electricity while reducing the volume sent to landfill轉廢為能焚化爐, built on an artificial island off Shek Kwu Chau at a cost of HK$31.3 billion. Firefighters raised a No. 3 alarmthe fire service's third-highest emergency classification, indicating a large-scale blaze三級火警 as thick black smoke billowed across the water, and close to 930 workers had to leave the site by boat. A 60-year-old staff member was airlifted to hospitaltransported urgently by helicopter to receive medical treatment以直升機送院, reportedly after suffering heatstroke while helping to tackle the blaze. The plant suspended operationstemporarily stopped running, pending an investigation暫停運作 to allow an investigation, barely three weeks after it began accepting household rubbish.

The fire has reignited a long-running debate over whether the government is leaning too heavily on incineration to solve the city's rubbish crisis. Environmentalists argue that burning waste merely converts one problem into another, since the bottom ashthe toxic solid residue left after waste is burned, which requires special treatment爐底灰 left behind is toxic and must be handled with extra care, trucked away and disposed of at specially lined sites. A former lawmaker said the shutdown exposed the risk of concentrating so much disposal capacity in a single plant, and urged officials to put far greater weight on cutting municipal solid wastethe everyday rubbish produced by homes and businesses in a city都市固體廢物 at source rather than simply burning whatever households throw away.

Others counter that abandoning incineration is not realistic. Hong Kong generates roughly 15,725 tonnes of rubbish daily, and the city's two remaining landfills are running out of landfill capacitythe amount of rubbish a landfill site can still hold before it is full堆填區容量, with one projected to reach saturation within years. A second incinerator is already being planned for Tsang Tsui in Tuen Mun to help the government meet its zero-waste goalthe government's target of eventually sending no untreated rubbish to landfill零廢堆填目標. Supporters note that even a lengthy shutdown at Shek Kwu Chau would not change the underlying arithmetic: without somewhere to send the rubbish households already produce, Hong Kong has little choice but to keep investing in incineration alongside any reduction drive.

中文翻譯

香港首座轉廢為能焚化爐「I·PARK1」星期一（廿六日）發生火警，火場位於石鼓洲人工島上，造價高達三百一十三億港元的設施第三個焚化模組天台。消防處將火警升為三級火警，濃烈黑煙在海面上翻騰，近九百三十名工人須乘船撤離。一名六十歲男職員懷疑在協助救火期間中暑，獲以直升機送院治理。焚化爐啟用接收家居垃圾僅三星期，事後隨即暫停運作以便調查。

這場火警再次掀起爭論：政府是否過度倚賴焚化解決本港垃圾危機。環保人士指出，燃燒垃圾只是將一個問題轉化為另一個問題，因為燃燒後剩下的爐底灰含有毒性，必須經特別處理，用車運走並棄置於有特殊襯墊的堆填區。一名前立法會議員表示，今次停運暴露將大量處理量集中於單一設施的風險，促請官員更著力從源頭減少都市固體廢物產生，而非單純燃燒住戶拋棄的一切。

亦有意見認為，全面放棄焚化並不現實。香港每日產生約一萬五千七百二十五公噸垃圾，餘下兩個堆填區的堆填區容量日漸見底，其中一個預計數年內即告飽和。政府已計劃在屯門曾咀興建第二座焚化爐，協助達成零廢堆填目標。支持者指出，即使石鼓洲長時間停運，亦無法改變背後的現實：在找到出路安置住戶已經製造的垃圾之前，香港除了繼續投資焚化設施並推動源頭減廢，幾乎別無選擇。
