Victoria Park has been transformed into a dazzling showcase of lantern craftsmanshipthe skilled art of designing and making decorative lanterns花燈工藝 this Mid-Autumn Festival, as the Hong Kong Tourism Board stages its inaugural International Lantern Spectacularthe first-ever event of its kind首屆國際花燈盛會. Running from 17 to 27 September under the theme of "Reunion", the free exhibition brings together more than 3,000 lanterns from 20 countries and regions, ranging from delicate silk creations from South Korea to vibrant patchwork designs from Brazil. It is widely regarded as one of the most ambitious cultural undertakings the Tourism Board has attempted in recent years.

The centrepiece of the display features four giant Qinhuai lanterns crafted by artisans from Nanjing using a technique dating back some 1,700 years. Nevertheless, local heritage has not been overshadowed by international contributions: hundreds of lanterns handcrafted by Hong Kong artisans pay tribute to beloved local delicacies, with playful designs incorporating egg waffles, milk tea and curry fishballs. By the same token, the festivities extend beyond Victoria Park, with the centuries-old Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, now inscribed on the national list of intangible cultural heritagecultural practices and traditions, as opposed to physical artefacts, recognised as worth preserving非物質文化遺產, due to wind through the streets from 24 to 26 September.
