Hong Kong's return to the classroom this September has been overshadowed by tragedy. Within the first week of the new academic year, at least seven cases of student self-harm or suicide were reported across the city, three of which proved fatal. It is widely argued that such incidents, though distressing in isolation, point to a far more entrenched systemic probleman issue rooted in the structure of a system rather than in isolated individual cases系統性問題 within the city's education culture, one in which academic achievement has become almost synonymous with personal worth.

Critics contend that Hong Kong's intensely examination-oriented culturean educational environment that places overwhelming emphasis on test results以考試為導向的文化 leaves little room for students who struggle to keep pace. Data compiled by the University of Hong Kong's Centre for Suicide Research and Prevention reveal that some 30 per cent of suicide victims under the age of 15 were enrolled in Band One schools, suggesting that academic peer pressurestress arising from competition with high-achieving classmates學業同儕壓力 may weigh most heavily precisely where expectations are highest. Nevertheless, education authorities caution against viewing school banding alone as the root cause, noting that parental expectations and students' own self-imposed expectationsstandards a person sets for themselves, often unrealistically high自我要求 compound the pressure considerably.

By the same token, schools have not been passive in the face of this crisis. A Three-tier School-based Emergency Mechanism, regularisedmade a standard, permanent part of practice rather than a temporary measure常規化 across all secondary schools during the 2025/26 school year, has now been extended on a trial basis to upper primary levels, offering earlier identification of at-risk studentsindividuals identified as being in particular danger of harm高危學生 and swifter referral to counselling. Even so, commentators insist that lasting change demands more than emergency intervention: it requires a fundamental shift towards fostering resiliencehelping someone develop the ability to recover from difficulty培養抗逆力 and genuine emotional well-beinga state of psychological and emotional health情緒健康 throughout a child's schooling, rather than treating mental health merely as a crisis to be managed after the fact.
