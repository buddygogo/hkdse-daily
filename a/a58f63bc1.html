English

A newly published analysis by Hong Kong Free Press has laid bare the extent to which the city's climate has shifted over the past two decades. Whereas Hong Kong recorded an average of just 13.4 “very hot days” per year in the 2000s, days on which the maximum temperature reaches 33 degrees or above, this figure has quadrupledincreased to four times the original amount增加至四倍 to more than 50 such days annually since 2020. The summer of 2026 alone brought a record-breaking heatwavea period of extreme heat that surpasses previous records破紀錄熱浪, underscoring a trend that meteorologists warn is unlikely to reverse.

It is widely argued that rainfall patterns have grown equally erraticirregular and unpredictable反覆無常. This year has seen 15 red rainstorm warningsan official alert for heavy rain in Hong Kong, second-highest tier紅色暴雨警告 and three black rainstorm warningsthe highest-tier official alert for extremely heavy rain in Hong Kong黑色暴雨警告, the second-highest tally on record for both categories. In June, the Observatory issued two black rainstorm warnings within a single day for the first time in the city's history, while July's rainfall total ranked as the second-highest ever recorded for that month. Such extremes, scientists contend, are consistent with broader predictions of a warming climate marked by intensified precipitation eventsinstances of rainfall that have become more severe加劇的降雨事件.

Nevertheless, there are grounds for cautious optimism: overall air quality has continued to improve, with concentrations of major pollutants steadily declining in recent years. By the same token, the government's pledge to achieve carbon neutralityachieving a net-zero balance of carbon dioxide emissions碳中和 before 2050 remains, critics argue, short on concrete implementation details. As extreme weather becomes an increasingly familiar feature of Hong Kong life, the pressure is mounting on policymakers to translate long-term targets into tangible, near-term action, particularly given the disproportionate impact of heatwaves and flooding on vulnerable communitiesgroups of people at greater risk of harm from adverse conditions弱勢社群 across the city.

中文翻譯

《香港自由新聞》最新發表的一項分析,清楚揭示本港氣候在過去二十年間出現的轉變。香港在2000年代,「極端炎熱日數」(即日最高溫度達33度或以上的日子)平均每年只有13.4日,但自2020年起,這個數字已經增加至四倍,達每年逾五十日。單在2026年夏天,本港便經歷了一場破紀錄熱浪,凸顯出一個氣象學家警告難以逆轉的趨勢。

輿論普遍認為,降雨模式同樣變得反覆無常。今年本港錄得十五次紅色暴雨警告及三次黑色暴雨警告,兩項數字均是歷來第二高紀錄。六月份,天文台首次在同一日內兩度發出黑色暴雨警告,為本港歷史首次;而七月份的雨量總數,亦是歷來該月第二高紀錄。科學家認為,這些極端現象,與氣候持續變暖、加劇的降雨事件日趨頻繁的更廣泛預測相符。

然而,也有值得審慎樂觀的地方:整體空氣質素持續改善,主要污染物濃度近年穩步下降。同樣地,政府承諾在2050年前達致碳中和的目標,批評者則認為仍然缺乏具體落實細節。隨著極端天氣愈趨成為香港生活中司空見慣的一環,政策制訂者正承受與日俱增的壓力,須將長遠目標轉化為實質的近期行動,尤其考慮到熱浪及水浸對弱勢社群造成的不成比例影響。
