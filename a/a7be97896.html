English

In the fishing village of Lau Fau Shan, where families have farmed oysters for some 700 years, a centuries-old trade passed downhanded from one generation to the next within a family or community代代傳承 through generations is being quietly reinvented in a university laboratory. Chan Kwok-leung, a sixth-generation farmer known locally as Brother Leung, has spent the past decade confronting a problem no amount of traditional skill could solve on its own: annual oyster die-offsevents in which large numbers of a species die at the same time蠔隻大量死亡 that farmers say can wipe out as much as 80 percent of a season's stock, driven by warmer, saltier water and shrinking breeding grounds.

Researchers at the University of Hong Kong believe part of the answer lies in genetics rather than technique. Using genomic selectiona breeding method that uses genetic data to predict and choose the hardiest individuals基因組選育, scientists have bred what they call the “HKU Super Oyster”, a strain engineereddeliberately designed or developed to have specific, useful qualities改良培育 to tolerate high-salinity conditions at roughly three to four times the survival rate of ordinary local oysters. Early trials are under way on farmers' rafts, though growers caution that judging the strain's true resiliencethe ability to survive and recover from difficult or harsh conditions抗逆能力 will take at least a full year, since oysters raised from lab-selected seed must still survive an entire natural growing cycle before anyone can call the experiment a success.

For Lau Fau Shan, where the number of working oyster farms has dwindled for decades, the stakes extend well beyond one harvest. A trade once central to the area's livelihooda person's or community's means of earning the income needed to live生計 and identity now survives mostly as memory for many younger residents, and whether science can restore its viabilitybring something back to a state in which it can function and continue successfully挽回其可持續性 may determine if the 700-year-old industry becomes a living trade again or fades into a story told at food-heritage exhibitions rather than practised on the water.

中文翻譯

在流浮山漁村——當地家庭養蠔的歷史已長達約七百年——一項代代傳承下來的古老行業，正悄悄在大學實驗室裡經歷一場重塑。第六代蠔農、本地人稱之為「良哥」的陳國樑，過去十年一直在設法解決一個單靠傳統技藝無法解決的問題：蠔農稱每年的蠔隻大量死亡情況，有時可令整季收成損失多達八成，原因包括水溫及鹽度上升，以及養殖場範圍日漸收窄。

香港大學的研究人員相信，部分答案在於基因，而非養殖技巧。科學家利用基因組選育技術，培育出所謂的「港大超級蠔」——一種經過改良培育、能在高鹽度環境下生存的品種，存活率約為本地普通蠔的三至四倍。相關品種現正在蠔農的養殖棚架上進行初步試養，但蠔農提醒，要判斷這個品種的真正抗逆能力，至少需要一整年時間，因為以實驗室篩選種苗養殖的蠔，仍須經歷完整的自然生長週期，才能判斷這次實驗是否成功。

對流浮山而言，隨著經營中的蠔場數目數十年來持續減少，這次嘗試的意義遠超一季收成。這個曾是當地生計與身份象徵核心的行業，對許多年輕居民而言，如今大多只剩記憶；科學能否成功挽回其可持續性，將會決定這個七百年歷史的行業，日後能否重新成為一門活生生的行業，還是只淪為美食文化展覽中被講述的故事，而非在水上真正被實踐的技藝。
