English

It is widely reported that the Education Bureau has given the go-ahead for eight so-called “zero-class” primary schools — institutions that failed to secure sufficient enrolmentsucceed in attracting enough students未能取得足夠收生人數 to open a Primary One class this September — to merge with neighbouring institutions, while two others will run self-financing classes and a further four are set to be phased outgradually closed down over time逐步淘汰 altogether. The wave of closures and mergers, among them the case of Fresh Fish Traders School joining forces with a nearby institution, is widely attributed to Hong Kong’s record-low birth ratethe lowest number of births ever recorded歷來最低出生率, with only 31,714 babies registered last year.

Critics contend that such large-scale restructuring, however necessary on paper, risks erodinggradually wearing away or weakening侵蝕；削弱 decades of institutional identity and alumni attachment overnight. Teachers at affected schools have voiced concern over job security, while parents worry about the disruption of moving children mid-way through their primary education to unfamiliar campuses. Nevertheless, the Education Bureau has sought to cushion the impactsoften or reduce the negative effect of something緩衝影響 by granting merged schools a three-year grace period during which class sizes are not strictly enforced, allowing time to iron outto resolve small problems or difficulties解決；理順 practical matters such as curriculum alignment and staff arrangements.

By the same token, education commentators argue that mergers alone are but a stopgap rather than a genuine solution to a much deeper demographic predicamenta difficult and unpleasant situation困境. With Hong Kong’s population continuing to age and its workforce contracting, some have called for a more holisticconsidering the whole picture rather than just individual parts全面的；整體的 population policy that goes beyond the education sector alone. It remains to be seen whether the current wave of consolidation will be sufficient to sustain smaller neighbourhood schools in the years ahead, or whether further closures are simply being deferreddelayed or postponed to a later time推遲 rather than avoided.

中文翻譯

據廣泛報導,教育局已批准八間所謂「零班」小學——即未能在今年九月取得足夠收生人數開辦小一班級的學校——與鄰近學校合併,另有兩間學校將開辦自資班,餘下四間則將逐步停辦。這一波停辦及合併潮,包括鮮魚行學校與附近學校合併的個案,普遍歸因於香港歷來最低的出生率,去年全港僅登記31,714名新生嬰兒。

評論者認為,此類大規模重組,縱使在紙面上有其必要性,卻有可能在一夜之間侵蝕數十年來建立的機構身份認同及校友情誼。受影響學校的教師表達了對職位保障的關注,家長則擔心子女需在小學學業中途轉往陌生校園所帶來的適應問題。然而,教育局試圖緩衝影響,向合併學校提供為期三年的寬限期,期間班級人數不會被嚴格執行,以爭取時間理順課程銜接及教職員安排等實際事宜。

同樣地,教育界評論者認為,單靠合併只是權宜之計,而非解決更深層人口困境的真正方案。隨著香港人口持續老化、勞動人口不斷萎縮,有意見呼籲制訂更全面的人口政策,範疇須超越教育界別本身。現時這一波整合措施,能否足以維持小型地區學校在未來數年的生存,抑或只是將進一步的停辦推遲而非避免,仍有待觀察。
