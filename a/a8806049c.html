English

A new survey by researchers at Tung Wah College has found that many working caregivers in Hong Kong quietly avoid the very arrangements meant to help them, out of fear of being stigmatisedtreated as different or shameful because of a personal circumstance被污名化 for appearing less committed to their jobs. Among 566 working carers aged between 21 and 73 who took part, researchers found that more than half had never once used the flexible working arrangementswork schedules that can be adjusted to suit an employee's personal needs彈性工作安排 their employers formally offered, even when juggling full-time work with caring for an ageing parent, a sick spouse or a child with additional needs.

Among those who did use such measures, flexible hours proved the most popular, taken up by a third of respondents, followed by part-time work, working from home and a compressed working weeka schedule that condenses the usual working hours into fewer, longer days壓縮工作週. The payoff appeared measurable: carers who used these arrangements reported noticeably fewer depressive symptomssigns of depression, such as persistent low mood and loss of energy抑鬱症狀 and a lighter caregiving burdenthe physical, emotional and practical strain of caring for a dependent family member照顧者的負擔 than those who continued to shoulder caregiving dutiesto take on and bear responsibility for caring for someone肩負照顧責任 largely in silence, suggesting the real barrier standing between carers and relief is less about availability and more about workplace stigma.

Researchers also pointed to a structural problem layered on top of the cultural one: many support services for carers still operate only during standard office hoursthe usual 9-to-5 working schedule followed by most employers標準辦公時間, making them almost impossible for a working carer to reach without taking leave. The study's authors argue that closing this gap will require more than new policies on paper; it demands a genuine workplace culture shifta broader change in the shared attitudes and unwritten norms of a workplace職場文化轉變 that frees carers from the sense that asking for help will cost them their professional standing.

中文翻譯

東華學院一項最新調查發現，許多在職照顧者在香港其實甚少使用原本為他們而設的支援安排，原因是他們害怕因照顧家人而顯得對工作不夠投入，擔心因此被污名化。在566名年齡介乎21至73歲的在職照顧者當中，研究人員發現超過一半人從未使用過僱主正式提供的彈性工作安排，即使他們正同時兼顧全職工作，以及照顧年邁父母、患病配偶或有特殊需要的子女。

在曾經使用相關安排的受訪者當中，彈性上班時間最受歡迎，有三分之一受訪者採用，其次依次為兼職、在家工作，以及壓縮工作週。成效似乎相當明顯：曾使用這些安排的照顧者，其抑鬱症狀明顯較少，照顧者的負擔亦較輕，相比之下，那些長期獨自默默肩負照顧責任的照顧者則情況較差，反映照顧者與支援之間真正的障礙，與資源是否存在的關係較小，反而與職場污名更有關。

研究人員亦指出一個疊加於文化問題之上的結構性困難：許多為照顧者而設的支援服務，仍然只在標準辦公時間提供，令在職照顧者幾乎無法在不請假的情況下使用。研究作者認為，要彌合這道差距，單靠紙上政策並不足夠，必須有一場真正的職場文化轉變，讓照顧者不再擔心開口求助會損害自己的職業地位。
