It is widely reported that foreign carers recruited under a new government pilot scheme for elderly care will be paid ten to twenty per cent more than existing domestic helpers, according to Secretary for Labour and Welfare Chris Sun. The initiative is intended to address Hong Kong's growing ageing populationa demographic trend in which the proportion of elderly people within a society steadily increases人口老化 by attracting carers with specialised training rather than relying solely on the existing domestic helper scheme.

Critics contend that the pay differential could create friction within an already strained care sector, where existing helpers frequently perform similar duties without comparable recognition or wage paritythe state of receiving equal pay for work of equal value薪酬平等. Nevertheless, officials argue that requiring applicants to hold at least three years of relevant experience justifies the higher pay, since it reflects genuine specialised expertiseadvanced skills and knowledge developed through focused training in a particular field專業技能 rather than a general willingness to perform household duties.

By the same token, the scheme forms part of a broader package announced in this year's Policy Address to reinforce support for family carers, including an expanded Living Allowance Scheme for low-income households caring for elderly relatives. It is worth noting that such measures reflect growing recognition that informal caregivingunpaid care provided by family members rather than professional institutions非正式照顧 places a considerable and often invisible burden on working families.

One cannot deny that Hong Kong's care economy faces mounting strain as its population ages and its workforce shrinks. Whether higher wages alone can attract sufficient carers, or whether deeper structural reform of the sector is required, remains an important question for policymakers and families alike.

    The reform highlights how demographic ageingthe gradual increase in the average age of a population人口老化現象 is reshaping labour demand, requiring society to reconsider how care work is valued and rewarded.
  • Economic dimension 經濟層面
    Higher wages for specialised carers may improve recruitment, but they also raise the cost of care provisionthe total financial expense involved in delivering care services照顧服務成本 for families who must eventually bear part of that expense.

    Paying new carers more than existing helpers for comparable work raises questions of distributive fairnessthe principle that benefits and burdens should be shared among people in a just manner分配公平 within an already vulnerable migrant workforce.

    Family members providing unpaid care frequently experience caregiver fatiguephysical and emotional exhaustion resulting from the sustained demands of caring for another person照顧者疲勞, underscoring the need for allowances that acknowledge this hidden cost.
