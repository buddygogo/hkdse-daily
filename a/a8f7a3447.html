English

Hong Kong's drive to stamp out substandard housingliving accommodation that fails to meet basic safety, size or hygiene standards不合標準的住房 hit an unexpected snag this week when the Housing Bureau revealed that owners of fourteen subsidised sale flats, originally allocated under schemes meant to help low-income families onto the property ladder, had applied to convert their units into legally registered subdivided homes. The disclosure, made at a Legislative Council housing panel, has raised concerns that a policy designed to weed outto identify and remove something unwanted from a larger group清除 dangerous "shoebox" rentals may inadvertently be legitimising arrangements that were never meant to generate rental income in the first place.

Lawmaker Mark Chong Ho-fung argued that flats sold under the Home Ownership Scheme amount to a form of social welfare provisiona benefit or form of support provided by the government to help people in need社會福利安排 and should never be treated as an income-generating assetproperty or possession kept mainly for the money it can earn可產生收入的資產, let alone one subdivided and let to tenants. Housing Secretary Winnie Ho acknowledged the tension, confirming that leasing a subsidised flat before the land premiumthe extra sum an owner must pay the government to remove resale restrictions on a subsidised flat地價 has been paid remains unlawful regardless of whether the unit has since been subdivided. Officials are nonetheless treading a fine linetrying carefully to balance two conflicting concerns or interests走在微妙的分界線上: refusing the applications outright could leave existing subdivided tenants, many of whom are themselves low-income, suddenly without a home.

Housing advocates warn the episode exposes a regulatory blind spota gap in a system of rules that nobody anticipated or planned for監管盲點 that the new subdivided-flat regime failed to anticipate, and that allowing even a handful of exemptions could set an unwelcome precedentan earlier decision that may be used, unhelpfully, to justify similar actions in future不良先例 for other owners hoping to skirt the premium requirement. Others counter that punishing tenants for their landlords' non-compliancefailure to follow a rule, law or regulation違規 would be unduly harsh given the city's acute shortage of affordable rentals. With the grace period still under review, the case has become an uncomfortable test of whether Hong Kong's housing policy can protect both the integrity of public subsidies and the welfare of its most vulnerable renters.

中文翻譯

香港致力打擊不合標準的住房的行動，本周遇上意料之外的波折：房屋局披露，十四個原本根據資助計劃批予低收入家庭、助其踏上置業階梯的資助出售單位業主，申請將其單位登記為合法的分間單位。這項在立法會房屋事務委員會會議上披露的消息，令人擔心一項旨在清除危險「㓥房」租賃的政策，可能會意外地令一些本不應用作出租賺取收入的安排變得合法化。

議員莊豪峰認為，在資助出售房屋計劃下售出的單位屬於一種社會福利安排，絕不應被視為可產生收入的資產，更不應被分間出租。房屋及規劃地政司何永賢承認當中存在張力，並確認在未繳付地價前出租資助單位仍屬違法，不論該單位事後是否已被分間。然而，官員正走在微妙的分界線上：若全面拒絕有關申請，可能令現有的分間單位租戶（當中許多本身亦是低收入人士）突然無家可歸。

房屋權益倡議人士警告，此事暴露出新分間單位制度未能預見的監管盲點，而即使只容許極少數豁免，亦可能為其他試圖逃避繳付地價的業主開創不良先例。另有意見認為，考慮到本港可負擔租盤嚴重短缺，若因業主違規而懲罰租戶，未免過於嚴苛。隨着寬限期的安排仍在檢討當中，此案已成為一次令人尷尬的考驗，考驗香港的房屋政策能否同時保障公共資助的完整性，以及照顧最脆弱租戶的福祉。
