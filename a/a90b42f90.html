English

Hong Kong has just endured one of its hottest and driest Septembers on record, according to the Hong Kong Observatory’s latest monthly reviewan official report summarising weather conditions over the past month月度回顧. The mean maximum temperature reached 31.8 degrees Celsius and the mean temperature stood at 28.8 degrees, both 0.9 to 1.3 degrees above normal and among the ten highest readings for September since records began. The mean minimum temperature of 27.1 degrees was the fifth-highest on record. Rainfall, meanwhile, totalled a mere 94.3 millimetres, a fraction ofa small part of; much less than僅為……的一小部分 the usual 321.4 millimetres.

Forecasters attributed the dry spell to an anticyclone alofta mass of high-pressure air sitting high in the atmosphere, which tends to suppress rain高空氣壓脊, which kept skies clear and conditions fine for six consecutive days towards the end of the month. The dry September followed an equally punishingextremely harsh or difficult to endure難以忍受的，酷熱的 August, during which the Observatory’s headquarters logged an all-time high of 36.9 degrees and Sheung Shui soared to 39.8 degrees, the hottest reading there since automatic weather stations began operating.

Observatory director Chan Pak-wai noted that 2026’s annual mean temperature is expected to be above normal, placing the year among the ten hottest on record for the city. Such back-to-back extremesunusual or severe conditions happening one after another without a break連續出現的極端天氣 carry consequences well beyond discomfort: prolonged dryness can depleteto reduce the amount of something, often a resource, over time消耗，耗盡 reservoir levels and stress crops, while sustained heat raises the risk of heatstroke among outdoor workers and the elderly.
