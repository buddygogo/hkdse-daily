English

It is widely argued that few events capture the intangible cultural heritagea tradition or practice, rather than a physical object, passed down through generations非物質文化遺產 of Hong Kong quite as vividly as the Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance. Returning this week from 24 to 26 September to coincide with the Mid-Autumn Festival on 25 September, the dance traces its origins back to the 1880s, when villagers first performed the ritual in an effort to ward offto prevent something harmful from affecting you驅除 a plague believed to be haunting the community. Nearly a century and a half later, the tradition endures as one of the city’s most treasured cultural spectacles, formally inscribed on the National List of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

This year’s performance will, as always, feature a colossalextremely large in size or extent龐大的 67-metre dragon studded with more than 12,000 smouldering incense sticks, borne aloft by close to 300 performers as it winds through the narrow lanes of Wun Sha Street and Tung Lo Wan Road in Causeway Bay. Nevertheless, the celebrations are by no means confined to Tai Hang alone: on the evening of the festival itself, the dragon will process to Victoria Park, where it joins an inaugural international lantern spectacular featuring elaborate Qinhuai lanterns imported from Nanjing. By the same token, this fusion of neighbourhood ritual and citywide festivity illustrates how deeply Mid-Autumn traditions remain woven intoclosely combined with something, forming an essential part of it融入 the rhythm of Hong Kong life.

Critics contend that, amid rapid urban redevelopment, such traditions are increasingly vulnerable to erosion, making sustained community stewardshipthe careful and responsible management of something entrusted to your care悉心保育 all the more essential. It is nonetheless heartening that the Tai Hang Residents’ Welfare Association continues to pass the craft of dragon-making down to younger generations, ensuring that this raucousmaking a loud, disorderly noise喧鬧的 , incense-scented spectacle will continue to light up the autumn sky for years to come.

中文翻譯

普遍認為，少有活動能像大坑舞火龍般生動地展現香港的非物質文化遺產。活動今年將於9月24至26日回歸，配合9月25日的中秋節舉行，其起源可追溯至1880年代，當時村民首次表演這項儀式，希望以此驅除相傳纏繞社區的瘟疫。接近一個半世紀後，這項傳統依然是香港最珍貴的文化盛事之一，並已正式列入國家級非物質文化遺產名錄。

今年的表演一如以往，將展示一條龐大的67米長火龍，龍身插滿逾12,000枝燃點中的香枝，由近300名表演者高舉巡遊，蜿蜒穿過銅鑼灣浣沙街及銅鑼灣道的狹窄巷弄。然而，慶祝活動絕不僅限於大坑：在中秋節當晚，火龍將巡遊至維多利亞公園，與首屆國際花燈盛會匯合，當中展出由南京引入的精緻秦淮花燈。同樣道理，這種社區儀式與全港節慶的融合，正好說明中秋傳統如何深深融入香港生活的節奏。

評論者認為，在急速的市區重建下，這些傳統日益容易受到侵蝕，因此持續的社區悉心保育變得更加不可或缺。然而令人欣慰的是，大坑坊眾福利會持續將製作火龍的工藝傳承給年輕一代，確保這項喧鬧而充滿香火氣息的盛事，將在未來多年繼續照亮秋夜天空。
