It is widely reported that pet owners across Hong Kong may find their dogs reclassifiedplaced into a different official category重新分類 as fighting breeds during otherwise routine licensing and vaccination appointments. According to the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, this measure follows a marked upticka small increase輕微上升 in public reports concerning suspected fighting dogs, prompting officers to be deployed more frequently for on-site inspections. Principal veterinary officer Michelle Yeung explained that identification currently relies on visual assessment, whereby veterinarians evaluate a dog's head shape, stature and overall body structure to determine its breed category.

Critics contend that such subjective, appearance-based assessments risk producing inconsistent outcomesresults that vary or contradict each other不一致的結果, particularly for mixed-breed dogs whose ancestry cannot be determined at a glance. Nevertheless, officials point out that the system offers a practical opportunity for oversight: since dog licences must be renewed every three years alongside a rabies booster vaccination, staff can observe each animal at that time and revise its classification if necessary. Owners of dogs confirmed as fighting breeds are not required to surrender their pets, provided they comply with all relevant restrictions and safety requirements.

By the same token, this policy tightening comes in the wake of a fatal attack last month in Ta Kwu Ling, in which a woman running a licensed dog shelter was mauled to death by an animal in her care, an incident that prompted the government to pledge a review of the legal definition of “fighting dogs” and to submit legislative amendments within the year. The episode illustrates how a single tragedy can galvaniseshock into action激勵；促使採取行動 swift regulatory change, while also raising important questions about public safety, animal welfare and the limits of visual identification methods.
