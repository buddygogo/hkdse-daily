English

Hong Kong is preparing to overhaul how it funds its nine flagship performing arts groups, shifting away from what has long been a near-automatic annual subsidymoney given by the government to support an organisation's running costs資助 towards a more flexible, merit-based system. Under the proposed changes, announced as part of a wider cultural policy review, the government will inject HK$200 million into the Arts and Sport Development Fund while introducing an in-and-out mechanisma system that allows organisations to gain or lose funded status depending on their performance能上能落機制 tied to artistic standards rather than guaranteed continuity.

The change is designed to free up room, and public money, for emerging companiesorganisations that are newly established or still building a reputation新興藝團 that have long struggled to compete with established institutions for recognition and resources. Yet the proposal immediately raises a thorny question: how exactly should officials measure artistic meritthe quality or value of a creative work, judged by its artistic rather than commercial success藝術價值 in a field where critical acclaim and box-office returnsthe amount of money a performance earns from ticket sales票房收入 frequently point in opposite directions? A daring, experimental production might win prizes while playing to half-empty houses, whereas a crowd-pleasing commercial hit may contribute little to the city's artistic reputation.

Directors of the nine designated groups, which include leading orchestras, dance and opera companies, have voiced cautious support paired with concern. They note that a prolonged economic downturn has made private fundraisingthe raising of money from businesses or individuals rather than the government私人集資 harder than at any point in recent memory, and have urged policymakers to set realistic targets and take a long-term view rather than judging companies on a single disappointing season. The Culture, Sports and Tourism Bureau says a fuller framework for assessing performance, including a review of venue-booking rules, will follow in the coming months, alongside wider consultation with practitioners across dance, theatre, music and Cantonese opera before any final criteria are locked in.

中文翻譯

香港正準備全面革新資助九個旗艦表演藝術團體的方式，由一直以來近乎自動發放的年度資助，轉向較具彈性、以表現為本的制度。作為更廣泛文化政策檢討的一部分，政府將向藝術及體育發展基金注資二億港元，同時引入與藝術水平而非保證延續性掛鈎的能上能落機制。

這項改變旨在為長期難與主流機構爭奪認受及資源的新興藝團騰出空間及公共資源。然而，建議隨即引出一個棘手問題：當局究竟應如何衡量藝術價值，尤其是在藝評口碑與票房收入往往背道而馳的藝壇之中？一部大膽前衛的作品，或許能贏盡獎項卻門票只售出一半；相反，一部迎合大眾口味的商業鉅製，對本港藝術聲譽的貢獻卻可能相當有限。

九個指定藝團（包括多個主要樂團、舞團及歌劇團）的總監均表示審慎支持，但同時有所保留。他們指出，持續的經濟低迷令私人集資比近年任何時候都更加困難，並促請政策制定者訂立切合實際的目標，以長遠角度看待問題，而非單憑一季表現欠佳便作出判斷。文化體育及旅遊局表示，一套更完整的表現評估框架，包括場地租用制度的檢討，將於未來數月內公布，並會在落實最終準則前，就舞蹈、戲劇、音樂及粵劇等不同範疇，與業界人士展開更廣泛的諮詢。
