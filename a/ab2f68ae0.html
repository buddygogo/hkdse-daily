English

A lighting installation along the walking trail on Victoria Peak has triggered a wave of public anger, forcing the Hong Kong Tourism Board to backpedalto retreat from a previous position or plan改變立場；退讓 on part of the scheme. The project, intended to accompany a new light-and-sound show replacing the long-running “A Symphony of Lights” for this year’s WinterFest, involved installing projectors and fittings along Lugard Road and Harlech Road. Residents accused the board of fellingcutting down trees砍伐 mature trees and flowering shrubs to make way for the equipment, posting photographs online of bare patches where greenery once stood. The backlash grew so intense that related works were suspended pending a review.

The Tourism Board has defended its handling of the project, insisting it briefed the local district council as early as April and held further sessions with residents, government departments and tree experts in May and September. Yet a lawmaker disputedto question or challenge the truth of something質疑；反駁 this account, stating that Central and Western district councillor Jeremy Young Chit-on had not in fact been consulted at the outset. Secretary for Culture, Sports and Tourism Rosanna Law sought to calm tensions, stressing that “nothing is set in stone yet” and promising a broader round of consultation before any further discussion at the District Council.

On Saturday, Commissioner for Tourism Ann Chan walked the trail alongside a lawmaker and the district councillor to inspect the disputed stretch firsthand. Shortly afterwards, two large metal projector stands were taken down, though strings of lights remain fixed to several trees along the route. The episode illustrates the delicate balance that Hong Kong’s tourism authorities must strike between staging eye-catchingattracting attention through visual appeal引人注目的 attractions for visitors and safeguardingprotecting from harm or damage保護；維護 green spaces that residents treasure as part of the city’s natural heritage. Whether the remaining installations survive the promised review remains to be seen.

中文翻譯

太平山頂行人徑上的一項燈飾工程引發公眾強烈不滿，迫使香港旅遊發展局在部分方案上改變立場。該項目原意是配合今年冬日購物節取代長年舉辦的「幻彩詠香江」而設的全新燈光音樂表演，涉及在盧吉道及夏力道沿線安裝投影機及燈具。居民指控當局為安裝設備而砍伐多棵成年樹木及花叢，並在網上發布光禿禿地面的照片。公眾反彈之猛烈，令相關工程須暫時擱置以待檢討。

旅遊發展局為其處理方式辯護，堅稱早於四月已向當區區議會簡介計劃，並於五月及九月進一步與居民、政府部門及樹木專家會面。然而有議員質疑此說法，指中西區區議員楊哲安在最初階段根本未獲諮詢。文化體育及旅遊局局長羅淑佩試圖平息爭議，強調「一切尚未成為定局」，並承諾在區議會進一步討論前展開更廣泛的諮詢。

星期六，旅遊事務專員陳蓉聯同一名議員及該區區議員親身步行該行人徑視察爭議路段。事件發生後不久，兩個大型金屬投影機支架被拆除，但沿途仍有多棵樹木纏繞着燈飾。這次事件說明香港旅遊當局須在打造引人注目的旅客景點與保護居民珍視的綠色空間之間取得微妙平衡。餘下的裝置能否通過承諾的檢討,仍是未知數。
