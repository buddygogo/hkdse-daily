English

The death of a 93-year-old Kai Tak woman last week, after her live-in domestic helper was arrested on suspicion of assault, has reopened a painful debate about how Hong Kong supports the carers of its most isolated elderly residentsolder people with little regular contact with family, neighbours or social services孤身無援長者. The victim, who had lived alone and needed round-the-clock carecontinuous care provided at all hours of the day and night全天候照顧 after a stroke, had relied almost entirely on one helper for years. Lawmakers and welfare groups say the case, however it is ultimately resolved in court, exposes a wider gap in how the city monitors households where an elderly person's wellbeing rests on a single, often overstretched carer.

Among the proposals gaining traction is expanding an existing at-risk databasea record used by authorities to flag people who may need extra support or monitoring高風險個案資料庫, through which the Hospital Authority already alerts the Social Welfare Department when listed carers are hospitalised, to cover domestic helpers caring for seniors as well. Advocates also want regular home visits and encouragement for carers to attend community centres, so that a third partysomeone outside the immediate household who can observe a situation objectively第三方 has a chance to notice warning signs before a crisis develops. Migrant workers' groups add that many helpers shoulder years of caregiving with little rest, training or outside support.

At the heart of the discussion lies a harder question for Hong Kong families: whether society does enough to prevent caregiver burnoutextreme physical and emotional exhaustion caused by prolonged, unsupported caregiving照顧者身心俱疲, whether the carer is a relative or a hired helper, before exhaustion curdles into harm. With Hong Kong's population ageing rapidly, advocates argue that expanding respite careshort-term relief care that gives a regular carer a break from caregiving duties暫託照顧服務 and community support networks is no longer optional, but essential to protecting both elderly residents and the people who care for them.

中文翻譯

上週，啟德一名93歲女子在其同住外傭因涉嫌襲擊而被捕後死亡，事件再次觸發社會對香港如何支援照顧孤身無援長者的照顧者這一沉重議題的討論。死者獨居多年，中風後需要全天候照顧，多年來幾乎完全依賴一名外傭照料。立法會議員及福利團體指出，無論案件最終在法庭上如何判決，事件都暴露出本港在監察那些長者福祉僅繫於單一、往往不勝負荷的照顧者的家庭方面，存在明顯缺口。

目前獲得較多支持的建議之一，是擴大現有的高風險個案資料庫——醫院管理局目前已會在資料庫所列的照顧者入院時，通知社會福利署——將照顧長者的外傭亦納入其中。倡議者亦希望加強定期家訪，並鼓勵照顧者多到社區中心，讓第三方有機會在危機發生前察覺警號。外勞團體則補充，不少外傭在缺乏休息、培訓及外界支援的情況下，長年肩負照顧重擔。

討論的核心，是香港家庭一個更難回答的問題：社會是否做得足夠，去防止照顧者身心俱疲——不論照顧者是家人還是聘請的外傭——在筋疲力竭演變成傷害之前及時介入。隨著香港人口急速老化，倡議者認為，擴大暫託照顧服務及社區支援網絡已不再是可有可無，而是保護長者與照顧者雙方的必要之舉。
