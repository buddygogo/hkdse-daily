English

Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu told the Global University Presidents and Leaders Summit on Tuesday that Hong Kong is building three university townslarge, purpose-built districts centred on higher-education campuses and research facilities大學城 in the Northern Metropolis specifically to draw the world's leading scholars and students. The towns — at San Tin, Hung Shui Kiu and Ta Kwu Ling — will together span 1,000 hectares and each carry a distinct specialism: San Tin will focus on artificial intelligence, robotics and health sciences; Hung Shui Kiu will favour a more vocationalfocused on practical, job-related skills rather than purely academic study職業導向, application-oriented model; and Ta Kwu Ling will concentrate on arts, culture and ecotourism.

Lee framed the project as central to the government's newly launched First Five-Year Plan, inviting overseas institutions to set up research institutesformally established bodies dedicated to carrying out academic or scientific research研究院 and shared platforms such as high-performance computing centres and joint laboratories. To back the push, the government will raise the number of UGC-fundedfinanced by Hong Kong's University Grants Committee, the body that allocates public money to universities教資會資助 research postgraduate places from 7,200 to 9,600 by the 2030/31 academic year, and the first building, Loop Academy One, is due for completion by the end of this year. Nineteen tertiary institutions have already expressed interest, and Hong Kong Baptist University is reportedly weighing whether to relocate its entire campus there.

The ambition has, however, drawn a note of caution from local academics. Gerard Postiglione of the University of Hong Kong warned that the sheer pace of the rollout risks catching universities flat-footedunprepared and unable to react quickly to a sudden change措手不及, with institutions asked to commit to new campuses before funding arrangementsthe agreed ways in which money for a project will be raised and allocated資金安排 and long-term enrolment numbers are fully settled. Supporters counter that Hong Kong cannot afford to wait: regional rivals are racing to build their own talent hubs, and a head startan early advantage gained by starting before competitors先發優勢 on infrastructure, they argue, matters more than having every detail resolved before ground is broken.

中文翻譯

行政長官李家超星期二在「全球大學校長及領導人峰會」上表示，香港正在北部都會區建設三座大學城，目的是吸引全球頂尖學者與學生。三座大學城選址於新田、洪水橋及打鼓嶺，總面積達一千公頃，各具專長：新田聚焦人工智能、機器人及健康科學；洪水橋走較職業導向、應用為本的路線；打鼓嶺則集中發展藝術、文化及生態旅遊。

李家超強調，這項計劃是政府新推出的「首個五年規劃」的核心部分，並邀請海外院校在當地設立研究院及共享平台，例如高效能運算中心及聯合實驗室。為配合這項推動，政府將把教資會資助的研究院研究生學額，由七千二百個增至二零三零至三一學年的九千六百個，首座建築「創新學院一號」預計今年底落成。目前已有十九間專上院校表示有興趣進駐，香港浸會大學更傳出正考慮將整個校園遷往該處。

不過，這項宏願亦令本地學者有所保留。香港大學學者白傑瑞警告，項目推展速度之快，恐令大學措手不及，院校在資金安排及長遠收生人數尚未完全確定之前，已須承諾興建新校園。支持者則反駁，香港等不起：區內其他地方正競相建立人才樞紐，相比把每項細節都敲定後才動工，搶佔基建發展的先發優勢更為重要。
