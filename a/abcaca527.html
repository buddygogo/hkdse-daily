English

On Sunday, Garden, one of Hong Kong’s best-known bakery brands, brought together more than 700 pupils from roughly 100 primary schools at a Wan Chai secondary school hall for an attempt at the world’s largest sandwich-making lesson. What was meant as a feel-good publicity stunt turned chaotic when the session overran by nearly an hourcontinued for much longer than its scheduled finishing time超出原定時間近一小時, leaving hundreds of restless children and anxious parents waiting far longer than planned. Organisers had not arranged a staggered dismissala method of releasing a large crowd in smaller, timed groups rather than all at once分批放行, and the single set of doors used for both entry and exit quickly became a bottlenecka point where the flow of people becomes blocked or dangerously congested人流樽頸.

As the delay dragged on, a livestream of the event kept cutting out, fuelling frustration among parents already standing for close to two hours. When staff finally opened the hall at the end, a crush of parents pushing toward their children caused a door handle to snap off entirely. Garden said the incident reflected a logistical lapsea failure in the practical planning or coordination needed to run an event smoothly後勤疏漏 rather than any deliberate disregard for safety, and it stressed that no one was injured despite what it called overcrowdinga situation in which too many people are gathered in a space built for far fewer人滿為患 near the exit. Some parents online described the scene as dangerously close to a crush, though the company’s own account insists staff brought the situation under control within minutes.

The bakery has since issued a public apologya formal statement of regret made openly to those affected公開道歉 and pledged a risk assessmenta systematic review of what could go wrong and how likely and serious each danger is風險評估 of its venue capacitythe maximum number of people a space can safely hold場地容納量 and crowd-flow planning before it runs another large-scale promotional event. Education officials and parent groups have welcomed the review but questioned why a brand with decades of experience running public promotions failed to anticipate a surge in demandto foresee and prepare for a sharp, sudden increase in something, here the number of attendees預料需求激增 at an event explicitly designed to draw a large crowd of children and their families.

中文翻譯

上周日,香港著名麵包品牌嘉頓在灣仔一所中學禮堂,召集逾700名來自約100間小學的學童,嘗試打破「最大型三文治製作課堂」的世界紀錄。這場原本旨在打造親善形象的宣傳活動,卻演變成一片混亂——活動超出原定時間近一小時,令數百名已顯得不耐煩的學童及心急如焚的家長久候未散。主辦方未有安排分批放行,而單一出入口亦迅即形成人流樽頸。

隨著延誤持續,活動的網上直播亦屢次中斷,令本已站立近兩小時的家長更添怒氣。當職員最終開啟禮堂讓人群離開時,家長蜂擁上前接回子女,竟將大門門柄硬生生扯斷。嘉頓表示,事件反映的是後勤疏漏,而非蓄意忽視安全,並強調儘管出口附近出現所謂人滿為患的情況,但無人受傷。部分家長在網上形容現場險象環生,幾乎釀成人踩人,但該公司則堅稱職員在數分鐘內已控制局面。

該麵包店事後發出公開道歉,並承諾在下次舉辦大型宣傳活動前,會就其場地容納量及人流規劃進行風險評估。教育界人士及家長組織歡迎有關檢討,但質疑一個擁有數十年舉辦公眾宣傳活動經驗的品牌,為何未能在一場明顯旨在吸引大量學童及其家人參與的活動前,預料需求激增。
