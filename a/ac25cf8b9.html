English

The government has scrappedcancelled or abandoned a plan entirely撤銷 a controversial plan to install coloured lighting along the popular Lugard Road and Harlech Road walking trail at The Peak, following weeks of public backlash from hikers and nearby residents. Culture, Sports and Tourism Secretary Rosanna Law confirmed the reversal on Thursday, telling the Central and Western District Council that officials had concluded there was no pressing needan urgent necessity requiring immediate action迫切需要 to proceed with the attraction, which had been intended as part of this year’s winter tourism display. The Hong Kong Tourism Board, which oversaw the project, pledged to remove all related installations and restorereturn something to its earlier condition恢復原貌 the trail to its original state as soon as possible.

The U-turna complete reversal of an earlier decision政策逆轉 comes after residents accused the board of clearing trees and vegetation to make way for projector stands and light fixtures, and of adding unwanted light pollution to one of Hong Kong’s most cherished natural viewpoints. The board has firmly denied felling any trees, insisting that neither it nor its contractor needed to remove vegetation for the project, though it had already taken down parts of two installations after an earlier round of complaints. One district councillor further lamentedexpressed regret or disappointment about something慨嘆 that residents had not been properly consulted before construction began, a claim that added to mounting criticismdisapproval that keeps increasing日增的批評 of how the project was rolled out.

The scheme had originally envisioned a year-long nightly display across ten locations, illuminating as many as 52 trees between 7pm and 10pm, though officials had already discussed scaling this back to just seven locations and 24 trees before scrapping it entirely. The episode highlights the tension between Hong Kong’s efforts to revitalisegive new energy or life to something振興 its tourism offerings and the need to safeguard fragile natural heritage, offering HKDSE students a timely example of how top-down urban planningthe organised design and development of land and public spaces in a city城市規劃 decisions can backfireproduce an unexpected and unwanted result, opposite to what was intended適得其反 when public consultation is seen as inadequate.

中文翻譯

政府已撤銷一項具爭議性的計劃，該計劃原打算在山頂熱門的盧吉道及夏力道遠足徑加設彩色燈飾，計劃在遭到行山人士及附近居民連日強烈反對後告終。文化體育及旅遊局局長羅淑佩星期四向中西區區議會確認撤回決定，並表示官員認為並無迫切需要推行這個原定作為今年冬日旅遊展示一部分的項目。負責該項目的香港旅遊發展局承諾，會盡快移除所有相關設施，並將山徑恢復原貌。

這次政策逆轉，發生在居民指控旅發局為安裝投影支架及燈光裝置而清除樹木及植被，並為香港其中一個最受珍視的天然觀景點帶來不必要的光污染之後。旅發局堅決否認砍伐任何樹木，強調該局及其承建商均無需為此項目移除植被，不過在早前一輪投訴後，已拆除部分裝置。一名區議員進一步慨嘆，指居民在動工前未獲充分諮詢，此說法令對項目推行方式的日增的批評更甚。

該計劃原本構思為一項長達一年的夜間展示，橫跨十個地點，點亮多達52棵樹木，展示時間為晚上七時至十時，不過官員在全面撤銷計劃前，已曾討論將範圍縮減至僅七個地點及24棵樹木。此事凸顯香港在致力振興旅遊業與保護脆弱自然遺產之間的張力，為香港中學文憑考試學生提供一個切合時宜的例子，說明當公眾諮詢被視為不足時，自上而下的城市規劃決策如何可能適得其反。
