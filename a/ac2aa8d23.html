English

Hundreds of visitors queued outside Sotheby's Maison in Central this week for a rare glimpse of a Vincent van Gogh landscape that had not been put on public displaynot shown to ordinary visitors, kept instead in storage or in a private place未曾公開展示 for more than sixty years. "Chestnut Trees in Blossom," painted in 1890 during the final months of the artist's life, arrived in the city as part of a travelling previewa short public viewing of items ahead of a sale, held in several cities before the actual auction巡迴預展 ahead of a November sale in New York, where auctioneers expect it to fetch a record priceto sell for an amount of money higher than any previous sale of its kind創下破紀錄高價 of around US$180 million.

The painting formed part of the estatethe total property and possessions left behind by someone after their death遺產 of the late Argentine collectors Nelly Arrieta de Blaquier and Carlos Pedro Blaquier, who had quietly built a collection that also included works by Monet, Pissarro and Cézanne. Twelve pieces from the collection have now been consigned to auctionformally handed over to an auction house to be sold on the owner's behalf委託拍賣, and houses such as Sotheby's increasingly treat the works' provenancethe documented history of who has owned an artwork over time來源紀錄 as part of the sales pitch itself, since a respected ownership history can add considerably to a painting's value.

For many in Tuesday's queue, though, the appeal lay less in the saleroomthe room or hall in which items are publicly sold at auction拍賣廳 arithmetic than in the rare chance to stand before a canvas that usually sits in a private collectiona group of artworks owned by an individual rather than displayed in a public museum私人收藏, unseen by the public for an entire generation. The stop in Hong Kong also underlines the city's growing role in the blue-chip art marketthe trade in works by famous, highly valued artists that are seen as a safe, prestigious investment頂級藝術市場, as international auction houses increasingly use the city to court Asian collectors before a work ever reaches the saleroom floor.

中文翻譯

本週,數百名參觀者在中環蘇富比藝術空間外排隊等候,只為一睹梵高一幅風景畫的真跡,該畫作逾六十年來從未公開展示。《盛放的栗樹》繪於1890年,即畫家生命最後數月的作品,今次作為紐約拍賣前的巡迴預展的一站來到本港,拍賣行預期該畫將於十一月拍賣中創下破紀錄高價,成交價料達約1.8億美元。

這幅畫作屬於已故阿根廷收藏家內莉·阿列塔·德·布拉基耶與卡洛斯·佩德羅·布拉基耶夫婦遺產的一部分,二人生前默默建立的收藏亦包括莫內、畢沙羅及塞尚的作品。該批收藏中有十二件作品現已委託拍賣,而蘇富比等拍賣行亦日益將作品的來源紀錄本身視為銷售賣點之一,因為一段備受肯定的收藏歷史,往往能大幅提升畫作的價值。

然而,對於週二排隊的許多人而言,吸引力與其說是拍賣廳裡的數字,不如說是難得一見的機會,能親身站在一幅平日藏於私人收藏、整整一代人未曾得見的畫作面前。是次在香港停留,亦印證了本港在頂級藝術市場中日益吃重的角色,國際拍賣行正愈趨利用本港,在作品踏上拍賣廳地板之前,搶先吸引亞洲收藏家垂青。
