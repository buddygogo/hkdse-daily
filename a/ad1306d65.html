English

Hongkongers in Wong Tai Sin will soon be able to check their risk of dementia with nothing more invasive than a photograph of the back of their eye, after the Chinese University of Hong Kong announced a free, district-based screening programmea health check-up scheme targeted at residents of one particular area地區性篩查計劃 set to begin in January 2027. The two-year initiative will use AI-powered retinal imagingusing artificial intelligence to analyse photographs of the back of the eye for signs of disease人工智能視網膜影像分析 to flag early signs of cognitive decline in up to 2,700 residents aged 60 to 79.

Unlike traditional memory clinics, the screening involves no blood draws and no brain scans. Participants instead complete a short questionnaire, have a single photograph taken of their retina, and sit a brief cognitive assessmenta short test used to measure a person’s thinking, memory and reasoning abilities認知能力評估, with an algorithm trained on nearly 13,000 earlier fundus images flagging those who may need closer attention. Professor Vincent Mok, who co-leads the project, said the model's accuracy is now comparable to costlier imaging tests, making it a practical tool for mass community screeningchecking a very large number of ordinary people for a condition, rather than only those already showing symptoms大規模社區篩查 rather than hospital-based diagnosis alone.

Wong Tai Sin was chosen because it has one of the highest proportions of elderly residents among Hong Kong's 18 districts, making early detection especially urgent there. Only residents flagged as higher risk will be referred onward, easing pressure on overstretched memory clinicsspecialist medical clinics for diagnosing dementia that already have more patients than they can easily handle人手緊絀的記憶診所 while still catching cases early enough for lifestyle changes or medication to help. Researchers hope the approach, if proven effective, could eventually be rolled out to older residents citywide, addressing what health officials have long called a looming demographic challengea difficult, large-scale problem approaching as a society’s population grows steadily older迫在眉睫的人口挑戰 as Hong Kong's population continues to age.

中文翻譯

黃大仙居民日後只需拍一張眼底照片，便可檢測自己患上認知障礙症的風險，香港中文大學宣布將推出免費的地區性篩查計劃，計劃於2027年1月展開。這項為期兩年的計劃將利用人工智能視網膜影像分析技術，為最多2700名年齡介乎60至79歲的居民，及早識別認知能力衰退的徵狀。

與傳統的記憶診所不同，這項篩查無需抽血，亦不涉及腦部掃描。參加者只需填寫簡短問卷、拍攝一張視網膜照片，並接受簡短的認知能力評估，再由一套根據近13000張早期視網膜影像訓練而成的演算法，篩選出需要進一步關注的人士。項目聯合主持莫仲棠教授表示，該模型的準繩度現時已可媲美造價更高昂的影像檢查，令其成為大規模社區篩查的實用工具，而非只局限於醫院內的診斷。

黃大仙獲選作試點，是因為該區在本港十八個行政區中，長者比例屬最高之一，令及早識別病情顯得尤其迫切。只有被評為高風險的居民會獲轉介跟進，藉此減輕人手緊絀的記憶診所的壓力，同時仍能及早發現個案，讓患者有機會透過調整生活方式或服藥受惠。研究人員希望，若此方法證實有效，日後或可擴展至全港長者，應對香港人口持續老化下，衛生官員一直形容為迫在眉睫的人口挑戰。
