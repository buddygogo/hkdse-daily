English

Hong Kong's first International Lantern Spectacular has opened its doorsto become available for public visits正式開放 at Victoria Park ahead of this year's Mid-Autumn Festival, running from 17 to 27 September under the theme “Reunion”. Organised by the Hong Kong Tourism Board alongside the Leisure and Cultural Services Department's traditional Lantern Carnival, the event brings together more than 3,000 lanterns from twenty countries and regions, including Nanjing Qinhuai masterpieces recognised as intangible cultural heritagetraditions, skills and practices passed down and officially recognised as part of a community's heritage非物質文化遺產 and locally handcrafted pieces at the Hong Kong Lantern Galleria.

It is widely argued that the event reflects a deliberate effort to reinvigorateto give new energy or strength to something為……注入新活力 a centuries-old festival for younger audiences, many of whom have grown up more familiar with imported holidays than with traditional Chinese customs. Alongside the international display, more than 800 lanterns have also been put up along Lee Tung Avenue in Wan Chai, while the Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance — a tradition dating back to the 1880s and now honoured as National Intangible Cultural Heritage — will once again weave through the neighbourhood's narrow lanes over three nights.

Nevertheless, some cultural commentators contend that large-scale commercial spectaclesbig, heavily promoted public events organised chiefly for economic or tourism benefit大型商業盛事 risk overshadowing the quieter, more intimate customs traditionally associated with Mid-Autumn Festival, such as family reunion dinners and mooncake-sharing. By the same token, tourism officials maintain that events of this scale are essential if Hong Kong is to showcase its cultural identityto display and promote the distinctive traditions and values of a place展示其文化身份 on the international stage while also drawing much-needed visitor numbers back to the city.

中文翻譯

香港首屆「國際花燈薈萃」已於今年中秋節前在維多利亞公園正式開幕,展期為9月17日至27日,主題為「團圓」。活動由香港旅遊發展局聯同康樂及文化事務署一直舉辦的傳統綵燈會共同籌辦,匯聚超過三千個來自二十個國家及地區的花燈,包括被列為非物質文化遺產的南京秦淮花燈傑作,以及在香港花燈廊內展出的本地手工花燈作品。

有人認為,這項活動反映當局有意為這個流傳數百年的節日注入新活力,以吸引年輕一代觀眾,畢竟他們當中不少人,成長過程中對外來節日反而更為熟悉,多於傳統中國習俗。除了這項國際花燈展覽,灣仔利東街亦同時設置逾八百個花燈,而源自1880年代、現已被列為國家級非物質文化遺產的大坑舞火龍,亦將一連三晚再度穿梭社區的狹窄街巷。

然而,部分文化評論員認為,大型商業盛事可能令與中秋節傳統上關聯的較寧靜、親密的習俗——例如家庭團圓飯及分享月餅——相形見絀。與此同時,旅遊官員則堅持,這類大型活動對於香港在國際舞台上展現其文化身份,同時吸引急需的遊客數字回流本港,實屬必要。
