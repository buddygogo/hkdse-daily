Workers in Causeway Bay have begun installingputting equipment or structures into position for use安裝 a row of fixed steel bollards along the pavement outside the landmark Sogo department store, the first visible stage of a HK$5 million government plan to guard against vehicle-ramming attacksdeliberate attacks in which a vehicle is driven into crowds車輛衝撞襲擊 in one of the city's busiest shopping districts. The Police Force, the Highways Department and the Transport Department jointly describe the risk of such an attack along the pedestrian crossing and the Paterson Street precinct as long-standingexisting for a long time without being resolved長期存在, given the sheer density of shoppers who gather there at weekends and during festive seasons.

According to the latest on-site reports, four permanent bollards have already been bolted intofixed firmly in place using metal fasteners以螺栓固定於 the pavement outside Sogo, while workers continue construction behind temporary barriers further along the precinct. Once finished, the barriers are meant to physically block any vehicle from mounting the kerb and driving into the crowded footpath, a measure officials frame as an urgently needed piece of public-safety infrastructure rather than a cosmetic gesture.

Not everyone welcomes the rollout, however. A delivery worker interviewed on site complained that the bollards will impedeget in the way of or slow down movement阻礙 his handcart deliveries, while a nearby resident questioned whether the metal posts come at the expense of the district's distinctive streetscapethe particular visual character of a street or neighbourhood獨特街景. A Wan Chai district councillor went further, warning that the bollards could bottleneckseverely restrict the flow of something, causing a build-up造成人流樽頸 pedestrian flow during peak hours and formally requesting detailed crowd-flow data before the project is finalised, underscoring the tension between hardening public spaces against rare but catastrophic threats and preserving the everyday rhythm of one of Hong Kong's most crowded corners.

中文翻譯

銅鑼灣工人已開始在崇光百貨外的行人路上安裝一排固定鋼製欄柱，是政府耗資500萬港元計劃的首個可見階段，旨在防範這個全港最繁忙購物區之一發生車輛衝撞襲擊。警務處、路政署及運輸署聯合形容，沿該行人過路處及百德新街專區發生此類襲擊的風險屬長期存在，因為每逢週末及節慶旺季，該處聚集的購物人流極為密集。

根據現場最新消息，四個永久欄柱已以螺栓固定於崇光百貨外的行人路，工人則在專區較遠處的臨時圍板後繼續施工。工程完成後，這些路障旨在實際阻止任何車輛駛上路緣並衝入擁擠的行人路，當局將此形容為刻不容緩的公共安全設施，而非純粹的裝飾性姿態。

然而，並非所有人都歡迎這項工程。一名接受訪問的速遞員投訴，欄柱會阻礙他使用手推車送貨，附近一名居民則質疑，這些金屬柱子是否要以犧牲區內獨特街景為代價。一名灣仔區議員更進一步警告，欄柱可能在繁忙時段造成人流樽頸，並正式要求當局在落實工程前提供詳細人流數據，突顯了在為應對罕見卻可能致命的威脅而強化公共空間，與維持香港其中一個最擁擠角落日常運作節奏之間存在的張力。
