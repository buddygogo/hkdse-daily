An investigation is under way after 39 pupils and staff at a Sha Tin primary school fell ill following a school lunch, in what health officials describe as a suspected food poisoning clustera group of illness cases traced to a common contaminated source食物中毒羣組. The affected group, comprising 37 pupils aged five to eleven and two staff members, had eaten a catered midday meal of soy-braised chicken drumsticks and long pasta at Baptist (Sha Tin Wai) Lui Ming Choi Primary School on 9 September before developing abdominal painpain or discomfort in the stomach area腹痛 and diarrhoea between eight and twenty-two hours later.

According to the Centre for Health Protection, two of the affected pupils sought medical attention but did not require hospital admissionbeing formally admitted to hospital for treatment住院 and have since remained in stable condition, while the remaining cases presented only mild symptoms. It is widely argued that classroom-based lunch portioningthe practice of dividing and serving catered meals inside individual classrooms rather than a central kitchen課室分餐模式, in which catered food is divided among pupils inside the classroom rather than under closer kitchen supervision, may heighten the risk of cross-contaminationthe transfer of harmful bacteria from one food item or surface to another交叉污染 once meals leave the supplier's premises.

Danny Catering Service, the school's lunch supplier, described the outbreak as an isolated incidentan event that occurred on its own, without being part of a wider or recurring pattern單一事件 and has since announced it will discontinue the classroom portioning arrangement at the affected school. Nevertheless, the episode has reignited debate over whether Hong Kong's school lunch suppliers are subject to sufficiently rigorous food safety standardsstrict rules and procedures designed to prevent contamination and illness嚴格的食物安全標準. Parent groups have called on the Education Bureau to conduct unannounced inspections of catering contractors, arguing that public confidence in school meal safety cannot rest on suppliers' assurances alone.
