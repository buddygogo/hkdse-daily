Secretary for Education Christine Choi Yuk-lin has clarified that the newly announced school merger scheme should not be viewed merely as a lifeline for shrinking institutions, but rather as a deliberate push towards better-quality education overall. Under the proposal, known as the “Confluence and Elevation – School Excellence Project,” the Education Bureau will support and incentivise suitable publicly-funded primary and secondary schools to pool their resourcesto combine resources for shared benefit集中資源 and merge, with the scheme set to launch as early as the 2027/28 academic year.

It is widely acknowledged that Hong Kong's shrinking birth ratea declining number of births in a population出生率下降 has placed considerable strain on smaller schools, many of which now struggle to fill classrooms. Nevertheless, Ms Choi was at pains to emphasise that the scheme carries no fixed quotaa set maximum limit固定配額 and is not intended solely as a rescue measure for institutions on the brink of closurevery close to shutting down瀕臨結業. Rather, she argued, sponsoring bodies should regard mergers as a proactive strategy, enabling schools to consolidate teaching expertiseto strengthen and combine teaching skills and knowledge整合教學專業知識, share facilities and offer a broader curriculum than either institution could sustain alone.

By the same token, officials have promised attractive financial and administrative incentivesmonetary and procedural benefits offered to encourage action財政及行政誘因 to encourage early participation, including a reduction in bureaucratic red tapeexcessive rules and procedures that cause delay官僚繁文縟節. Critics contend, however, that mergers can be disruptive for students and teachers alike, particularly where two distinct school cultures must be reconciled. For families weighing up school choices in the years ahead, the debate over consolidation versus continuity is likely to remain a pressing concern, with far-reaching implicationsconsequences that extend widely深遠影響 for the shape of Hong Kong's education landscape.
