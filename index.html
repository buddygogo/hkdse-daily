📱 Hong Kong's Gen Z underestimate how much social media fuels their loneliness

Social Trend

A survey of 572 young people aged 11 to 24 by the Hong Kong Christian Service has found that Gen Z underestimatesto judge something to be smaller or less significant than it really is低估 how much social media affects their sense of loneliness. While 93.4 per cent of respondents were active social media users – starting at an average age of just 10.3 – those who used the platforms most heavily scored higher on the UCLA Loneliness Scale than non-users, averaging 20.5 points out of 40 compared with 17.7.

Researchers found that although objective scores showed heavier users were lonelier, these users often subjectivelybased on personal feelings or opinions rather than facts主觀地 felt they were not particularly isolated, with 5.8 per cent of active users found to be experiencing severe social isolation without fully recognising it. The findings suggest that it is not social media itself but a combination of negative online experiencesharmful or upsetting interactions that occur on the internet負面網絡經歷 and reduced real-world social contact that drives the trend.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Comparison and validation-seeking: Constant exposure to curated, idealised posts encourages young users to compare their own lives unfavourably, deepening feelings of isolation even while appearing connected.
  • Erosion of face-to-face bonds: Time spent scrolling often substitutes for in-person interaction, weakening the real-world friendships that most effectively protect against loneliness.
  • Fear of missing out (FOMO): Anxiety about being left out of online conversations and events keeps young people tethered to their screens, reinforcing the very cycle that fuels their isolation.
