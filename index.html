HKU Raises Alarm Over Suicide Rate Among Under-15s, Warns of Social Media Impact

SCMP ·12 September 2026

It is widely reported that Hong Kong is facing a deeply troubling paradoxa situation that seems contradictory but may be true看似矛盾卻可能屬實的情況 in youth mental health. According to new research from the University of Hong Kong's Centre for Suicide Research and Prevention, the city's overall suicide rate declined across nearly all age groups last year. Nevertheless, the rate among children under the age of fifteen climbed to a decade-highthe highest level in ten years十年來最高水平 of 2.9 per 100,000, the worst figure recorded since 2016. This finding runs directly counter to the broader downward trend, and it has prompted researchers to sound the alarm over what appears to be an emerging crisisa serious problem that is only just becoming apparent剛開始浮現的嚴重問題 among the city's youngest residents.

Critics contend that heavy social media use is a significant contributing factorsomething that helps cause a result促成某結果的因素 behind this troubling shift. The centre's researchers found that social media activity was strongly predictive of suicide cases among young people, suggesting that online exposure to harmful content, cyberbullying and unrealistic comparisons may exacerbateto make a problem or situation worse使問題或情況惡化 existing vulnerabilities. By the same token, the timing of a recent spate of suicide attempts, clustered around the start of the new academic year, points to the added weight of examination pressure and the notoriously competitive nature of Hong Kong's schooling system, which is often blamed for placing undue strainan unreasonable or excessive amount of pressure不合理或過度的壓力 on students.

In light of these findings, the centre's founding director has urged schools to give pupils more time and space to build rapport with teachers and classmates, arguing that strong relationships can act as a protective factorsomething that reduces risk or vulnerability能減低風險的因素 against psychological distress. It is widely argued that a purely academic response is insufficient; instead, a holistic approachone that considers the whole person, not just one aspect顧及整體而非單一方面的方法 involving families, educators and platform regulators will be necessary if Hong Kong is to reverse this alarming trajectory and safeguard the wellbeing of its next generation.

China Teen's Hong Kong Concert Trip Costs Family Government Subsidies, Sparking Mixed Reaction

SCMP ·12 September 2026

A seemingly minor decision by a teenager in mainland China has sparked a nationwide debate over welfare policy, consumer culture and family responsibility, after it emerged that her trip to Hong Kong for a concert cost her family their government living allowance. The girl, a technical secondary school student from Ganzhou in Jiangxi province, reportedly told her family that she could financeto provide money for something為某事提供資金 the trip independently. However, local authorities subsequently classified the outing as a form of high-end consumptionspending on non-essential or luxury items非必要或奢侈品的消費, deeming it incompatible with the household's continued eligibility for a subsidy scheme intended for low-income families.

The case has since gone viral, dividing public opinion online. On one hand, some commentators argue that the policy is a reasonable safeguard, contending that public funds should be reserved strictly for essential needs rather than discretionary entertainment. On the other hand, many netizens have voiced sympathy for the family, arguing that penalisingpunishing someone for an action因某行為而懲罰某人 them over a single concert ticket is disproportionate, particularly given that the girl's parents depend on the monthly allowance of 2,000 yuan due to ongoing health issues. Nevertheless, officials maintain that the rules must be applied consistentlyin the same way every time, without exception始終如一,不作例外 to prevent abuse of the welfare system.

This incident, though seemingly small in scale, raises broader questions that resonate well beyond Ganzhou. It is widely argued that the episode illustrates the tension between rigid bureaucratic criteria and the nuanced realitiescomplex and detailed aspects of a situation that are easy to overlook容易被忽略的複雜細節 of family life, especially as fan culture and cross-border travel become increasingly accessible to young people. By the same token, the controversy invites reflection on how welfare systems everywhere, including Hong Kong, might strike a fairer balance between accountabilitybeing responsible for your own actions or decisions對自己的行為或決定負責 and compassion when administering support to vulnerable households.

Man Arrested After Allegedly Refusing to Share HK$30m Mark Six Jackpot With Group

SCMP ·12 September 2026

A dispute over a multimillion-dollar lottery prize has landed a Hong Kong man in police custody, after he allegedly refused to share a Mark Six jackpot worth approximately HK$30 million with fourteen of his colleagues. According to police, the group, made up of staff members from the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department, had reportedly pooled their moneycombined funds from several people for a shared purpose集合各人的金錢作共同用途 to purchase the winning ticket together, with the 36-year-old man responsible for buying the ticket and holding the bank account into which the prize was ultimately paid.

Nevertheless, once the ticket proved to be a winner, the man allegedly refused to distribute the winnings evenly among the group, despite a meeting having been convened in an attempt to resolve the disputea disagreement or conflict between parties爭議或衝突 amicably. A 53-year-old colleague subsequently reported the matter to police, prompting an investigation on suspicion of theft. Officers have since frozento officially prevent money in an account from being used or withdrawn正式凍結戶口內的資金,禁止動用 the HK$30 million held in the man's bank account while the case is investigated further.

It is widely argued that this case serves as a cautionary tale about the importance of establishing clear, written agreements before entering into any form of shared financial venture, however informal it may seem. By the same token, the incident highlights a recurring legal grey area in Hong Kong: verbal arrangements between friends or colleagues, though seemingly built on mutual trust, often carry little weight without documented proof, leaving aggrieved parties with limited recourse besides pursuing costly and time-consuming legal action. Critics contend that greater public awareness of such risks could help prevent similar disputes from arising in the future.

Quality Concerns Prompt Further Tests on Concrete Used in 3 Public Housing Blocks

SCMP ·12 September 2026

Concerns over building safety have once again surfaced in Hong Kong's public housing sector, after the Housing Authority disclosed that the structural integritythe ability of a building or structure to withstand its intended load without failing結構承受預期負荷而不損壞的能力 of concrete used in three blocks at the Tung Chung Area 42 estate requires further investigation. The issue came to light during routine concrete cube strength tests conducted between late August and early September, when engineers found that certain structural components in Blocks 3, 4 and 5 did not meet the expected quality benchmarks, prompting authorities to order additional, more rigorous testing before construction can proceed.

The main contractor overseeing the project, Able Engineering, is no stranger to scrutinyclose and careful examination仔細和認真的審查; the firm is separately embroiled in a scandal involving defective lift installation work at an expansion project at Queen Mary Hospital. This is by no means the first time that quality control issues have affected Hong Kong's public housing pipeline, and it is widely argued that such recurring lapses raise serious questions about oversightthe act of supervising or monitoring a process監督或監察過程 within the construction industry, particularly given the government's ambitious targets for accelerating the supply of subsidised flats to address the city's chronic housing shortage.

Nevertheless, officials have sought to reassure the public that safety will not be compromised for the sake of speed, stressing that no units will be handed over until the concrete's quality is fully verified. By the same token, critics contend that greater transparency and stricter accountability measuressystems that ensure people or companies answer for their actions確保人或公司對其行為負責的制度 for contractors are urgently needed, arguing that public trust in the housing supply chain cannot be rebuilt through reassurances alone, but only through consistently rigorous enforcement of building standards.

🎡 "Feel Like Dance" Challenge Takes Over Hong Kong Disneyland Fans

Social Trend

A new dance challenge tied to Hong Kong Disneyland's anniversary celebrations, dubbed the "Feel Like Dance" Challengea short, imitable dance routine shared widely online一段簡短、易於模仿並在網上廣傳的舞蹈, has taken over TikTok and Instagram feeds across the city, inviting fans to relive 90s dance vibes with a nostalgic, upbeat routine. Thousands of local teenagers and young adults have posted their own versions, often filmed at the park itself or recreated at home with friends.

Part of the appeal lies in its low barrier to entrysomething that is easy for most people to try or join容易讓大部分人參與或嘗試的門檻: the choreography is short, repetitive and easy to imitate, making it accessible even to casual users who rarely post content. Brands and influencers have quickly capitalised on the momentum, tagging the challenge to boost visibility, while Hong Kong Disneyland itself has amplified user-generated clips through its official accounts, turning ordinary visitors into unofficial brand ambassadors.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: Nostalgic music and simple, repetitive choreography trigger positive emotional associations, making the trend easy and rewarding to join.
  • Social media influence: Amplification by official park accounts and influencers rapidly extends the challenge's reach among Hong Kong youth.
  • Low barrier to entry: The short, imitable format allows even infrequent content creators to participate without specialised skill or equipment.
  • Consumer culture: Association with a well-known entertainment brand encourages participants to visit the park in person, blending online trends with real-world spending.
  • Sense of community: Shared participation in a recognisable challenge fosters a feeling of belonging among peers, both online and offline.
