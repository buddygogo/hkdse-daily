Over half of HK entrepreneurs see AI as catalyst for workforce growth: HSBC

The Standard ·29 September 2026

A recent survey commissioned by HSBC has found that more than half of Hong Kong's entrepreneurs regard artificial intelligence as a catalystsomething that causes rapid change or action促成因素；催化劑 for workforce growth rather than a threat to employment. According to the findings, 58 percent of local business owners expect the adoptionthe act of starting to use something new採用；採納 of AI tools to increase headcount over the next two years, a proportion markedly higher than the global average.

It is widely argued that this optimism reflects a shift in how small and medium-sized enterprises in the city perceiveto become aware of or understand something察覺；理解 automation. Rather than viewing AI as a means of supplantingto take the place of, especially through force or scheming取代；排擠 human staff, many firms now see it as a way to augmentto make something greater by adding to it增加；提升 productivity, freeing employees to focus on higher-value tasks such as client relations and strategic planning. Sectors including retail, logistics and financial services are reportedly leading this trend, deploying AI-powered chatbots, data analytics platforms and inventory systems.

Nevertheless, critics contend that the benefits of AI adoption are not evenly distributed. Entry-level and routine clerical positions remain particularly vulnerablecapable of being easily harmed or affected易受影響的；脆弱的 to displacementthe forced removal of someone or something from their usual place取代；裁減, raising concerns for young jobseekers, including secondary school leavers, who may find it harder to secure a foothold in the labour market. By the same token, education authorities have been urged to prioritiseto treat something as more important than other things優先處理 digital literacy and critical thinking in the curriculum, equipping students with the adaptabilitythe quality of being able to adjust to new conditions適應能力 needed to thrive alongside, rather than in competition with, intelligent machines. As Hong Kong positions itself as an innovation and technology hub, the ability of its workforce to upskill continuously will likely prove decisive.

HK to pulp all recovered waste paper locally from October 1 in recycling overhaul

The Standard ·30 September 2026

Hong Kong has begun pulping all of its recovered waste paper locally at a new facility in EcoPark, Tuen Mun, a move officials say will end the city's long-standing reliancethe state of depending on something for support依賴 on export markets for recycling. The plant, which started operations on 27 July this year and has been gradually ramping upincreasing the level or amount of something逐步提升 its capacity, formally took over full pulping duties from 1 October.

It is widely argued that the overhaul marks a significant milestonean important point in the development of something里程碑 in the city's recycling chain. Previously, collectors were restricted to the so-called "three types of paper" — cardboard, newspapers and office paper — but the scheme has now been broadenedmade wider in range or scope擴大；拓寬 to accept mixed paper, including paper bags, packaging boxes and wrapping paper. Annual recovery volumes are projected to rise from roughly 500,000 tonnes to 720,000 tonnes, and the facility recently set a single-day intake record of 1,608 tonnes, four percent higher than last year's daily average.

By the same token, the scheme has been designed to safeguard the livelihoods of frontline collectors. The operator is obligedrequired to do something because of a law, rule or promise有義務的 to purchase waste paper at no less than a "Designated Purchasing Price," which has been raised from HK$1,200 to HK$1,295.51 per tonne, alongside a minimum rate of HK$0.65 per kilogram to ensure collectors are paid fairly amid fluctuatingchanging irregularly in level or amount波動的 market conditions. Nevertheless, environmentalists caution that sustained public cooperation in sorting waste correctly remains essential if the ambitious recovery targets are to be met. As Hong Kong strives to curb its reliance on landfills and strengthen its circular economyan economic system aimed at eliminating waste through continual reuse of resources循環經濟, this locally rooted recycling model is widely seen as a test case for other sectors seeking to reduce dependence on international waste markets.

Gang conflict over 'space oil' market triggers massive street brawl in Tin Shui Wai

The Standard ·30 September 2026

A mass street brawl in Tin Shui Wai, reportedly linked to a dispute over the illicit trade in "space oil" — a vape-based drug containing the anaesthetic etomidate — has renewed public debate over Hong Kong's ongoing battle against youth drug abuse. Footage of the clash, which showed a large group of men fighting in a public area, quickly circulatedspread widely among people, especially online流傳；傳播 on social media, prompting a police investigation into suspected organised crime involvement.

It is widely argued that incidents of this kind are symptomatic of a deeper, more troubling trend. Since the government outlawedmade something illegal將……列為非法 etomidate and several related substances, authorities have uncovered illicit laboratories manufacturing homemade versions of the drug, suggesting that demandthe desire of buyers for a particular product需求 among young users has not abatedbecome less intense or widespread減弱；減退. Health researchers note that a detoxification centre recently found that around ten percent of its caseload involved space oil abuse, with half of those affected being minors — the youngest only twelve years old.

Critics contend that public understanding of the drug's dangers remains alarmingly limited. A survey of over 1,600 residents found that nearly a third of respondents were unaware of its harmful effects, while fewer than three in ten could correctly identify its main ingredient. By the same token, medical professionals have warned that etomidate misuse has been linked to adrenal insufficiency, a potentially serious hormonal condition, among teenage users. Nevertheless, the recurrence of gang-related violence connected to the drug trade suggests that enforcementthe act of making sure laws are obeyed執法 measures alone may be insufficient. Educators and parents alike are being urged to engage young people in frank conversations about substance abuse before curiosity turns into dependencythe state of relying on something, especially a harmful substance依賴；上癮.

Hong Kong cuts the sky with a 23-minute National Day fireworks show

Dim Sum Daily ·1 October 2026

Hong Kong marked the 77th anniversary of the founding of the People's Republic of China with a 23-minute fireworks display over Victoria Harbour on the evening of 1 October, illuminatinglighting up or making something bright照亮 the skyline with more than 31,000 pyrotechnicrelating to fireworks or explosive devices used for display煙火的 shells. Organised by the Culture, Sports and Tourism Bureau and exclusively sponsored by Sun Life Hong Kong, the show was staged under the theme "Eternal Radiance, Fireworks in Bloom," with an estimated budget of around HK$18 million.

It is widely argued that such large-scale celebrations serve a purpose beyond mere spectacle. The display was divided into eight segments, each choreographedcarefully planned and arranged in sequence精心編排的 to music and featuring intricatevery detailed or complicated in design複雜精細的 patterns including red hearts, golden five-pointed stars, and a newly designed sun-and-pendulum motif that had never previously been used in a Hong Kong fireworks show. Crowds gathered at popular vantage pointspositions that give a good view of something有利位置 including Tsim Sha Tsui, Central, Wan Chai and Causeway Bay, while others watched from the Hung Hom Bypass and nearby rooftops.

By the same token, the event is widely regarded as an opportunity to strengthen a sense of civic identity and shared heritagevalued traditions, qualities and culture passed down through generations傳統；文化遺產 among residents. Tourism officials noted that the National Day period, often called "Golden Week," typically brings a significant influx of mainland visitors, giving such spectacles added economic as well as symbolic significance. Nevertheless, some commentators have questioned whether the considerable cost of these displays might be better allocateddistributed or assigned for a particular purpose分配 to other community needs. Proponents counter that the fireworks generate immense goodwill and tourism revenue, and that such moments of collective celebration help to fosterto encourage the development of something培養；促進 unity in an increasingly fragmented society. As the final shells faded over the harbour, the event once again underscored Hong Kong's enduring role as a stage for grand civic spectacle.

🧸 Labubu blind-box craze keeps collectors hooked

Social Trend

The "Labubu" craze, centred on Pop Mart's mischievous, pointy-toothed collectible figures, continues to dominate feeds on Instagram and TikTok among Hong Kong youth. Designed by Hong Kong-raised artist Kasing Lung, the "ugly-cute" monster is typically sold in blind boxespackaging that hides which specific item is inside until opened盲盒, meaning buyers only discover which figure they have bought after tearing open the packaging — a mechanic that has proven highly addictivecausing a strong, often harmful urge to repeat an activity令人上癮的.

Celebrity endorsements, including from K-pop and Hollywood stars spotted with the dolls clipped to designer handbags, have fuelled the toy's status as a fashion accessory as much as a collectible. Resale prices for rare editions can soar well above retail value, prompting queues outside stores and heated online bidding. For many Hong Kong teenagers, collecting and trading Labubu figures has become a social ritual, shared and compared constantly across social media platforms.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: The unpredictable reward of not knowing which figure is inside a blind box triggers a dopamine-driven urge to keep buying and unboxing.
  • Social media influence: Celebrity endorsements and viral unboxing videos on Instagram and TikTok rapidly amplify the toy's visibility and desirability.
  • Consumer culture: Limited editions and resale scarcity feed a culture of status-driven collecting, especially among image-conscious teenagers.
