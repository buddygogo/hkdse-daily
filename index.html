Queen Elizabeth Hospital's Move to Kai Tak Set to Be 'Seamless', Health Minister Pledges

RTHK ·18 September 2026

Hong Kong's Health Bureau has pledgedpromised firmly承諾 that the relocation of Queen Elizabeth Hospital's services to the newly built Kai Tak Hospital will be carried out through a phased relocationa move carried out in stages rather than all at once分階段遷置 to ensure a seamless transitiona smooth changeover with no disruption無縫過渡 for patients. Secretary for Health Lo Chung-mau confirmed that the first phase, comprising specialist out-patient and oncology services, is due to launch next month, with the new complex set to become Hong Kong's largest healthcare huba central facility bringing together many medical services醫療樞紐, boasting a bed capacitythe number of beds a hospital can provide床位容量 of 2,400 once fully operational.

Critics contend that transferring an entire hospital's clinical servicesmedical services involving direct patient treatment臨床服務 without disrupting ongoing patient care is an exceptionally delicate undertaking. Officials have therefore chosen to move out-patient and specialist departments first, while in-patient caremedical care requiring an overnight hospital stay住院護理 and accident and emergency services will not shift until 2028. Nevertheless, health officials maintain that this cautious, staggered approach will minimise disruption toreduce interference with as much as possible將對……的干擾減至最少 essential services throughout the transition.

By the same token, the project carries significance far beyond logistics. As Hong Kong contends with an ageing populationa population in which the proportion of elderly people is increasing人口老化, the new hub is expected to significantly reduce waiting timescut down substantially on how long patients must wait大幅縮短輪候時間 for over 1.8 million residents across four districts. Whether the handover proceeds as smoothly as promised remains to be seen, but it is widely regarded as a pivotal test of Hong Kong's capacity to expand its public healthcare systemgovernment-funded medical services available to all citizens公營醫療系統 to meet rising future demand.

HK Electric to Cut Fuel Charge for First Time in Four Months as CLP Raises Its Rate

SCMP ·26 September 2026

Hong Kong's two power suppliers are moving their electricity charges in opposite directions this October, illustrating just how volatile energy market volatilityunpredictable ups and downs in the price of fuel and power能源市場波動 has become. HK Electric will cut its fuel clause chargean extra fee added to electricity bills to reflect changing fuel costs燃料條款收費 by 1.4 cents to 59.3 cents per kilowatt-hour — its first reduction in four months — while CLP Power will raise its own charge by 1.1 cents to 46.2 cents. Both companies attribute the shifting figures to geopolitical tensionsinternational political conflicts that affect global markets地緣政治緊張局勢 and fluctuating international oil prices.

It is widely argued that such unpredictable tariff adjustmentschanges made to the price charged for a utility such as electricity電費調整 place additional strain on household budgetingthe process of planning how a family will spend its income家庭預算編制, particularly for lower-income families already grappling with a rising cost of livingthe amount of money needed to cover basic expenses生活成本. In response, both suppliers continue to offer limited rebate schemesprogrammes that return a portion of money already paid回扣計劃 to residential customers, though such measures only partially offset the overall financial burdenthe weight of financial obligations a person or family must bear經濟負擔 on ordinary households.

Nevertheless, economists caution that short-term rebates cannot substitute for longer-term solutions. Many argue it is imperative that Hong Kong diversify its energy supplyobtain energy from a wider variety of sources rather than relying on just one or two使能源供應多元化, investing further in renewable energypower generated from natural sources that are not depleted, such as solar or wind可再生能源 to reduce dependence on volatile international fuel markets. With this in mind, the debate over October's charges is likely to reignite broader discussion about how best to safeguard the city's long-term cost competitivenessthe ability to keep prices and expenses low relative to other places成本競爭力 and protect residents' disposable income.

Editorial Urges Renewed Focus on Student Mental Health as Term Gets Under Way

Sunday Examiner ·18 September 2026

As the new school term settles in, a recent editorial has renewed calls for greater attention to students' mental wellbeinga state of psychological and emotional health精神健康. Surveys conducted in recent years have consistently found that a significant proportion of secondary students report elevated levels of academic pressurestress arising from the demands of schoolwork and examinations學業壓力, driven largely by heavy workloads, an intensely competitive exam-centric culturean environment in which examination results are treated as the primary measure of success以考試為中心的文化, and high expectations from both parents and students themselves.

It is often observed that many young people remain reluctant to seek support when struggling, often citing fears of stigmanegative judgement attached to a particular condition or behaviour污名化 or of being misunderstood by those around them. By the same token, welfare groups have increasingly advocated a whole-of-society approacha strategy involving cooperation across families, schools and the wider community全社會參與的方針, encouraging closer collaboration between families and schools to build emotional resiliencethe capacity to cope with and recover from stress or adversity情緒韌性 without labelling struggling students as problem cases.

Nevertheless, there are encouraging signs of progress. School-based counsellingsupport services provided directly within schools by trained counsellors校本輔導 programmes and youth wellbeing initiatives have already reached large numbers of secondary students across the city, offering peer supportassistance and encouragement offered by fellow students facing similar challenges朋輩支援 alongside professional guidance. Commentators argue it is imperative that schools foster a stronger sense of belonginga feeling of being accepted and valued within a community歸屬感, recognising that academic achievement alone should not define a student's worth, and that a genuine work-life balance benefits learning as much as it benefits wellbeing.

BigBang Set for Four-Night Comeback at Kai Tak Stadium as 'Cosmos' World Tour Hits Hong Kong

Time Out Hong Kong ·15 September 2026

Legendary K-pop group BigBang are set for a triumphant comeback toura concert tour marking an artist's return after a period of absence復出巡演 to Hong Kong, with four nights scheduled at the newly built Kai Tak Stadium as part of their 2026–2027 world tour, "XX: Cosmos." Membership presalean early ticket sale open only to certain fans before general release優先售票 begins this Monday, with general ticket demandthe level of public interest in purchasing tickets for an event門票需求 expected to be intense given the group's enduring popularity two decades after their debut.

It is widely argued that BigBang's continued drawing power reflects the broader resilience of first-generation K-pop groups, whose appeal now spans multiple generations. Long-time fandoman organised community of devoted fans surrounding a particular artist or franchise粉絲文化 members, many now in their thirties and forties, are joined by younger listeners discovering the group retrospectively through social media clips. By the same token, this phenomenon has fuelled a wider nostalgia economythe commercial demand generated by consumers' fondness for the past懷舊經濟, in which reunion tours by veteran acts consistently sell out arenas worldwide.

Nevertheless, the concerts also serve as a testament to Hong Kong's continued status as a regional touring hub, with the new stadium's venue infrastructurethe physical facilities and systems that support large-scale events場地基建 helping to attract major international acts. The announcement has, however, sparked familiar debate over high ticket prices and the risk of ticket scalpingthe illegal or unethical resale of tickets at inflated prices黃牛炒票. Even so, for many fans, the chance to relive a defining era of K-pop in person is expected to outweigh such concerns entirely.

👑 Disney Princesses' 'Pop Star' Dance Moves Spark Online Debate at Hong Kong Disneyland

Social Trend

A clip from Hong Kong Disneyland has ignited polarised reactionsstrongly divided opinions, with people taking opposing sides兩極化的反應 online after several Disney princesses swapped their familiar ballroom waltz for a contemporary, beat-driven routinea modern dance style set closely to the rhythm of a pop song貼合節拍的現代舞步 set to a pop song. Clips shared by fan accounts on TikTok and Instagram quickly went viral, with some viewers praising the fresh energy and others complaining that the modern choreography breaks the illusionspoils the sense that something magical or fictional is real打破了幻想感 long associated with the characters.

Comments ranged from enthusiastic ('so much fun!') to disapproving ('this is not fairytale'), with the debate spreading well beyond Hong Kong as international Disney fan outlets picked up the footage. The episode has become a small but telling case study in how theme parks balance brand modernisationthe process of updating a brand's image to stay current品牌現代化 against the expectations of long-time audiences.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Generational clash: Long-time fans who grew up with the classic waltz routines expect timeless, storybook-style choreography, while younger viewers embrace more contemporary, pop-influenced performance styles.
  • Social media amplification: Short video clips of the routine spread rapidly once fan accounts and Disney news outlets reposted them, turning a single park appearance into a wider cross-platform debate.
  • Brand modernisation pressure: Theme parks face growing commercial pressure to refresh long-running character performances so that they stay relevant to a generation raised on viral dance trends.
