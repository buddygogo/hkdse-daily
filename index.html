Hong Kong Launches AI Privacy Sandbox to Safeguard Students' Data in Schools

OpenGov Asia ·28 August 2026

Hong Kong has rolled outto launch or introduce something推出 a new AI Privacy Sandbox for Schools, a government-backed scheme that allows publicly funded primary and secondary schools to trial artificial intelligence tools in a controlled environmenta regulated setting designed to limit risk受控環境 before adopting them more widely. Applications for the first phase are open to schools until 30 October 2026, with a briefing session having already been held to explain how the scheme will operate. Only fifteen schools will be selected to take part in the initial six-month pilot.

It is widely argued that the initiative reflects a broader attempt to strike a balanceto achieve an even compromise between two demands取得平衡 between technological advancement and the protection of students' personal data. Critics contend that six months is too short a period to assess the long-term implicationsto evaluate the lasting effects or consequences評估長遠影響 of AI adoption in classrooms, particularly given how quickly the technology evolves. Nevertheless, supporters maintain that a cautious, phased approacha plan carried out in stages rather than all at once分階段方式 is preferable to rushing untested systems into daily use. By the same token, parents' groups have called for greater transparency, urging the government to discloseto make information known publicly披露 exactly what data each participating school collects. As Hong Kong positions itself as a regional AI hub, this small-scale trial may prove to be an important litmus testa decisive indicator of future success or failure試金石 for how the city balances innovation with accountability in education.
