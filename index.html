Policy Address Unveils Northern Metropolis Push and New 'AI City Brain'

The Standard ·16 September 2026

Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu delivered his fifth Policy Address on Wednesday, unveilingto reveal or present something publicly for the first time揭示；公開推出 a blueprint billed as ‘A Strategic Vision for a Bright New Era.’ It is anchored infirmly based on or connected to something以……為基礎 Hong Kong’s inaugural Five-Year Plan for Economic and Social Development (2026-2030), which sets out ambitious targets for reshaping the city’s economy and skyline alike. Central to this vision is the Northern Metropolis, a vast development spanning some 300 square kilometres near the border with mainland China, which Mr Lee confirmed would see all nine of its New Development Areas break ground in the coming year.

By the same token, the government pledged to modernise city governance through an ambitious “AI City Brain”, an integrated management system designed to streamlineto make a process simpler or more efficient使（程序）更簡化、更有效率 everything from traffic flow to emergency response. Officials also committed to replacing or rehabilitating 350 kilometres of ageing infrastructurepublic facilities and systems that are old and deteriorating老化的基礎設施, including corroded water mains, over the next five years. Nearby, a Northern Metropolis University Town spanning more than 1,000 hectares is envisaged as a hub for interdisciplinary research, with a new MTR station near the Hong Kong Science Park expected to strengthen the area’s role as a magnet for talent and investmentsomething that strongly attracts capable people and money人才與投資的磁石.

It is widely argued that the success of such initiatives will ultimately be judged by whether ordinary residents feel the benefits in their daily lives. Once fully developed, the Northern Metropolis is projected to house 2.5 million people and generate 650,000 jobs, offering secondary students a tangiblereal, concrete, and able to be clearly perceived實在的；具體可見的 glimpse of the careers and communities that may await them. Critics contend, nevertheless, that delivering on such a sprawling agendaa very large, wide-ranging plan or set of goals龐大而分散的議程 within budget and on schedule will prove to be no small feat.

Hong Kong's Largest Public Hospital to Open in Phases From October

OpenGov Asia ·28 September 2026

Hong Kong's largest public hospital is finally set to swing open its doorsto officially open and begin operating正式開放；開始運作 next week, as Kai Tak Hospital begins accepting patients in phases from 5 October. The Specialist Out-patient Clinic Block and the Oncology Block will be the first to launch, with follow-up consultations, blood-drawing services and the Family Medicine Integrated Centre transferred progressively from the nearby Queen Elizabeth Hospital. Once fully operational, Kai Tak Hospital will boast a gross floor areathe total built area of a building, floor by floor建築總樓面面積 of 500,000 square metres and 2,400 beds, making it the largest healthcare hub in the city by both measures.

It is widely argued that the new facility could not come at a more opportune time. The hospital is designed to relieve pressure onto reduce the burden or strain placed on something緩解……的壓力 Queen Elizabeth Hospital, which has long struggled with overcrowding, and will serve as the flagship of the Kowloon Central Cluster, covering Yau Tsim Mong, Kowloon City, Wong Tai Sin and Kwun Tong districts, home to more than 1.8 million residents. The Hospital Authority has set up a dedicated teama group of staff assigned specifically to one task專責小組 to contact affected patients directly, notifying them of new appointment details by phone, text message, post and its mobile application.

Nevertheless, health officials have cautioned that the transition will take time, with full inpatient care, surgery and round-the-clock accident and emergency services not scheduled until 2028. Critics contend that the phased rolloutthe gradual introduction of something in stages rather than all at once分階段推行 may cause short-term confusion among patients, particularly the elderly. By the same token, few dispute that the hospital represents a significant milestonean important stage or achievement in a process重要的里程碑 in Hong Kong's efforts to expand and modernise its public healthcare system for the coming decades.

Hong Kong Inflation Holds at 1.7% as Utility and Transport Costs Bite

The Standard ·23 September 2026

Hong Kong's Census and Statistics Department reported this week that the Composite Consumer Price Index rose 1.7 percent year-on-year in August, matching July's increase and meeting market expectations. Excluding the effect of one-off government relief measures, the underlying inflation ratethe rate of price increase after removing temporary distortions such as subsidies基本通脹率 stood at 1.9 percent, unchanged from the previous month. It is widely argued that the figures reflect an economy that continues to hum along steadily, even as certain household expensesthe costs a family regularly pays for daily living住戶開支 climbed sharply.

Nevertheless, not all categories moved in the same direction. Electricity, gas and water charges surged 11.5 percent compared with a year earlier, by far the steepest rise of any component, while miscellaneous services and transport costs increased 4.7 percent and 3.9 percent respectively. By the same token, the cost of eating out and durable goods edged up only modestly, and housing costs, which make up a significant share of the basketthe proportion that an item represents within a set of goods used to measure prices在（物價）籃子中所佔的比例, rose by a mere 0.1 percent.

Critics contend that the headline figure understates the squeeze on household budgetsthe financial pressure felt by families as costs rise faster than income對家庭預算的壓力, given how disproportionately utility bills have climbed. The government has since revised its full-year forecast upward, now projecting headline inflation of 2.6 percent for 2026, while Standard Chartered raised its own estimate to 2.1 percent, citing higher energy and imported input prices. This comes against a backdrop of robust economic growthstrong and healthy expansion of the economy強勁的經濟增長, with gross domestic product having expanded 5.9 percent in the first quarter, its best performance in five years.

Hong Kong Opens First Dedicated Psychiatric Facility for Children and Teenagers

SCMP ·19 August 2026

Hong Kong has unveiled its first psychiatric facility dedicated exclusively to patients under the age of 18, marking a significant expansion of youth mental health services at the newly redeveloped Kwai Chung Hospital. The Child and Adolescent Block forms part of a wider redevelopment schemea large-scale project to rebuild and modernise existing facilities重建計劃 that has increased the hospital's total floor area from 80,000 to 134,000 square metres, allowing it to eventually provide 1,000 beds for psychiatric patients of all ages.

It is widely argued that the facility's design reflects a deliberate shift towards more therapeutic environmentssettings specifically designed to aid recovery and healing具療癒作用的環境. Wards feature spacious six-bedded and single rooms, each equipped with an en-suite bathrooma bathroom directly connected to and accessible only from a bedroom套內浴室, while every ward opens onto a garden where young patients may relax outdoors accompanied by healthcare staff. Outpatient psychiatric services began relocating to the new blocks in stages from March, with inpatient wards following from late July.

Nevertheless, the timing carries added weight, given growing alarm over student wellbeing since the start of the new school term. Critics contend that infrastructure alone cannot resolvephysical facilities by themselves are insufficient to fix an underlying problem單靠基建設施無法解決 a crisis rooted in academic pressure, housing unaffordability and digital overload. By the same token, mental health advocates have broadly welcomed the new facility as a long-overdue stepping stonean action or event that helps achieve a longer-term goal踏腳石；邁向目標的一步 towards a healthcare system better equipped to support Hong Kong's younger generation.

🧸 The Labubu Blind-Box Craze Won't Let Go

Social Trend

A small, wide-eyed, sharp-toothed plush monster named Labubu has spent much of 2026 colonisingto spread through and dominate a space, used figuratively here佔據；蔓延至 feeds across TikTok, Instagram and Threads. Originally designed by Hong Kong-based artist Kasing Lung and sold by mainland retailer Pop Mart, the figure is distributed through blind boxessealed packaging that hides which specific item a buyer will receive盒玩；盲盒, meaning buyers do not know which variant, common or rare, they will receive until they open the box.

Unboxing videos, styling tutorials and even “authenticationthe process of verifying that an item is genuine rather than counterfeit驗證真偽” clips have racked up billions of views, with the toys now commonly clipped onto handbags as status-signalling accessories among Hong Kong teenagers and young professionals alike. Resale prices for rare variants have, in some cases, reached many times the original retail price.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: The unpredictable reward of not knowing which figure lies inside the box activates the brain's dopamine-driven reward system, encouraging repeat purchases.
  • Social media influence: Unboxing and styling videos from influencers spread the trend rapidly, turning a toy collection into a shareable, performative identity marker.
  • Consumer culture: Rising discretionary income among young people, combined with a fear of missing out, has fuelled a thriving resale market for rare figures.
