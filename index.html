Hong Kong Parents Urged to Vaccinate Children as Summer Flu Cases Climb

Hong Kong Free Press ·14 September 2026

Hong Kong health authorities have issued a fresh appeal for parents to vaccinate their children as the city faces a marked increase in flu-related hospitalisations among young people. According to recent public health data, a growing number of paediatric patientspatients who are children兒科病人 have presented with high fevers exceeding 40 degrees Celsius that persistcontinue without stopping持續 for several days, alongside other severe influenza symptoms. Doctors have noted that this summer's flu season has proven unusually persistent, straining paediatric wards and prompting renewed calls for widespread immunisationthe process of being made immune to a disease, typically by vaccination免疫接種 before the situation worsens further.

It is widely argued that early vaccination remains the most effective safeguard against severe illness, particularly among younger children whose immune systems are less equipped to fight off aggressive strains of the virus. Public health experts contend that a robust vaccination programme not only protects individual children but also plays a pivotal role inis extremely important in擔當關鍵角色 curbing transmissionpreventing a disease from spreading further遏止傳播 within schools and households, thereby easing pressure on an already stretched healthcare system. By the same token, health officials argue that timely intervention could prevent a repeat of previous flu seasons, during which emergency departments were pushed to capacity.

Nevertheless, critics contend that vaccine uptake among Hong Kong families remains inconsistent, hampered by lingering vaccine hesitancyreluctance to be vaccinated despite the vaccine being available疫苗猶豫 and a lack of awareness regarding the severity of this year's outbreak. Some parents remain reluctant, citing mild side effects or scepticism about the necessity of annual immunisation for otherwise healthy children. Proponents counter that the current spike in severe cases should serve as a wake-up callan event that makes people realise a problem needs urgent attention警號, urging households to weigh the proven benefits of vaccination against comparatively minor risks. Whether public health messaging can shift entrenched attitudesfirmly held beliefs that are difficult to change根深蒂固的態度 in time to curb this outbreak remains to be seen, but the accumulating clinical evidenceevidence obtained through medical observation or treatment臨床證據 lends considerable weight to the case for prompt action.

Consumer Watchdog Questions Value of Supermarket 'Buy-One-Get-One-Free' Deals

South China Morning Post ·14 September 2026

Hong Kong's Consumer Council has cast doubt onraised doubts about something質疑 the genuine value of "buy-one-get-one-free" and bulk-buy promotionsdiscounts offered for purchasing items in large quantities大量購買優惠 offered by the city's three major supermarket chains — AEON, ParknShop and Wellcome. A year-long survey of 222 everyday products, ranging from instant noodles to tissue paper, found that the single-item price during some promotional periods was in fact higher than the price charged outside the promotion, meaning shoppers saved considerably less than the advertised discount implied, if anything at all. The watchdog further noted that no single "bargain day" applies consistently across the three chains, complicating efforts by consumers to plan their grocery shopping around genuine savings.

It is widely argued that such findings expose a troubling gap between the perceived and actual value of retail promotions, particularly at a time when households are already grappling with a rising cost of living. Consumer advocates contend that headline discounts of around fifty percent create a powerful psychological incentivea mental motivation that encourages a particular behaviour心理誘因 to purchase, even when the underlying saving is minimal or non-existent. By the same token, the Council's call for shoppers to compare unit prices rather than relying on promotional labels reflects a broader push towards greater price transparencyopenness and clarity about how prices are set價格透明度, a principle increasingly regarded as essential to consumer protectionmeasures to safeguard the rights and interests of buyers消費者保障 in a competitive market.

Nevertheless, industry representatives might counter that pricing strategies of this nature are commonplace across global retail markets and do not necessarily amount to deliberate deception, given that prices naturally fluctuate according to supply costs and inventory cycles. Critics, however, maintain that supermarkets bear a responsibility to ensure promotional labellinglabels used to advertise discounts or special offers促銷標籤 accurately reflects genuine savings, especially given the disproportionate impactan effect that is not fairly or reasonably proportioned不成比例的影響 of inflated grocery bills on lower-income families. Whether this report will prompt tighter regulation of promotional pricing remains uncertain, but it has undeniably reignited public debatecaused a discussion or disagreement to start again重燃公眾爭論 over transparency and accountabilitythe state of being responsible for one's actions問責性 in Hong Kong's retail sector.

Cantopop Legend Alan Tam Returns to Hong Kong Coliseum After 11-Year Hiatus

Dim Sum Daily ·11 September 2026

Cantopop veteran Alan Tam returned to the Hong Kong Coliseum on 11 September for the first time in eleven years, launching his "Unforgettable" World Tour – Grand Finale before a packed arena. The opening run, billed as the "Chapter of Gratitude," continues until 15 September, with a second block, the "Chapter of Unchanged Love," scheduled to follow from 18 to 22 September. Across the ten-show residencya series of performances by the same artist at one venue over a period of time駐場演出, the singer has revisited beloved hits from Cantopop's golden era of the 1980s, weaving personal anecdotes and expressions of gratitude to long-time fans between musical numbers.

It is widely argued that the enduring appeal of veteran performers such as Tam speaks to a deep well of nostalgia among Hong Kong audiences, many of whom grew up with his music during a formative period for the local entertainment industry. Cultural commentators contend that such concerts do more than simply entertain; they serve as a form of collective memoryshared memories held by a community about its cultural past集體回憶, reconnecting audiences with a shared cultural heritagetraditions, values and achievements passed down within a society文化遺產 at a time when K-pop and Mandopop dominate much of the regional music scene. By the same token, Tam's ability to sell out an eleven-year-return residency underscores the extent to which Cantopop, once thought to be in decline, continues to command genuine commercial and emotional loyaltycontinued support driven by both profit and feeling商業與情感上的忠誠度.

Nevertheless, some critics contend that the local music industry's continued reliance on veteran acts risks overshadowing emerging homegrown talentnew artists developing within the local scene冒起的本地新秀, who often struggle to secure comparable stage time and media attention. Sceptics further point out that concerts of this nature tend to draw an ageing fanbasea group of supporters that is growing older over time日漸老化的粉絲群, raising questions about whether Cantopop can cultivate a new generation of listeners rather than simply relying on nostalgia. Supporters counter that veteran performers like Tam, by continuing to headline major venues, help sustain the broader live music ecosystemthe interconnected network supporting live performances現場音樂生態系統 that newer artists ultimately benefit from. Whether nostalgia alone can secure Cantopop's long-term future remains an open question, but Tam's triumphant returna highly successful comeback after an absence凱旋歸來 to the Coliseum suggests its emotional pull is far from fading.

Quality Concerns Prompt Further Tests on Concrete Used in Tung Chung Public Housing Blocks

South China Morning Post ·12 September 2026

Quality concerns have prompted the Housing Department to order further testing of concrete used in three blocks of a public housing development under construction in Tung Chung, on Lantau Island. Between late August and early September, routine cube testsstandard tests used to check the compressive strength of concrete立方體強度測試 flagged irregularities in the concrete used for Blocks 3, 4 and 5 of the Tung Chung Area 42 project, prompting the site contractor, Able Engineering Company Limited, to switch concrete suppliers as a precautionary measurean action taken in advance to prevent a possible problem預防措施. Officials have since instructed the contractor to submit a detailed report along with proposals for any necessary corrective and repair work, while engineers have ordered additional core samples to be taken to verify whether the concrete's compressive strengththe ability of a material to withstand pushing forces without breaking抗壓強度 meets required design specifications.

It is widely argued that rigorous quality control in public housing construction is non-negotiablesomething that must be accepted and cannot be changed or argued about不容妥協, given that any structural compromise could have far-reaching consequences for residents' safety and long-term confidence in publicly funded housing. Housing advocates contend that the prompt identification of the issue through routine testing demonstrates that existing quality assurance mechanismssystems designed to ensure a consistent standard of quality品質保證機制 are functioning as intended, catching potential problems before flats are handed over to tenants. By the same token, officials argue that transparency in reporting such concerns, rather than concealing them, is essential to maintaining public trustpreserving people's confidence in an institution or process維持公眾信任 in a construction sector that has faced scrutiny over similar incidents in the past.

Nevertheless, critics contend that recurring quality issues across public housing projects raise uncomfortable questions about oversight of contractors amid an intensifying drive to accelerate housing supply. Given Hong Kong's chronic housing shortagea persistent, long-term lack of available housing長期房屋短缺 and lengthy waiting lists for public rental flats, any delay caused by remedial workwork carried out to correct or repair a defect補救工程 risks compounding an already pressing problem, leaving would-be tenants waiting even longer. Supporters of the current approach counter that swift, thorough testing now is preferable to rushing flawed structures into occupation, even if it means a short-term setback to delivery targets. Whether this incident will lead to tighter contractor oversight across future projects remains to be seen, but it has undeniably placed renewed scrutinyincreased or repeated close examination重新受到審視 on construction quality standards in Hong Kong's public housing pipeline.

🧵 HK Youth Turn to Threads for 'Burnout' and Boundary-Setting Talk

Social Trend

Among Hong Kong's 18-to-34 age group, Threadsa text-based social media app linked to InstagramThreads（社交媒體應用程式） has quietly become a go-to space for candid conversations about burnoutextreme physical or emotional exhaustion caused by prolonged stress身心俱疲 and workplace boundary-setting. Awareness of the platform locally has surged, with usage nearly tripling in under two years, and posts on remote work, professional identity and personal limits now rank among the highest-performing content. Rather than glossy lifestyle posts, users appear drawn to relatableeasy to identify with or understand from personal experience容易產生共鳴的, unpolished confessions about work-life balance, suggesting a shift in what young Hongkongers value online.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: Sharing struggles with burnout offers young users a sense of validation and reduces feelings of isolation in a highly competitive work culture.
  • Social media influence: A text-first, less curated format lowers the pressure to appear perfect, encouraging more honest self-disclosure than image-heavy platforms.
  • Workplace pressures: Long working hours and job insecurity amid Hong Kong's competitive economy make burnout a widely shared experience worth discussing openly.
