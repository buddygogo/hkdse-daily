Education Bureau unveils Values Education Curriculum Framework for all Hong Kong schools

GovHK / Education Bureau ·16 April 2026

The Education Bureau has unveiledto reveal or make public公布 the Values Education Curriculum Framework (2026), a roadmap that all primary and secondary schools in Hong Kong are required to implementto put a plan into effect實施 from the 2026/27 school year onwards. It is widely argued that the framework, compiled by the Standing Committee on Values Education, marks one of the most significant shifts in moral and civic education in recent years, as it formally sets out twelve core values — including perseverance, integrity, empathy and national identity — that schools are expected to weave into everyday teaching.

Critics contend that mandating such a broad and prescriptive set of values risks reducing a nuanced subject to a tick-box exercisea task completed superficially just to satisfy a requirement形式主義的任務, leaving teachers with little room to encourage independent moral reasoning among students. Supporters, however, maintain that a coherent frameworka logically connected and consistent set of principles連貫一致的框架 is long overdue, arguing that fragmented, ad hoc lessons have previously failed to cultivate a strong sense ofto develop and nurture over time培養強烈的……感 responsibility and belonging among young people. The framework also places notable emphasis on Chinese culture, national education and media and information literacythe ability to critically evaluate and responsibly use information and digital media媒體與資訊素養, reflecting growing official concern over how students navigate an increasingly complex online environment.

Nevertheless, questions remain over how schools with limited resources will be able to translate policy into practiceto turn an abstract plan into concrete, workable action將政策轉化為實踐 without additional funding or teacher training. By the same token, some educators worry that an overly centralised approach could stifleto prevent something from developing or being expressed freely扼殺 the flexibility that individual schools currently enjoy in tailoring moral education to their own student populations. As the 2026/27 school year approaches, it is expected that schools across the city will begin drawing up detailed implementation plans, with the Bureau pledging further guidance and support in the coming months.

From Europe to Hong Kong: connecting tech talent to Greater Bay Area opportunities

The Manila Times (Media Outreach Newswire) ·14 September 2026

Hong Kong is positioning itselfto establish oneself in a favourable role or status將自己定位為 as a gateway connecting European technology talent with opportunities across the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, following a hackathon co-organised by Hong Kong Talent Engage and the EuroTech Federation. According to organisers, more than 90 per centnine-tenths, used here to indicate an overwhelming majority百分之九十 of the European finalists reported a stronger interest in relocating to Hong Kong or the wider GBA region after taking part in the programme, which gave winning teams a first-hand look at the city's innovation ecosystem.

It is widely argued that such initiatives play a pivotal role ina role that is central and highly influential to an outcome關鍵作用 addressing Hong Kong's long-standing shortage of specialised technology talent, particularly in fields such as artificial intelligence, robotics and advanced manufacturing. Proponents point out that the scheme not only exposes overseas talent to local opportunities but also helps to cultivate cross-border collaborationto encourage and develop cooperative links across different regions培養跨境合作 between European start-ups and Mainland Chinese enterprises within the Bay Area. Critics, however, caution that one-off exposure programmesshort, isolated initiatives rather than sustained, long-term ones一次性的體驗計劃 are unlikely to translate into lasting relocation decisions unless they are backed by competitive salaries, housing support and a smoother visa process.

Nevertheless, the initiative comes at a time when Hong Kong has been doubling down onto increase commitment or effort towards something加倍投入 its ambitions to become a regional artificial intelligence hub, with a growing number of Mainland AI firms choosing to list on the Hong Kong stock exchange this year. By the same token, officials argue that talent attraction and capital markets development must go hand in hand if the city is to maintain its competitive edgeto preserve an advantage over rivals保持競爭優勢 as a global centre for innovation and technology in the years ahead.

Hong Kong's first International Lantern Spectacular lights up Victoria Park for Mid-Autumn Festival

The Standard ·15 September 2026

Hong Kong's first International Lantern Spectacular has opened its doorsto become available for public visits正式開放 at Victoria Park ahead of this year's Mid-Autumn Festival, running from 17 to 27 September under the theme “Reunion”. Organised by the Hong Kong Tourism Board alongside the Leisure and Cultural Services Department's traditional Lantern Carnival, the event brings together more than 3,000 lanterns from twenty countries and regions, including Nanjing Qinhuai masterpieces recognised as intangible cultural heritagetraditions, skills and practices passed down and officially recognised as part of a community's heritage非物質文化遺產 and locally handcrafted pieces at the Hong Kong Lantern Galleria.

It is widely argued that the event reflects a deliberate effort to reinvigorateto give new energy or strength to something為……注入新活力 a centuries-old festival for younger audiences, many of whom have grown up more familiar with imported holidays than with traditional Chinese customs. Alongside the international display, more than 800 lanterns have also been put up along Lee Tung Avenue in Wan Chai, while the Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance — a tradition dating back to the 1880s and now honoured as National Intangible Cultural Heritage — will once again weave through the neighbourhood's narrow lanes over three nights.

Nevertheless, some cultural commentators contend that large-scale commercial spectaclesbig, heavily promoted public events organised chiefly for economic or tourism benefit大型商業盛事 risk overshadowing the quieter, more intimate customs traditionally associated with Mid-Autumn Festival, such as family reunion dinners and mooncake-sharing. By the same token, tourism officials maintain that events of this scale are essential if Hong Kong is to showcase its cultural identityto display and promote the distinctive traditions and values of a place展示其文化身份 on the international stage while also drawing much-needed visitor numbers back to the city.

Hong Kong Green Week 2026 spotlights sustainable finance and climate resilience

Hong Kong Green Week / HKMA ·11 September 2026

Hong Kong Green Week 2026, held from 7 to 11 September under the theme “Future-proofing Sustainable Growth”, brought together financial regulators, investors and sustainability experts for a series of events including the HKMA-organised Green and Sustainable Finance conferences and the AIIB-HKMA Joint Conference on mobilising private capitalto encourage and organise privately owned funds to be invested towards a goal調動私人資本 for climate resilience. It is widely argued that the week-long programme, now an established fixture on the city's calendar, underscores Hong Kong's ambition to position itself as Asia's leading hub for green and sustainable financefinancial products and investment specifically directed at environmentally friendly projects綠色和可持續金融.

Proponents point out that Hong Kong has made tangible progressreal, measurable improvement rather than something abstract or symbolic實質進展 on environmental fronts in recent years, with the annual average concentration of major air pollutants having fallen sharply since 2004 and the number of registered electric vehicles rising roughly six-fold since 2021. Critics, however, contend that financial conferences and roadmaps alone cannot substitute forto serve as a replacement for something取代 concrete emissions-reduction policy, noting that the government has yet to publish a detailed timetable for achieving its 2050 carbon neutrality target.

Nevertheless, organisers maintain that channelling private investment intodirecting financial resources from private, non-government sources towards a specific purpose將私人投資引導至 climate adaptation projects is precisely what will allow the city to close the funding gap that public spending alone cannot cover. By the same token, environmental groups argue that Hong Kong must pair its financial ambitions with binding, measurable commitmentspromises that are legally or formally required to be fulfilled and can be objectively assessed具約束力、可量度的承諾 if the city's sustainability rhetoric is to be matched by real-world outcomes.

🐉 Chinamaxxinga Gen Z internet trend of embracing and performing Chinese cultural habits and aesthetics online「中式生活風」網絡熱潮: the trend putting Chinese culture centre stage

Social Trend

A viral trend known as “Chinamaxxing” has taken hold among mostly Western Gen Z users on TikTok and Instagram, who are embracingto accept and adopt something enthusiastically欣然接受 Chinese lifestyle habits — drinking hot water instead of iced coffee, wearing house slippers, and following the 養生 (yangsheng) philosophy of aligning daily habits with the seasons. Influencers, including members of the Chinese diaspora, post videos on herbal skincare and traditional wellness routines, describing themselves as entering “a very Chinese time” in their lives.

Hong Kong youth have picked up on the trend too, sharing their own takes on traditional customs and framing them as aesthetically appealingattractive or pleasing in appearance or style具美感吸引力的 lifestyle choices rather than outdated habits. The trend has sparked debate online over whether it represents genuine cultural appreciation or a form of cultural appropriationthe adoption of elements of one culture by members of another, often without understanding or proper acknowledgement文化挪用, particularly given the trend's origins among non-Chinese creators.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: Slower, ritual-based routines such as herbal tea and early bedtimes offer a sense of calm and control that appeals to anxious, overstimulated young users.
  • Social media influence: Diaspora influencers with large followings have amplified the trend rapidly, turning niche cultural practices into globally recognisable content formats.
  • Soft power and identity: The trend has been read by commentators as boosting China's cultural soft power, while prompting Hong Kong youth to reconsider their own relationship with traditional customs.
