Hong Kong Chief Executive Vows to Ensure Safety as AI Push Accelerates

RTHK ·16 September 2026

In his latest Policy Address, delivered on 16 September, Chief Executive John Lee reaffirmed the government's determination to transform Hong Kong into a regional powerhouse for artificial intelligencea leading centre driving AI development in the region區域人工智能強國. It is widely argued that the city's long-term competitiveness now hinges on its ability to convert academic research into commercial applicationturn university research into marketable products or services將學術研究轉化為商業應用. Officials have set an ambitious target: total domestic expenditure on innovation activitiesthe overall amount spent locally on research and innovation本地創新活動總開支 is to reach three per cent of GDP after 2030, almost double the 1.63 per cent recorded in 2024. The initiative will be underpinned by three new innovation parks and an expanded Northern Metropolis huba planned development zone in northern Hong Kong dedicated to technology and innovation北部都會區樞紐 devoted to AI, robotics and health technology.

Nevertheless, the government has been careful to temper enthusiasm for rapid technological advancement with a parallel emphasis on regulation. Mr Lee pledged that safety considerations would play a pivotal role inbe a crucial, central factor in擔當關鍵角色 shaping how AI is deployed across finance, healthcare and public administration. New proposals addressing deepfakes and algorithmic accountabilitythe principle that those who design and deploy algorithms must answer for their effects演算法問責 are expected to be tabled in the coming legislative session. Critics contend that without robust oversightstrong, effective supervision and monitoring嚴謹的監管, the rapid rollout of AI systems could outpace the safeguards needed to protect ordinary citizens from misinformation and data misuse.

By the same token, the plan has implications that extend well beyond the technology sector itself. Analysts suggest that as automation becomes more deeply embedded in daily operations, the labour market will be reshaped considerablychanged substantially in structure or form大幅重塑, with some traditional roles rendered obsolete while entirely new career pathways emerge. For Hong Kong's students, the message is unambiguous: digital literacythe ability to use, understand and evaluate digital technology effectively數碼素養 and adaptability are no longer optional extras but prerequisites for future employabilityessential conditions one must meet to be considered employable later on未來就業能力的先決條件 in an economy increasingly driven by intelligent systems.

Education Chief Unveils School Merger Scheme Amid Falling Enrolment

RTHK ·18 September 2026

Secretary for Education Christine Choi announced on 18 September that a new scheme encouraging publicly funded primary and secondary schools to merge would be launched as early as the 2027/28 academic year. It is widely acknowledged that Hong Kong's declining birth ratethe ongoing fall in the number of babies born each year出生率下降 has placed considerable strain on smaller schools, with more than 120 aided and government primary schools having already trimmed the number ofreduced the quantity of削減……的數目 Primary One classes they offer. The scheme is intended to consolidate resourcescombine and streamline resources for greater efficiency整合資源 rather than force outright closures, allowing schools facing dwindling enrolment to pool staff, facilities and funding.

Nevertheless, the proposal has proven contentiousturned out to be a source of dispute or disagreement引起爭議 among educators and parents alike. Critics contend that mergers risk eroding the distinct charactergradually wearing away the unique identity侵蝕獨特特色 and community ties that many long-established schools have cultivated over decades. Some school sponsoring bodies have voiced concern that consolidation could be imposed too hastily, without adequate consultationsufficient discussion with relevant stakeholders beforehand充分諮詢 with staff and parents who fear job losses or disruption to students' learning environments. By the same token, supporters argue that maintaining chronically under-enrolled schools is unsustainableunable to be maintained at the current level over the long term不可持續的 and ultimately does students a disservicecauses harm despite good intentions對學生造成損害, as limited resources make it difficult to offer a broad curriculum.

For students themselves, the implications are considerable. A merger could mean a change of campus, new classmates, and an adjustment perioda span of time needed to become accustomed to new circumstances適應期 that some worry may affect academic performance and emotional wellbeing. Education Bureau officials have stressed that further details, including eligibility criteria and transitional supportassistance provided during a period of change過渡支援, will be announced in due course, and that affected schools will be given sufficient lead time to prepare.

Hong Kong Weighs Tougher Climate Finance Disclosures for Banks

Bloomberg ·21 September 2026

Hong Kong's financial regulator is exploring new rules that would require banks to disclose more information about how they fund climate-related projectsinitiatives connected to mitigating or adapting to climate change氣候相關項目, according to a report published on 21 September. It is widely argued that greater transparency is essential if the city's sustainable finance ambitions are to be taken seriously by international investors. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority plans to conduct a territory-wide survey next year examining how lenders are applying the existing sustainable finance taxonomyan official classification system distinguishing genuinely green investments from others可持續金融分類法.

The move comes amid growing scrutiny of so-called greenwashingthe practice of overstating or falsely claiming the environmental benefits of an activity漂綠, whereby financial institutions overstate the environmental benefits of the projects they fund. Nevertheless, banking industry representatives have cautioned that overly stringent disclosure requirements could increase compliance coststhe expenses incurred in meeting regulatory requirements合規成本 and discourage smaller lenders from participating in the green finance market altogether. By the same token, environmental groups maintain that without mandatory and standardised reportingcompulsory reporting that follows a uniform, consistent format強制性及標準化匯報, it remains difficult to hold institutions accountable for their claims, undermining public confidenceweakening the trust the public places in an institution or system削弱公眾信心 in the sector as a whole.

The initiative forms part of a broader effort to position Hong Kong as a hub for climate finance in the Asia-Pacific region, complementing measures such as the Monetary Authority's ongoing consultation on decarbonisation strategiesplans aimed at reducing carbon emissions across an economy or sector去碳化策略 for the banking sector. Given that climate change poses mounting risks to the financial system, officials argue that closer scrutiny of how capital is allocated is no longer a peripheral concern but a central pillar of long-term economic resiliencethe capacity of an economy to withstand and recover from shocks經濟韌性.

Victoria Park Lights Up for Hong Kong's First International Lantern Spectacular

The Standard ·17 September 2026

Victoria Park has been transformed into a dazzling showcase of lantern craftsmanshipthe skilled art of designing and making decorative lanterns花燈工藝 this Mid-Autumn Festival, as the Hong Kong Tourism Board stages its inaugural International Lantern Spectacularthe first-ever event of its kind首屆國際花燈盛會. Running from 17 to 27 September under the theme of "Reunion", the free exhibition brings together more than 3,000 lanterns from 20 countries and regions, ranging from delicate silk creations from South Korea to vibrant patchwork designs from Brazil. It is widely regarded as one of the most ambitious cultural undertakings the Tourism Board has attempted in recent years.

The centrepiece of the display features four giant Qinhuai lanterns crafted by artisans from Nanjing using a technique dating back some 1,700 years. Nevertheless, local heritage has not been overshadowed by international contributions: hundreds of lanterns handcrafted by Hong Kong artisans pay tribute to beloved local delicacies, with playful designs incorporating egg waffles, milk tea and curry fishballs. By the same token, the festivities extend beyond Victoria Park, with the centuries-old Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, now inscribed on the national list of intangible cultural heritagecultural practices and traditions, as opposed to physical artefacts, recognised as worth preserving非物質文化遺產, due to wind through the streets from 24 to 26 September.

Cultural commentators contend that such large-scale celebrations play a pivotal role inare a crucial, central factor in擔當關鍵角色 reinforcing a sense of collective identitya shared sense of belonging to a community or culture集體身份認同, particularly among younger residents who may otherwise engage with time-honoured customstraditions that have been respected and followed for a very long time歷久不衰的習俗 only superficially. By blending tradition with a contemporary, highly shareableeasily and widely spread on social media極易於分享的 visual spectacle, organisers hope to rekindle enthusiasm for heritage practices that might otherwise risk fading from public consciousnessgradually being forgotten by the general public從公眾意識中消失.

🫔 Lantern Carnival Selfie Spots Go Viral Across Hong Kong Social Media

Social Trend

As Victoria Park's International Lantern Spectacular draws record crowds this Mid-Autumn Festival, TikTok and Instagram feeds across Hong Kong have been flooded with clips of the exhibition's most photogenic displays. It is widely observed that the "Feast of Light" installationa themed lantern display featuring food-shaped designs《光影盛宴》燈飾, featuring giant lanterns shaped like egg waffles and milk tea cups, has become the single most photographed spot in the park, with visitors queuing patiently for a chance to capture the perfect shot. Nevertheless, some commentators have noted that the rush to document the event for social media sometimes overshadows genuine appreciationtakes attention away from real, heartfelt enjoyment蓋過真正的欣賞 of the traditional craftsmanship on display, prompting a modest but growing debate about the tension between authentic cultural engagement and content creation.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: The desire for social validation drives users to seek out visually striking, easily photographable scenes likely to attract likes and comments.
  • Social media influence: Influencers and content creators amplify awareness of photogenic locations rapidly, prompting a surge of imitative visits within days of the first viral post.
  • Consumer culture: Blending nostalgic local food imagery with festive tradition creates content that feels both novel and relatable, encouraging widespread sharing among Hong Kong youth.
