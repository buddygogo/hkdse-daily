Hong Kong official defends U-turn on public flats at Pak Shek Kok rail stop site

SCMP ·2 October 2026

The Development Bureau has come under renewed scrutiny after confirming a last-minute U-turna reversal of an earlier decision or policy政策上的大轉變 on plans for the new Pak Shek Kok railway station, with public housing quietly dropped from the site in favour of private flats. Permanent Secretary for Development Doris Ho Pui-ling told reporters on Friday that the station, part of the East Rail Line extension works and earmarkedset aside for a particular purpose指定用途的；預留的 to open as early as 2033, would instead be financed by allowing the project's developer to build and sell private housing on the surrounding land.

It is widely argued that such reversals undermine public trust in long-term planning, yet officials insisted the decision was pragmatic rather than political. Ho explained that the area around the proposed station currently lacks the requisiterequired; necessary for a particular purpose必需的；所需的 community facilities — schools, clinics and transport links — to support a sizeable public housing estate, and that building purely private flats would better align withto be consistent or in agreement with與……一致；配合 existing development in the neighbourhood. Nevertheless, lawmakers have pressed the bureau to explain why the original mixed-use blueprint, which had promised a share of subsidised flats, was not honoured.

By the same token, the government was keen to allayto reduce fear, worry or doubt減輕；緩解（憂慮） public anxiety over the city's overall housing supply. Officials stressed that the revised proposal would not jeopardiseto put something at risk of being harmed or destroyed危害；使陷入危險 the administration's ten-year target of delivering 420,000 flats, including 294,000 public units, by 2036, since sufficient land has reportedly already been securedobtained or guaranteed, especially through effort獲得；確保 elsewhere to meet that quota. Critics, however, contend that ad hocdone or arranged only when necessary, not planned in advance臨時的；特別安排的 adjustments like this one risk eroding confidence in the Northern Metropolis strategy as a whole, and have called for greater transparencyopenness and honesty in communicating decisions透明度；公開程度 in how such housing trade-offs are decided.

Widen mental health support for Hong Kong youth beyond high-risk cases, adviser urges

SCMP ·30 September 2026

Hong Kong needs to broadento make something wider in range or scope擴闊；擴大範圍 mental health support well beyond students already flagged as high-risk, a key government adviser has urged, warning that the city's current approach may be missing many young people in genuine distress. Dr Lam Ching-choi, a member of the Executive Council and chairman of the Advisory Committee on Mental Health, pointed out that some students who took their own lives had shown no obvious warning signsindications that something bad might happen警號；預警跡象 at home or in school.

It is widely argued that this pattern complicates prevention efforts considerably. According to Dr Lam, several of the affected teenagers came from stable, middle-class families, performed well academically, and were not known to have experienced bullying — meaning that schemes targetingaiming at or directed towards a particular group針對（某群體） only "high-risk" individuals may fail to reach them in time. He attributed part of the problem to social media, where young people can be exposed to harmful contentmaterial that causes damage, especially psychologically有害內容 and online bullying largely invisible to parents and teachers.

Nevertheless, there are signs of a wider policy shift. Dr Lam called for support measures to be extended to the general student populationall students as a whole, not just a specific subgroup全體學生 rather than narrowly targeted groups, and separately, a prominent local tycoon has pledged funding to empower more young philanthropists to tackle the issue. By the same token, mental health experts caution that any expanded scheme must be adequately resourced, or risk becoming merely symbolicrepresenting an idea rather than having real, practical effect象徵性的；流於表面的 rather than genuinely protective. Critics contend that without sustained investment in school counselling and community outreach, Hong Kong's youth mental health crisis will continue to worsen.

Hong Kong graduates at a crossroads as AI takes over entry-level jobs, experts warn

SCMP ·30 September 2026

Hong Kong's fresh graduates are facing an increasingly uncertain labour marketthe overall system of supply and demand for workers勞動市場, as artificial intelligence steadily absorbs the routine tasks that once served as a stepping stonean action or event that helps achieve a bigger goal踏腳石；過渡階段 into full-time careers. According to labour market experts, the number of full-time vacancies suitable for university graduates has plummetedto fall suddenly and sharply急劍下跌 from roughly 80,000 in 2022 to just 31,000 last year, a decline widely attributed to the rapid adoption of AI tools in offices across the city.

It is widely argued that administrative support, clerical duties and other entry-levelrelating to a job requiring little or no prior experience初級；入門級的 roles — historically the easiest point of entry for young job-seekers — are being systematically replaced by automation. One expert warned starkly that "it is no longer the case that a young person willing to learn and work hard will definitely find a job," underscoring how traditional assumptions about meritocracythe belief that success is earned through ability and effort精英制度；論功行賞的制度 in the workplace are being tested. A member of the Labour Advisory Board further cautioned that employers' growing emphasis on immediate productivity has made many reluctant to invest in on-the-job trainingskills training given to employees while they work在職培訓 for inexperienced hires.

Nevertheless, not every voice is pessimistic. By the same token that automation threatens certain roles, it is also reshaping demand towards new skill sets: a global survey found that employers increasingly prize critical thinking, data analysis and ethical decision-makingmaking choices guided by moral principles合乎道德的決策 over routine competence. Critics contend, however, that without targeted retraining schemes and government support, a persistent shortagea continuing lack of something over a long period持續的短缺 of experienced local talent could emerge, even as entry-level opportunities continue to disappear.

Hang Lung CEO Weber Lo steps down on October 1, hands over to successor

The Standard ·1 October 2026

Hang Lung Group, one of Hong Kong's most prominent property developers, has confirmed a significant leadership transitiona change from one state or stage to another過渡；轉變 at the top of the company. Weber Lo officially stepped down as Chief Executive Officer and executive director on 1 October, handing the role to Leo Tsoi Tak-lun, a veteran of more than three decades in retail and business management across Asia, who had joined the firm the previous month as CEO-designate to ensure a smooth handoverthe act of transferring control or responsibility交接；交班.

It is widely argued that such carefully staged successions reflect a broader trend among Hong Kong's family-controlled conglomerates, which have faced mounting pressure to future-proofto make something able to withstand future developments or needs為未來做好準備；具前瞻性 their leadership structures amid a challenging property market. Lo, who had led the group through a lengthy period of retail headwindsdifficult conditions that hinder progress in the retail sector零售業的不利因素 and shifting consumer habits, will remain with the company for a further year as Advisor to the Chair, lending continuity even as day-to-day decisions pass to his successor.

Nevertheless, analysts caution that leadership change alone will not resolve the structural challenges facing Hong Kong's real estate sector, including elevated interest rates and subdued mainland tourist spending. By the same token, some investors view Tsoi's extensive retail background as a potentially favourableadvantageous or beneficial有利的 sign, given the group's heavy reliance on shopping mall revenue. Critics contend that genuine recovery will depend less on who occupies the corner office than on whether the broader economy can regain momentumto start moving forward successfully again重新獲得動力；復甦.

🧵 Why Threads is quietly taking over Hong Kong teens' feeds

Social Trend

Meta's text-based platform Threads has quietly become one of the fastest-growing social apps among Hong Kong youth, with awareness among local users climbing from 36 percent shortly after its 2023 launch to 66 percent more recently, while active usage has roughly tripled over the same period. The 18-to-34 age group is driving most of this growth, gravitating towardsbeing naturally attracted or drawn towards something自然地傾向於 the platform's fast-moving, text-first format for real-time commentary on everything from celebrity gossip to breaking local news.

Much of the appeal lies in Threads' seamless integrationthe combination of two things so they work as one整合；融合 with Instagram, which most Hongkongers already use — joining Threads requires little more than a single tap, instantly carrying over an existing network of followers. This low barrier to entry, combined with video content increasingly permeatingspreading through and becoming a noticeable part of something滲透；瀰漫 the platform, has made it an attractive alternative to more saturated apps.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Social media influence: Seamless cross-posting from Instagram, which almost all young Hongkongers already use, lowers the barrier to joining and lets networks transfer instantly.
  • Psychological appeal: The fast, text-first, real-time format satisfies a desire for quick social validation and immediate participation in trending conversations.
  • Consumer culture: Rising use of short video content on the platform mirrors broader youth demand for low-effort, highly shareable entertainment.
