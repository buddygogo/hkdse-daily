Hong Kong Schools Pilot AI Tutoring Systems as Education Bureau Unveils New Digital Literacy Framework

RTHK · 2 September 2026

The Hong Kong Education Bureau has unveiled a landmarkhistoric; marking an important milestone具里程碑意義的 digital literacy framework that will see artificial intelligence tutoring systems introduced across secondary schools citywide by the end of 2027. The initiative, announced on Tuesday, marks one of the most far-reachinghaving wide and significant effects影響深遠的 reforms to classroom technology in Hong Kong's recent history, affecting an estimated 450,000 students.

It is widely argued that personalised learningtailoring education to individual student needs and pace個人化學習 represents the future of education. The AI systems, which adapt in real time to each student's learning pace and weaknesses, are intended to alleviate pressurereduce stress or burden減輕壓力 on teachers while providing more targeted academic support. Pilot schools have reported a notable improvement in student engagement, particularly among those who previously struggled to keep pace in conventional classroom settings.

Critics contend, however, that an uncritical reliancedepending on something without questioning its limitations不加批判的依賴 on algorithmic instruction risks undermining the irreplaceable human dimension of teaching. Education scholars have cautioned that empathy, mentorship, and the cultivation of moral valuesthe process of developing ethical principles培育道德價值觀 cannot be replicated by a machine. By the same token, concerns have been raised over digital equityequal access to technology across different socioeconomic groups數碼公平, as families in lower-income districts may lack the stable internet connectivity required to make full use of the new tools.

One cannot deny that the integration of AI into education reflects a broader global trend, yet Hong Kong's implementation will serve as a benchmarka standard against which others are measured基準；標杆 for the wider Asia-Pacific region. Whether the initiative ultimately narrows or widens the academic achievement gap remains, at this stage, an open question that only time and rigorous evaluation will answer.

Hong Kong Records Hottest August in 145 Years as Government Pledges Accelerated Climate Action

South China Morning Post ·2 September 2026

The Hong Kong Observatory confirmed on Monday that this August was the hottest on record since measurements began in 1884, with mean temperatures averaging 30.4°C — a figure that has sparked alarmcaused widespread fear or concern引發警覺 among climate scientists and public health experts alike. The city recorded 28 very hot weather days during the month, surpassing the previous record of 21 set in 2023.

In response, the government has pledged to acceleratemade a firm promise to speed up承諾加快 its transition to renewable energy, with authorities announcing a target to source 15% of electricity from solar panels installed on public buildings by 2030. Nevertheless, environmentalists argue that such measures remain woefully insufficientseverely lacking; far below what is needed嚴重不足 given the scale of the crisis.

It is worth noting that extreme heat disproportionately affects the elderly and those living in subdivided flats without air conditioning, raising urgent questions about climate justicefair distribution of climate impacts and resources across social groups氣候公義 in one of the world's most unequal cities.

The iconic Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, a century-old traditiona custom practised for over a hundred years百年傳統 that has been inscribed on China's National Intangible Cultural Heritage List, drew more than 80,000 spectators to the streets of Causeway Bay over three evenings this week. The event, widely regarded as a symbol of community solidaritythe sense of unity and shared purpose within a group社區凝聚力, saw over 300 performers carry a 67-metre dragon studded with more than 70,000 incense sticks through the neighbourhood's narrow lanes.
