A seemingly trivial gripe about handheld fans has snowballed into one of Threads' most heated debates this week, after Hong Kong commuters began swapping stories of being trapped beside strangers blasting piercingsharp, high-pitched and uncomfortable to hear刺耳的 fan noise on packed buses and trains. One widely shared post, in which a passenger was filmed confronting a fan-wielding commuter, racked up tens of thousands of views and ignited a sprawling thread of agreement and backlash. 中文翻譯

一宗看似微不足道、關於手提風扇的小爭拗，本星期在Threads演變成最熾熱的話題之一，事緣有香港乘客開始分享自己被困在擠迫巴士及列車上，被陌生人開著的刺耳風扇聲音「夾擊」的經歷。其中一則廣傳帖文，拍到一名乘客與開著風扇的乘客當場理論，瀏覽量高達數萬，並掀起一連串支持與反對的龐大討論。有網民要求就密閉公共空間的風扇噪音訂立分貝上限的法例，亦有人反駁批評者是在一個本已擁擠不堪的城市裡，過度干預他人的基本生活舒適。

DSE writing angles
  • Noise etiquette on crowded public transport: As Hong Kong's MTR carriages and buses grow ever more crowded, even a small personal device can intrude on dozens of strangers at once. The portable fan row reveals how shared, confined spaces amplify minor irritations into full-blown disputes, testing whether commuters can agree on unwritten rules of consideration when private comfort collides with communal space.
  • Calls to legislate against handheld fan noise: Frustrated commuters have urged lawmakers to set decibel limits on portable fans, much as noise ordinances already govern construction and nightlife. Critics counter that legislating against such a minor, everyday object risks trivialising the Legislative Council's time and raises thorny questions about how authorities would even enforce such a rule on a moving bus.
  • Physiological need versus shared-space comfort: Defenders of noisy fan users argue that the backlash overlooks genuine physical discomfort: outdoor labourers, the elderly and those prone to overheating may rely on a fan as a basic necessity rather than a luxury. This counter-view challenges the assumption that quietness should always trump a person's immediate bodily comfort in public.
  • Judging strangers by the sound of their habits: Underlying the dispute is an unspoken judgment about who gets to feel entitled to comfort in public: critics note that fan users are often assumed to be inconsiderate without regard for their circumstances, exposing how quickly online strangers leap to class-tinged assumptions about manners, upbringing and who truly belongs in a civilised public space.
