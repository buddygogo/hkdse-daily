Hong Kong Stages First International Lantern Spectacular for Mid-Autumn Festival

The Standard ·11 September 2026

Hong Kong is preparing to welcome the Mid-Autumn Festival with a citywide celebrationan event that takes place across the whole city全市性的慶祝活動 on an unprecedented scale. The Hong Kong Tourism Board will stage its first-ever International Lantern Spectacular at Victoria Park from 17 to 27 September, running alongside the long-running Mid-Autumn Lantern Carnival. Centred on the theme of "Reunion," the event brings together around 3,000 specialty lanternslanterns made with distinctive or unique craftsmanship特色花燈 from 20 countries and regions, including four giant lanterns crafted by a team of Qinhuai lantern makers invited especially from Nanjing. A World Lantern Street will showcase these international pieces, while a dedicated Hong Kong lantern court will display more than 400 traditional hand-made lanterns. Lighting hours will run from 6:30pm to 11pm daily, extending to midnight on Mid-Autumn Day itself, 25 September, with free admission throughout.

It is widely argued that the event does more than simply entertain. Running in tandem with the 147th edition of the Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, an intangible cultural heritagetraditions and skills passed down that are not physical objects非物質文化遺產 item recognised on the national list, the festival period illustrates how the city is attempting to safeguard age-old customstraditions practised for a very long time古老習俗 while simultaneously projecting an image of international openness. By the same token, inviting overseas artisans to contribute to a distinctly local festival signals an ambition to position Hong Kong as a cultural crossroadsa place where different traditions and influences meet文化交匯點 rather than a city defined by a single heritage alone.

Nevertheless, critics contend that such large-scale, internationally flavoured festivities carry a risk of diluting authenticityweakening the genuine or original character of something削弱真確性, turning a solemn family occasion into a tourist spectaclean event staged mainly to attract and impress visitors吸引遊客的盛事. Supporters counter that exposing younger generations to traditional lantern-making alongside global art forms may in fact rekindle interestrenew or revive enthusiasm for something重燃興趣 in heritage that might otherwise fade. Whether this fusion approach represents a sustainable model for cultural preservation or merely a one-off commercial success remains to be seen, but it undeniably sets a notable precedentan earlier example used as a guide for future action先例 for how Hong Kong festivals might evolve in years to come.

Paul Chan Unveils HK$50 Million AI Upskilling Drive for Hong Kong Workers

RTHK ·23 August 2026

The Hong Kong government has unveiled a major upskilling drivean organised effort to teach people new, higher-level skills提升技能行動 aimed at preparing the city's workforce for the growing role of artificial intelligence. Financial Secretary Paul Chan announced that professional AI courses would roll out from November, forming part of a broader HK$50 million initiative targeting 50,000 workers through some 200 training activities over two years. The scheme is designed to reach employees across a wide range of sectors, not merely those already working in technology, reflecting official recognition that AI is set to permeatespread through and become a noticeable feature of something滲透 almost every corner of the economy.

It is widely argued that such a programme is both timely and necessary. Hong Kong has been ranked among the world's leading economies in AI readiness, trailing only New York and London on one recent global index, and the city's computing power is projected to expand dramatically by 2032. Proponents maintain that without a workforce capable of applying these tools effectively, such infrastructure investment risks going to waste. Critics contend that the funding, while welcome, remains modest in scalesmall when compared with what is actually needed規模有限 relative to the scale of disruption AI is expected to bring, and that many workers in vulnerable sectorsindustries where jobs are more likely to be lost or changed易受影響的行業 may still be left behind.

By the same token, education experts point out that upskilling alone cannot substitute for deeper curriculum reform, particularly for young people entering the job market. There is growing consensus that AI literacy should be treated as a foundational competencya basic skill that underlies many other abilities基礎能力 comparable to digital literacy a decade ago, rather than an optional add-on. Whether this latest initiative can be scaled up quickly enough to keep pace with rapid technological change remains an open question, but it nevertheless marks a significant step in the government's effort to ensure the workforce is not caught off guardunprepared for something that happens措手不及 by the AI transition.

HKU Makes AI Literacy Compulsory as New Students Begin 2026-27 Academic Year

HKU Press Office ·26 August 2026

The University of Hong Kong welcomed its new cohort of undergraduates at an inauguration ceremony held under the theme "Excellence with Purpose: Shaping Tomorrow's Leaders Today." What sets this academic year apart, however, is a curricular change that reaches far beyond a single ceremony: HKU has become the first university in the city to make AI literacy a compulsory componenta required part that cannot be avoided必修部分 of its undergraduate studies. All students admitted from the 2025-26 intake onwards are now required to complete a foundational course in artificial intelligence literacy, covering everything from the workings of large language models to the ethical dilemmas such technology raises.

It is widely argued that this move reflects a broader shift in how universities view their core mission. Rather than treating AI as a specialist subject reserved for computing students, the course is designed to be multidisciplinaryinvolving more than one academic subject or field跨學科的, drawing in perspectives from law, the arts and social sciences. Supporters maintain that graduates entering almost any profession will need at least a working understanding of AI's capabilities and limitations, much as computer literacy became indispensableso important that it cannot be done without不可或缺的 a generation ago. Nevertheless, some educators caution that a single mandatory course cannot, by itself, guarantee genuine critical engagement with the technology.

Critics contend that there is a danger of such initiatives becoming tokenisticdone only for appearance's sake rather than to have real effect流於形式的 unless they are backed by sustained investment in teaching capacity and updated regularly as the technology evolves. By the same token, others point out that requiring every student to grapple with AI's ethical implications, not merely its technical mechanics, could help cultivate a more discerning generationa group of people able to judge and evaluate things wisely有辨別能力的一代 of graduates. As other local universities weigh whether to follow suit, HKU's experience over the coming year is likely to be closely watched as a test case for AI education policy across Hong Kong's tertiary sector.

Hong Kong Logs Hottest Day on Record as Typhoon Dolphin Brings Extreme Heat

Hong Kong Free Press ·9 August 2026

Hong Kong recorded its hottest day on recordthe highest temperature ever measured since records began有紀錄以來最熱的一天 this summer, as the Observatory headquarters registered 36.9 degrees Celsius while Typhoon Dolphin swept warm air across the region. The reading, the highest since records began in 1884, was eclipsed in some districts: Sheung Shui in the New Territories logged 39.8 degrees, the highest figure recorded since automatic weather stations were introduced in the 1980s. The extreme heat forced many residents to seek refuge in shopping malls and even artificial snow parks, while outdoor workers and domestic helpers were reported to have struggled significantly under the scorching conditionsextremely hot weather conditions酷熱的天氣.

It is widely argued that such records are no longer isolated anomalies but part of a discernible patterna pattern that can be clearly noticed or recognised明顯的規律 of intensifying heat linked to climate change. Meteorologists have noted that Hong Kong is projected to face several typhoons annually in the coming years, and that rising sea and air temperatures are likely to make such record-breaking days increasingly common rather than exceptional. Critics contend that current public health measures, including heat warnings and outdoor work guidelines, have not kept pace with the frequency and severity of these events, leaving vulnerable groups disproportionately exposedaffected to an unfairly large degree compared with others承受不成比例的影響 to heat-related risks.

By the same token, environmental groups argue that record heat should serve as a wake-up callan event that makes people realise a serious problem exists警號 for accelerating the city's transition towards carbon neutrality, rather than being treated merely as a short-term inconvenience. Nevertheless, sceptics point out that individual weather records, however striking, cannot alone justify sweeping policy change without sustained long-term data. What seems increasingly clear is that as extreme heat becomes a more regular feature of Hong Kong summers, pressure will continue to mount on policymakers to reassess how the city prepares its infrastructure, workforce and public health systems for a warmer future.

🍼 "Jayden ah, mami already told you..." — Jayden-mania Still Going Strong

Social Trend

A meme known as "Jayden-mania" has been sweeping the internetspreading extremely quickly across online platforms席捲網絡 in Hong Kong since it first emerged from a video posted by two local influencers earlier this year. The clip parodies a distinctive, clunkyawkward and unnatural in style笨拙的 style of Cantonese-English code-switching often associated with certain parenting habits, built around the now-famous line "Jayden ah, mami already told you..." The phrase has since been remixed endlesslyreused and altered repeatedly in many different versions被無限次改編 across social media, spawning parody videos, merchandise and marketing campaigns that borrow its instantly recognisable rhythm.

While largely intended as light-hearted mockery, the trend has also triggered a more serious debate. Some commentators worry about its effect on children who happen to share the name Jayden, who may now face unwanted teasing, while others raise concerns that the meme's stilted, code-mixed phrasing could subtly shape how younger Hongkongers use English and Cantonese in everyday speech. Marketers, meanwhile, have been quick to capitalise on its popularity, weaving the catchphrase into advertising aimed at younger, digitally native audiences.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: The exaggerated, instantly repeatable catchphrase is easy to imitate and remix, giving audiences a low-effort way to participate and feel part of an in-joke.
  • Social media influence: Influencer-driven origin content and rapid reposting across Threads, Instagram and TikTok allowed the meme to spread far beyond its original audience within weeks.
  • Consumer culture: Brands seeking to reach younger, digitally fluent consumers have adopted the catchphrase in marketing, reinforcing and prolonging its virality.
