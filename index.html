Hong Kong Unveils First Five-Year Plan to Almost Double Spending on AI and Innovation

The Standard ·17 September 2026

It is widely reported that the Hong Kong government has unveiled its first-ever five-year plan for innovation and technology, announced alongside the 2026 Policy Address. Under the blueprint, the city aims to almost double its domestic expenditure on innovation activitiestotal spending across the economy on research, development and new technology本地創新活動總開支, targeting three per cent of GDP after 2030, up from just 1.63 per cent in 2024. Critics contend that ambitious spending pledges mean little without a coherent strategy for translating research into a genuinely commercially viable industry basea group of companies capable of profitably selling their products具商業效益的產業基礎.

Nevertheless, the scale of the plan is difficult to overstate. Officials have proposed three new innovation parks and five research institutions, alongside a dedicated Northern Metropolis hub intended to clusterto group similar things closely together in one place群聚 artificial intelligence, robotics and health-technology firms. By the same token, the government has pledged to train 40,000 workers through an eighteen-month AI upskilling campaign, reflecting growing recognition that technological transformationa fundamental change brought about by new technology科技轉型 cannot succeed without a correspondingly skilled workforce.

It is worth noting that the plan also addresses the risks accompanying rapid AI adoption. New regulatory proposals will govern deepfakes, employment impacts and broader AI governancethe systems and rules used to manage and oversee the use of artificial intelligence人工智能治理, an acknowledgement that unchecked technological expansiongrowth in technology that proceeds without sufficient oversight or limits不受制約的科技擴張 carries genuine social costs. One cannot deny, however, that the HK$10 billion government injection, expected to catalyse a further HK$40 billion in market contributions, signals a serious attempt to close the gap with regional rivals such as Shenzhen and Singapore.

Ultimately, whether this ambitious blueprinta bold and far-reaching plan for the future宏大的藍圖 delivers lasting benefits will depend on sustained implementation rather than the scale of the initial announcement alone. For students, the plan is a timely reminder that future employabilitythe likelihood of being able to find and keep a job in years to come未來的就業能力 increasingly hinges on adaptability to an AI-driven economy.

Hong Kong to Offer 'Attractive' Merger Deals for Schools Beyond Those Already Struggling

South China Morning Post ·16 September 2026

It is widely acknowledged that Hong Kong's shrinking student population has placed considerable strain on the city's smaller secondary and primary schools. In response, the Education Bureau has announced that it will offer more attractive merger incentivesappealing benefits designed to encourage two organisations to combine into one具吸引力的合併誘因 to schools facing enrolment difficulties, extending support beyond institutions already on the brink of closure under the "Confluence and Elevation" scheme.

Critics contend that the policy, while pragmatic, risks eroding the distinctive identitythe unique character and traditions that set an institution apart from others獨特身份 of long-established schools, some of which have served their communities for decades. Parents and alumni groups have expressed concern that mergers, however well-intentioned, may disrupt continuity for students partway through their secondary education. Nevertheless, officials argue that consolidationthe process of combining several smaller units into a stronger, more efficient whole整合 is preferable to the alternative of forced closures, which would leave students with even less certainty about their futures.

By the same token, the government has paired the merger incentives with broader measures aimed at reversing the city's declining birth rate, including new housing support for young families. It is worth noting that this dual approach reflects an understanding that the demographic pressuresdifficulties arising from changes in a population's size or age structure人口壓力 facing schools cannot be solved through education policy alone.

One cannot deny that the coming years will test whether these policy interventionsdeliberate government actions intended to influence a particular outcome政策介入 can genuinely stabilise the sector, or whether Hong Kong's schools will continue to face mounting enrolment shortfallsa situation in which the number of students falls below what is needed or expected收生不足 regardless of the incentives on offer.

Greenpeace Criticises Lack of Wetland Protection in Northern Metropolis Development Plan

Hong Kong Free Press ·17 September 2026

It is widely argued that Hong Kong's environmental commitments have failed to keep pace with the scale of its development ambitions. Following this year's Policy Address, Greenpeace criticised the government's Northern Metropolis blueprint for omitting binding wetland protection targetslegally enforceable goals for safeguarding wetland habitats具約束力的濕地保護目標, arguing that the plan falls short of standards already adopted across the border.

Critics contend that the omission is particularly striking given that Shenzhen, Hong Kong's immediate neighbour, has committed to protecting fifty per cent of its wetlands by 2030 under its latest five-year plan. By the same token, environmental groups warn that the wetlands bordering the Northern Metropolis provide crucial ecological servicesthe natural benefits that ecosystems provide to humans, such as clean water or flood control生態服務, including habitats for migratory birds and natural flood mitigation, both of which are placed at risk by large-scale development.

Nevertheless, government officials maintain that the development blueprint incorporates adequate environmental safeguardsmeasures put in place to prevent or limit damage to the natural environment環境保障措施, and that conservation will be balanced against the pressing need for housing and economic growth. It is worth noting, however, that similar assurances have accompanied previous large-scale developments, only for conservationists to later report irreversible habitat losspermanent damage to natural environments that cannot be restored無法逆轉的棲息地喪失.

One cannot deny that the tension between development and conservation will only intensify as construction proceeds. Whether Hong Kong ultimately adopts more stringent conservation benchmarksstrict, clearly defined standards for protecting the natural environment嚴格的保育基準 comparable to those of its neighbours remains to be seen, but the debate underscores the difficulty of reconciling rapid urban expansion with long-term ecological responsibility.

Inaugural International Lantern Spectacular Opens at Victoria Park for Mid-Autumn Festival

Hong Kong Free Press ·15 September 2026

It is widely celebrated that Hong Kong has this year staged its first-ever International Lantern Spectacular at Victoria Park, marking a significant expansion of the city's Mid-Autumn festivitiesa considerable increase in the scale and scope of the city's Mid-Autumn Festival celebrations中秋節慶祝活動的擴展. Organised by the Hong Kong Tourism Board and running from 17 to 27 September, the event brings together lanterns from twenty countries and regions under the theme of "reunion".

By the same token, the festival highlights Hong Kong's enduring role as a meeting point of cultural traditionscustoms, beliefs and practices passed down within a society文化傳統 both local and international. Alongside giant master-crafted lanterns imported from Nanjing, visitors can view a dedicated Hong Kong Lantern Galleria featuring over four hundred traditional handmade lanterns, as well as playful designs shaped like local dim sum and street food, offering a distinctly Hong Kong interpretation of a centuries-old festival.

Nevertheless, some cultural commentators caution that heavily tourism-oriented events risk diluting the festival's original significanceweakening or reducing the depth and authenticity of a tradition's true meaning沖淡節日原有的意義, transforming a family-centred occasion into a spectacle designed primarily for visitors. It is worth noting, however, that the nearby Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, now in its 147th edition and recognised as national intangible cultural heritage, continues to be led by local residents, suggesting that grassroots tradition and large-scale tourism promotion can coexist.

One cannot deny that events of this scale carry considerable value in reinforcing civic identitystrengthening a shared sense of belonging and pride among a city's residents強化市民身份認同, particularly for younger residents who may otherwise engage with traditional festivals only superficially. Whether such initiatives successfully balance commercial appeal with authentic heritage preservation remains an open question worth close observation.

✨ The "Gold Star Behaviour" Trend Sweeps Hong Kong Youth

Social Trend

A new wave of short videos under the banner of "Gold Star Behaviour"a social media trend in which creators praise themselves for small, often mundane, acts of self-discipline「金星行為」 has become popular among Hong Kong teenagers on TikTok and Instagram. Creators film themselves completing ordinary tasks, such as replying to a message promptly or drinking enough water, and reward themselves with an exaggerated sense of celebration, often set to upbeat audio clips.

It is widely argued that the trend reflects a broader shift towards normalising small winstreating minor personal achievements as worthy of genuine recognition將微小成就視為正常 at a time when many young people report high levels of academic and social pressure. Nevertheless, critics contend that framing basic responsibilities as noteworthy achievements may, paradoxically, reveal just how emotionally depletedlacking in the emotional energy or resilience needed to cope with daily demands情緒耗竭 many teenagers currently feel.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: Publicly celebrating minor accomplishments provides a low-stakes dopamine reward, offering encouragement to viewers who may otherwise feel overwhelmed by everyday demands.
  • Social media influence: Short-form video platforms reward relatable, low-production content, allowing ordinary daily routines to spread rapidly among peer networks.
  • Mental health awareness: The trend coincides with growing openness among young people about stress and burnout, framing self-care as something worth publicly acknowledging.
  • Consumer culture: Brands have begun co-opting the trend to market wellness products, illustrating how youth culture is quickly absorbed into commercial marketing strategies.
  • Sociological dimension: The popularity of the trend suggests a generational reaction against relentless productivity culture, replacing achievement-based validation with gentler, self-compassionate framing.
