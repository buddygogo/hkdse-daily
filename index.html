Hong Kong Leader Says Firms Must Strive to Survive Despite Government Support

South China Morning Post ·19 September 2026

It is widely reported that Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu has defended the economic measures set out in his 2026 Policy Address, insisting that private enterprisesbusinesses owned and operated by individuals or companies rather than the government私營企業 must still strive to remain competitive even as the government extends fresh support. Speaking on Saturday, Mr Lee stressed that the livelihood section of this year's address was the broadest in scope to date, forming part of the city's inaugural five-year development plana government blueprint setting economic and social goals over a five-year period五年發展規劃.

Critics contend that repeated rounds of government assistance risk fostering a culture of dependency on state interventiona reliance on government action rather than independent effort依賴政府干預, particularly among small and medium enterprises still recovering from years of subdued consumer spendingweaker than usual spending by households on goods and services疲弱的消費支出. Nevertheless, officials argue that targeted relief remains necessary to prevent further business closures while the broader economy adjusts to structural change.

By the same token, Mr Lee emphasised that businesses must first help the economy grow before the government can expand support for livelihoods, a formulation suggesting that welfare spending will remain contingent on continued economic growthan increase in the production and consumption of goods and services within an economy經濟增長 rather than expanding indefinitely. It is worth noting that this framing places considerable pressure on firms to demonstrate market-driven resiliencethe ability of businesses to adapt and recover through their own commercial strategies rather than external aid市場主導的韌性 rather than treating government support as a permanent safety net.

One cannot deny that the tension between offering meaningful assistance and avoiding an over-reliance on public funds will remain a defining feature of Hong Kong's economic policy in the years ahead. For students, the debate offers a useful illustration of how governments must constantly rebalance short-term relief against long-term fiscal sustainabilitythe capacity of a government to maintain its spending levels without risking future financial instability財政可持續性.

Foreign Carers for Elderly to Get 10 to 20 Percent More Pay Than Current Helpers

South China Morning Post ·18 September 2026

It is widely reported that foreign carers recruited under a new government pilot scheme for elderly care will be paid ten to twenty per cent more than existing domestic helpers, according to Secretary for Labour and Welfare Chris Sun. The initiative is intended to address Hong Kong's growing ageing populationa demographic trend in which the proportion of elderly people within a society steadily increases人口老化 by attracting carers with specialised training rather than relying solely on the existing domestic helper scheme.

Critics contend that the pay differential could create friction within an already strained care sector, where existing helpers frequently perform similar duties without comparable recognition or wage paritythe state of receiving equal pay for work of equal value薪酬平等. Nevertheless, officials argue that requiring applicants to hold at least three years of relevant experience justifies the higher pay, since it reflects genuine specialised expertiseadvanced skills and knowledge developed through focused training in a particular field專業技能 rather than a general willingness to perform household duties.

By the same token, the scheme forms part of a broader package announced in this year's Policy Address to reinforce support for family carers, including an expanded Living Allowance Scheme for low-income households caring for elderly relatives. It is worth noting that such measures reflect growing recognition that informal caregivingunpaid care provided by family members rather than professional institutions非正式照顧 places a considerable and often invisible burden on working families.

One cannot deny that Hong Kong's care economy faces mounting strain as its population ages and its workforce shrinks. Whether higher wages alone can attract sufficient carers, or whether deeper structural reform of the sector is required, remains an important question for policymakers and families alike.

Hospital Authority Calls for Early Flu Vaccination Amid Infection Rise

RTHK ·15 September 2026

It is widely reported that Hong Kong's Hospital Authority has urged residents to seek early influenza vaccination as seasonal flu infections continue to rise across the city. Health officials describe current viral activitythe level of a virus's spread and transmission within a population病毒活躍程度 as remaining high, with hospitals reporting an increase in serious cases requiring admission.

Critics contend that public awareness campaigns alone may be insufficient to reach vulnerable groups, particularly the elderly and those with chronic illnesses who face the greatest risk of severe complications. Nevertheless, the Hospital Authority has procured around 1.3 million vaccine doses this season, offering inactivated, recombinant and nasal spray formulationsdifferent types of vaccine preparations suited to varying medical needs and age groups滅活、基因重組及噴鼻劑型疫苗 to accommodate different patient needs.

By the same token, doctors have emphasised that the body requires roughly two weeks after vaccination to build sufficient immune protectionthe body's ability to resist and fight off a particular infection免疫保護, meaning delays in getting vaccinated could leave individuals exposed during the peak infection period. It is worth noting that immunity from the vaccine is expected to last at least six months, covering much of the traditional winter flu season.

One cannot deny that persuading the public to act before symptoms appear remains one of the most persistent challenges in preventive healthcaremedical measures taken to prevent illness before it occurs, rather than treating it afterwards預防性醫療. The current campaign illustrates the recurring tension between individual complacency and the collective benefits of timely public health action.

BABYMONSTER Draws Over 1,000 Fans at Tsim Sha Tsui Appearance

The Standard ·13 September 2026

It is widely reported that the K-pop girl group BABYMONSTER drew more than 1,000 fans to a fashion brand appearance at 1881 Heritage in Tsim Sha Tsui, with dedicated fans reportedly beginning to queue outside the venue the night before. The event underscores the enduring commercial drawing powerthe ability of a celebrity or group to attract large crowds and generate business interest商業號召力 of K-pop acts within Hong Kong's entertainment scene.

Critics contend that such intense fan mobilisationthe organised gathering and coordinated activity of a celebrity's supporters粉絲動員 raises concerns about crowd safety and public order, particularly when large numbers of young fans camp outside venues overnight. Nevertheless, organisers noted that designated fan areas and wristband distribution helped manage the crowd in an orderly fashion.

By the same token, the appearance illustrates how brands increasingly rely on celebrity-driven marketinga promotional strategy that uses the popularity of a well-known figure to attract customers名人營銷 to generate publicity, treating fan enthusiasm as a measurable commercial asset rather than a purely cultural phenomenon. It is worth noting that Hong Kong continues to serve as a regional hub for such events, given its transport links and established fan infrastructure.

One cannot deny that K-pop's continued popularity among Hong Kong youth reflects broader patterns of cross-border cultural consumptionthe practice of engaging with entertainment, fashion, or media produced in other countries or regions跨地域文化消費, in which local audiences increasingly participate in global entertainment fandoms rather than confining their interests to homegrown celebrities alone.

🧸 The Blind Box Collecting Craze Continues to Grip Hong Kong Youth

Social Trend

Blind-box collectibles such as Pop Mart's Labubu, designed by Hong Kong artist Kasing Lung, remain one of the most persistent consumer crazeswaves of intense and often short-lived enthusiasm for a particular product消費熱潮 among Hong Kong youth on TikTok and Instagram. Videos of fans unboxing mystery packaging to reveal a random figure regularly attract millions of views, with the anticipation itself forming a central part of the appeal.

It is widely argued that the trend endures because randomised rewardsoutcomes determined by chance rather than guaranteed in advance隨機獎勵 create a compelling psychological pull, keeping collectors engaged even after the novelty of the character itself might otherwise fade. Nevertheless, critics warn that the format closely resembles gambling mechanics, encouraging repeated purchases in pursuit of rare variants.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: The unpredictable outcome of each blind box activates the brain's dopamine-driven reward system, reinforcing repeat purchasing behaviour similar to other chance-based activities.
  • Social media influence: Unboxing videos are highly shareable and algorithm-friendly, allowing the trend to spread rapidly through short-form video platforms popular with Hong Kong teenagers.
  • Consumer culture: Limited-edition and rare variants create a sense of scarcity, encouraging collectors to make repeated purchases in pursuit of a complete set.
  • Cultural pride: The character's design by a Hong Kong-born artist has fostered a degree of local pride, adding a cultural dimension to what is otherwise a global commercial phenomenon.
