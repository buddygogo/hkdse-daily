Hong Kong Extends Late-Night Transport as Tai Hang Fire Dragon Lights Up Mid-Autumn Festival

RTHK ·25 September 2026

As Hong Kong marked Mid-Autumn Festival this week, transport operators extended late-night serviceskept public transport running for longer than usual hours延長夜間服務 across the MTR, trams and buses to accommodate crowds converging ongathering from different directions toward one place匯聚於 lantern displays and the city's most iconic festival tradition: the Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance. It is widely argued that this 147-year-old customa tradition practised continuously for 147 years擁有147年歷史的傳統, in which a 67-metre dragon studded with thousands of burning incense sticks is carried through the narrow lanes of Causeway Bay by roughly 300 performers, has become inseparable fromtightly linked to; impossible to separate from與……密不可分 the city's sense of identity during the festive season.

Critics contend that as the event grows in international profile — this year drawing an inaugural lantern spectacular featuring exhibits from twenty countries — organisers face a delicate balancing act between welcoming tourists and preserving the ritual's original community spirit. The dance was first performed in the 1880s to ward off plague, and it is now inscribed as National Intangible Cultural Heritage. Nevertheless, heritage experts caution that large-scale commercialisationturning a cultural event into a large commercial attraction大規模商業化 risks reducing a solemn communal rituala serious ceremony shared by the whole community莊嚴的社區儀式 to mere spectacle, stripping it of the meaning it once held for residents.

By the same token, many young Hongkongers appear to be rediscovering pride in such traditions rather than dismissing them as old-fashioned. It is often observed that festivals like this serve as a vehicle foract as a means of achieving or expressing something作為……的載體 passing cultural memory between generations, especially when local schools and community groups are actively involved in the preparations. Whether Hong Kong can continue to safeguardprotect from harm or loss保護；捍衛 such intangible heritage while accommodating ever-larger crowds remains a question worth close attention as the city's tourism ambitions continue to grow.

Hong Kong Kindergarten Teachers Face Unpaid Festival Overtime Amid Admission Crunch

The Standard ·25 September 2026

According to a recent report, kindergarten teachers across Hong Kong are increasingly required to work late into the night hosting festive galas and open days without any additional pay, as preschools compete fiercely to attract prospective pupils amid a deepening enrolment crisis. It is widely argued that this unpaid overtimeextra work hours for which no additional wages are paid無償超時工作 has quietly become an industry norm, driven largely by Hong Kong's plunging birth ratea rapidly and sharply falling number of births急劇下降的出生率, which has left many kindergartens scrambling to fill their classrooms amid an admission cruncha sudden, severe shortage of new student enrolments收生荒.

Teachers interviewed described single-handedly building elaborate decorations and moving heavy furniture with little janitorial support, often remaining on campus past nine or ten at night without overtime pay or compensatory leave. Critics contend that such practices amount to exploitation disguised as dedicationunfair treatment of workers presented instead as admirable commitment被包裝成敬業精神的剝削, placing an unsustainable burden on an already under-resourced professionan occupation lacking sufficient funding, staff or support資源不足的行業. Nevertheless, school operators maintain that hosting eye-catching promotional events has become an unavoidable necessity in a shrinking market where every enrolment counts.

By the same token, labour advocates argue that the sector's working conditions cannot be divorced from the wider demographic pressures reshaping Hong Kong's education landscape. It is often observed that when institutions are forced to compete for a shrinking pool ofstruggle against rivals over a decreasing number of爭奪日益減少的…… pupils, front-line staff frequently absorb the resulting pressure. Whether the Education Bureau will introduce statutory safeguardslegally required protections法定保障 for early-years educators, or leave the matter to market forces, remains to be seen — a question with real consequences for the wellbeing of those shaping Hong Kong's youngest learners.

Hong Kong to Pulp All Recovered Waste Paper Locally From October 1 in Recycling Overhaul

The Standard ·24 September 2026

Hong Kong will begin pulping all locally recovered waste paper at a newly upgraded facility in EcoPark, Tuen Mun, from October 1, marking a long-awaited shift away from the city's decades-long reliance on exporting recyclables overseas. It is widely argued that this move addresses a chronic vulnerabilitya long-standing weakness that persists over time長期存在的弱點 in Hong Kong's recycling system, which has repeatedly been disrupted whenever importing countries tightened restrictions on foreign waste.

Under the new arrangement, the range of paper accepted for recycling will be substantially broadenedincreased or expanded to a great extent大幅擴闊 beyond the traditional categories of cardboard, newspaper and office paper to include mixed items such as paper bags and packaging boxes, with annual processing capacity expected to rise from roughly 500,000 to 720,000 tonnes. Supporters contend that this represents a genuine step toward a closed-loop, waste-to-resources modela system in which waste materials are fully converted back into usable resources domestically廢物轉化為資源的閉環模式, rather than merely shifting the problem elsewhere. Nevertheless, environmentalists caution that local processing capacity alone cannot resolve Hong Kong's underlying over-reliance onexcessive dependence on過度依賴 single-use packagingpackaging materials designed to be thrown away after one use一次性包裝.

By the same token, some observers point out that the success of this initiative will ultimately hinge on public participation in sorting waste correctly at source. It is often observed that even the most sophisticated recycling infrastructureadvanced and technically well-developed facilities for recycling先進的回收基礎設施 cannot function effectively without consistent cooperation from households and businesses. As Hong Kong positions itself as a regional leader in sustainability, this facility stands as a test case forserves as an example that will show whether something works作為……的試金石 whether ambitious environmental policy can translate into everyday behavioural change among residents.

Hong Kong Seeks Deeper AI and Innovation Ties With Austria

OpenGov Asia ·24 September 2026

Hong Kong used the Applied Artificial Intelligence Conference 2026 in Vienna this week to set out its ambitions in artificial intelligence, meeting Austrian officials and industry representatives to discuss deeper collaboration in research and innovation. Secretary for Innovation, Technology and Industry Sun Dong told delegates that the city has elevated AI into a core industrymade artificial intelligence a central, priority sector of the economy將人工智能提升為核心產業, pursuing a dual strategy of developing "industries for AI" alongside "AI for industries".

It is widely argued that such international outreach carries real strategic weight for a city seeking to remain competitive in a crowded global AI raceintense worldwide competition among many countries developing AI競爭激烈的全球人工智能競賽. Discussions in Vienna reportedly touched on industrial digitalisationthe process of applying digital technology to transform traditional industries產業數碼化, start-up collaboration and joint research partnerships, with Hong Kong positioning itself as a bridge between Mainland Chinese and European innovation ecosystems. Nevertheless, sceptics caution that high-level diplomatic engagementofficial cooperation conducted at a senior government level高層外交參與 does not automatically translate into concrete technology transferthe passing of technical knowledge or equipment from one party to another技術轉移 or investment on the ground.

By the same token, some local commentators question whether the benefits of such global partnerships will be felt by ordinary students and workers, rather than remaining confined to policy circles and corporate boardrooms. It is often observed that Hong Kong's own AI ambitions can only be realised if they are underpinned bysupported at a fundamental level by以……為基礎 sustained investment in local talent, computing infrastructure and school-level digital literacy. Whether this latest round of overseas engagement will eventually filter down to the classroom and the workplace remains, for now, an open questiona matter not yet resolved or answered懸而未決的問題.

🎢 Hong Kong Disneyland's 90s 'Feel Like Dance' Challenge Goes Viral

Social Trend

A new short-form video trend has taken over Hong Kong social feeds this month: the "Feel Like Dance" challengea viral dance trend built around a specific song and set choreography「感覺像跳舞」挑戰, tied to Hong Kong Disneyland's 2026 season and styled with deliberately nostalgic 90s aestheticsvisual and stylistic references evoking the 1990s懷舊九十年代美學. Clips of families and friend groups performing the choreography inside and outside the park have racked up views across TikTok and Instagram, with creators competing to add their own playful twists.

The trend fits into a broader wave of nostalgia-driven contentsocial media posts that deliberately evoke fondness for the past懷舊導向的內容 currently popular among Hong Kong youth, who appear drawn to reliving childhood associations with the park rather than chasing entirely new experiences.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: Nostalgia triggers comforting childhood associations, giving participants a strong emotional connection to the content they create and share.
  • Social media influence: Short-form video platforms reward easily replicable choreography, allowing the trend to spread rapidly through duets and remixes.
  • Consumer culture: Tying the challenge to a theme park season blurs the line between organic fan content and branded marketing, encouraging repeat visits.
  • Cultural identity: Celebrating a shared, recognisably local experience lets Hong Kong youth express pride in home-grown entertainment rather than only following overseas fads.
