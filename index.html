🐀 Rat Pack Caught on Camera at Tai Kok Tsui Park Sparks Clean-Up Demand

Social Trend·24 Oct 2026
English

A short clip showing about ten rats scurrying around Cherry Street Park in Tai Kok Tsui went viral on local Facebook groups this week, reigniting complaints about a rodent infestationa situation in which an area is overrun by rats or mice鼠患 that residents say has lingered for years. Neighbours in the clip's comments blamed the illegal feeding of pigeonsgiving food to pigeons in a public place where it is prohibited by law違例餵飼野鳩 inside the park for leaving behind food scraps that, they argued, continue to attract rats despite repeated complaints to the district's environmental hygiene office.

After the video spread, the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department inspected the park alongside a district councillor and confirmed signs of rat activity, including several burrows found in the park's flower beds, which officials described as a health hazarda condition or object that poses a risk of illness or injury衞生隱患. The department issued a formal notice to abatean official order requiring someone to remove or correct a nuisance within a set time消除蟲鼠通知 under the Public Health and Municipal Services Ordinance, giving its pest control contractora company hired to remove or control insects, rodents or other unwanted creatures滅鼠承辦商 until 3 November to eliminate conditions conducive to rats, warning that a default notice would follow if the work was unsatisfactory, given the park's status as a known black spota location officially recognised as having a recurring or severe version of a particular problem已知黑點 for rodents since at least 2024.

中文翻譯

一段短片顯示約十隻老鼠在大角咀櫻桃街公園內四處走動，本週在本地Facebook社區群組瘋傳，再次引起居民對公園內鼠患問題的不滿，指情況已持續多年。片段留言區有街坊指出，公園內有人違例餵飼野鳩，遺下大量食物殘渣，認為這正是即使屢次向區內環境衞生部門投訴，老鼠仍不斷聚集的原因。

短片流傳後，食物環境衞生署聯同區議員一同巡查公園，確認有老鼠出現的跡象，包括在多個花槽發現鼠洞，署方形容情況構成衞生隱患。食環署根據《公眾衞生及市政條例》發出正式的消除蟲鼠通知，要求其滅鼠承辦商在十一月三日前除去可致鼠患的因素，並警告若表現不理想，將發出失責通知書；該公園自二零二四年區議會資料顯示起，已被列為鼠患的已知黑點。

DSE writing angles 寫作角度
  • Reactive, video-driven enforcement 由短片驅動的被動執法
    The department only inspected Cherry Street Park once the clip had gathered public attention, despite the area being flagged as a rodent black spot in 2024 district council papers. Enforcement that follows viral exposure, rather than scheduled monitoring, raises doubts about whether known problem sites receive adequate routine attention before residents feel compelled to film evidence themselves.
    食環署僅在短片引起公眾關注後，才對櫻桃街公園展開巡查，儘管該處早於二零二四年區議會文件中已被列為鼠患黑點。這種待短片引起關注才執法、而非定期監察的做法，令人質疑已知的問題地點，在居民被迫自行拍片曝光之前，是否得到足夠的日常跟進。
  • Blame falls on anonymous pigeon-feeders, not shared upkeep 責任歸咎於匿名餵鳥者而非共同維護責任
    Online commenters quickly singled out unnamed pigeon-feeders as the sole cause of the rat problem, rather than treating the park's upkeep as a shared neighbourhood responsibility. Framing the issue around a convenient, faceless villain lets residents vent frustration without confronting harder questions about littering, irregular refuse collection, or whether anyone reports problems early enough to prevent them escalating.
    網上留言迅速將責任全歸咎於不知名的餵鳥者，而非將公園的維護視為社區共同的責任。將問題簡化為一個方便、不具名的「元兇」，讓居民得以宣洩不滿，卻無需面對更棘手的問題，例如亂拋垃圾、垃圾收集安排是否不定時，或者居民是否有及早舉報問題，以免事件惡化。
  • Rats beside a children's play area raise a disease risk 鼠患與兒童遊樂設施相鄰帶來的衞生風險
    Cherry Street Park includes play equipment used daily by young children, and rodents are known carriers of diseases such as leptospirosis, spread through contaminated soil or surfaces. A rat colony nesting in flower beds metres from where children play turns an apparent nuisance into a genuine public-health concern deserving faster action than a single clean-up notice.
    櫻桃街公園設有供兒童日常使用的遊樂設施，而老鼠是鈎端螺旋體病等疾病的已知傳播者，可透過受污染的泥土或表面傳播。鼠群在距離兒童遊玩地點僅數米的花槽築巢，令看似輕微的滋擾，變成需要比一張清理通知更迅速處理的真正公共衞生隱患。
  • A ten-second clip may overstate a routine, already-managed problem 十秒短片或誇大了一個本已受控的日常問題
    A brief video filmed from one angle cannot show how often the park is normally inspected, and the department's swift response suggests existing monitoring was not entirely absent. Treating every viral clip as proof of official neglect risks diverting limited pest-control resources toward whichever problem trends online that week, rather than toward the locations that genuinely pose the greatest risk.
    一段從單一角度拍攝的短片，並不能反映公園日常巡查的頻率，而食環署迅速回應亦顯示現行監察並非完全缺失。若將每段瘋傳短片都視為官方失職的證據，有可能令本已有限的滅鼠資源，被導向當週網上熱議的地點，而非真正風險最高的地方。

💉✨ Hong Kong's 'Cadaver Skin' Beauty Jab Trend Triggers Health Crackdown

Social Trend·23 Oct 2026
English

A skincare treatment known online as the 'cadaver skin jab' has become one of the most talked-about beauty trends among Hong Kong's anti-ageing crowd this month, promoted through glossy social media posts and promises of 'structural replenishment' for sagging skin. The product, Elravie Re2O, is derived from processed human tissue sourced from deceased donors and marketed as a premium alternative to conventional collagen boosters, typically priced at around HK$3,480 per session.

The buzz came to an abrupt halt this week when the Department of Health and police raided four beauty establishments across different districts, seizing 20 vials of the product along with more than 70 other unregistered injectables. Investigators found that several sellers had failed to clearly disclose the human-derived origin of the treatment, and that at least one premises offered the jab without a doctor present, prompting fresh questions about how such trends spread unchecked before regulators catch up.

中文翻譯

一款網上稱為「屍皮針」的護膚療程,本月成為香港抗衰老愛好者之間熱議的美容趨勢,透過精美的社交媒體帖文宣傳,聲稱能為鬆弛皮膚帶來「結構性修復」效果。該產品名為Elravie Re2O,由已故捐贈者的人體組織加工製成,被包裝為傳統膠原蛋白療程的升級版本,每次療程收費約3,480港元。

然而,這股熱潮本周突然被打斷:衞生署及警方聯合突擊搜查四間位於不同地區的美容機構,檢獲20支上述產品,以及超過70件其他未經註冊的注射劑。調查發現,部分銷售者未有清楚披露療程涉及人體組織來源,其中至少一間店舖在沒有醫生在場的情況下,為顧客注射該產品,事件亦引來外界質疑,這類潛在風險趨勢為何總能在監管機構介入前已廣泛流傳。

DSE writing angles 寫作角度
  • Unregulated injectables slipping past safety checks
    The raids exposed how easily unlicensed clinics can obtain and administer invasive cosmetic products without proper medical oversight. Students could examine how gaps in enforcement, rather than the absence of regulation itself, allow risky treatments to reach consumers, and argue for stricter inspection regimes at the point of sale rather than after harm occurs.
  • Dual regulatory gap between transplant and pharmaceutical law
    Because the product straddles organ-donation rules and medicine regulations, authorities only acted after public complaints rather than through routine monitoring. This case offers material for discussing how overlapping legal categories can create blind spots, and whether Hong Kong needs a single, clearer licensing pathway for tissue-derived cosmetic products.
  • Influencer marketing obscuring a product's true origin
    Sellers reportedly described the injection only as 'human collagen', omitting that it derives from cadaver tissue, a disclosure gap that online promotion made easy to overlook. Students could explore how curated beauty content on social media discourages consumers from questioning a product's actual composition before paying for it.
  • Demand reflects legitimate anti-ageing need, not mere vanity
    Critics who dismiss users as reckless overlook that ageing skin concerns are a genuine, widespread issue, and that many buyers sought a seemingly advanced option rather than acting frivolously. A counter-argument holds that the real failure lies with inadequate consumer protection, not with individuals seeking effective skincare solutions.

😡📱 Viral 'Family Discipline' Alley Beating Clip Ignites Vigilante Justice Debate

Social Trend·22 Oct 2026
English

A short video showing a teenager being knocked to the ground and repeatedly slapped in a Hong Kong alley, while three bystanders stood by and did nothing, has become one of the most argued-over clips on local social media this week. The man seen attacking the teenager wore a cap and struck him both with an open hand and on the head, all while shouting at him; the person who uploaded the footage gave no account of when or where it happened, captioning it only as ‘time for family discipline’ — language that immediately split opinion between viewers who assumed it showed a parent disciplining a wayward child and those who saw nothing but an unprovoked assault dressed up in more acceptable-sounding words.

The ambiguity is precisely what has kept the clip circulating. Commenters have pressed the uploader to explain who the attacker was and whether anyone had reported the incident to police, while others have pointed out that filming and sharing an assault, rather than intervening or calling for help, risks turning real violence into entertainment. With neither the people involved nor the exact circumstances ever confirmed, the clip has become less a single verified incident than a mirror for whatever assumptions viewers already hold about discipline, masculinity and family authority in Hong Kong.

中文翻譯

一段片段顯示,一名少年在香港一條後巷內被推倒在地並遭多次掌摑,三名途人在場卻無人阻止,片段近日在本地社交媒體上引起熾熱爭論。片中一名戴着帽子的男子衝向少年,用手掌及拳頭擊打其頭部,同時高聲喝罵;上載片段者並未透露事發的時間或地點,僅以「教仔」(家庭教訓)作為描述——這種說法,立即令網民意見兩極:有人假設片中情景只是家長教訓頑劣子女,亦有人則認為,這純粹是一宗以較「順耳」字眼包裝的無故襲擊事件。

正是這種模糊性,令片段持續被轉發。有網民追問上載者,片中施襲者的身份,以及事件有否已經報警;另有網民則指出,目擊襲擊卻只顧拍片上載、而非上前阻止或求助,恐會令真實的暴力行為淪為娛樂內容。由於片中人物身份及事發經過始終未獲證實,這段片段與其說是一宗經核實的單一事件,倒更似一塊鏡子,映照出香港社會對「教養」、「男子氣概」與「家長權威」各自既有的假設與想像。

DSE writing angles 寫作角度
  • Filming an assault instead of stopping it
    Three onlookers watched the beating unfold yet none intervened or called for help; the footage exists only because someone chose to film rather than act. A Paper 2 essay could ask whether smartphones have quietly replaced a moral duty to help a stranger with a reflex to document them instead, and what that substitution costs a community.
  • 'Family discipline' as a euphemism that reframes violence
    Calling the beating 'family discipline' let the uploader present unverified, uncontextualised violence as something socially acceptable, without ever confirming who the people involved were. Students could explore how a single caption can steer thousands of viewers toward forgiving, or even endorsing, an act they would otherwise condemn, and why audiences rarely demand evidence before reacting.
  • Calling it discipline doesn't exempt it from assault law
    However the clip was captioned, repeatedly striking a minor's head in public meets the legal threshold for assault, regardless of any claimed disciplinary motive. An essay could examine why online audiences debated the footage for days while seemingly few considered reporting it, and what that says about public reliance on formal authorities.
  • Counter-argument: online outrage can misfire and punish the wrong person
    Without confirmed identities, a wave of comments demanding the attacker be named risks misdirecting anger at an innocent bystander wrongly assumed to be him, a pattern seen in past Hong Kong cases of mistaken online identification. A sharper essay might argue that collective fury, however well-intentioned, is a poor substitute for a verified police investigation and due process.

🍺 Viral Video of Girl Carrying Beer Cans Sparks Debate on Child Welfare and Online Shaming

Social Trend·16 Oct 2026
English

A video showing a child struggling to carry two bulging bags of canned beer through a Tuen Mun housing estate has become one of the most discussed clips on Hong Kong social media this month, after it was shared on Facebook and quickly reposted across other platforms. Viewers who could not identify the child by name nonetheless debated her situation at length, and within days police had arrested her parents on suspicion of ill-treatment, with the case referred to a dedicated child abuse investigation unit.

The family's own explanation — that the exercise was meant to build the ten-year-old's muscles and improve her coordination — did little to settle the argument. If anything, it opened a second front: once commentators learned the household receives welfare support, attention shifted from the girl's wellbeing to scrutiny of her parents' spending, turning a child-protection case into a wider, more uncomfortable conversation about poverty, judgement and how much the public is entitled to know about a stranger's home life before a single video goes viral.

中文翻譯

一段片段顯示,一名女童在屯門一個公共屋邨內,艱難地拖着兩大袋脹鼓鼓的罐裝啤酒前行,片段在Facebook上載後迅速被轉發至其他平台,成為本月香港社交媒體上最熱門的討論話題之一。即使未能知悉女童的姓名,網民仍就其處境爭論不休;數日之內,警方以涉嫌對女童疏忽照顧或虐待拘捕其父母,案件已轉交專責的虐待兒童調查組跟進。

一家人的解釋——指該安排旨在幫助這名十歲女童訓練肌肉及改善協調能力——未能平息爭議,反而掀起另一輪討論:當網民得悉該家庭正領取社會福利援助後,輿論焦點便由女童的福祉,轉移至其父母的消費方式,一宗兒童保護事件,因而演變成一場更廣泛、亦更令人不安的對話,探討貧窮、社會評判,以及公眾在一段影片爆紅之前,究竟有多大「權利」去窺探陌生人的家庭生活。

DSE writing angles 寫作角度
  • Filming a child without consent before judging the parents
    Before any welfare concern was raised, a stranger's camera turned a struggling child into unwitting content, letting millions scrutinise her face and home life without consent. A Paper 2 essay could ask whether online justice depends on first violating the very child it claims to protect, and whether platforms should restrict footage identifying minors altogether.
  • CSSA scrutiny turning poverty into public spectacle
    Once commentators learned the family received welfare payments, debate shifted from the girl's safety to whether poor parents deserve public sympathy at all, with some accusing them of squandering aid on beer. A student could argue this reveals a harsher standard applied to low-income families than to wealthier ones caught in similar disputes.
  • Child protection reporting thresholds under the spotlight
    The case only reached a formal child abuse unit after a video had already spread online, raising the question of how many similar situations go unreported without viral exposure. A writer could examine whether Hong Kong's current thresholds for intervention rely too heavily on public visibility rather than routine welfare checks.
  • Counter-argument: viral outrage as a crude stand-in for child-welfare law
    A student could push back against treating the backlash itself as a victory, arguing that a short clip cannot capture years of context a social worker would assess, and that trial-by-comment-section risks punishing parents based on appearances rather than evidence, while leaving the underlying welfare system no better equipped for the next case.

🥊🚇 'MTR MMA' Clip Has Hongkongers Joking About a City on Edge

Social Trend·15 Oct 2026
English

A shaky video of two men trading punches inside a crowded MTR carriage has become this week's go-to shorthand for Hong Kong's fraying nerves, racking up shares across Threads and Facebook groups since Tuesday. Neither the cause of the fight nor its exact time and station are known, but the footage shows one passenger pinning the other to the floor by the neck before continuing to throw punches, while the rest of the carriage reportedly stood by and watched without intervening.

What turned a short, ugly clip into a proper talking point was less the violence itself than the reaction underneath it: commenters joked that the city should "just hold an MMA tournament on the MTR already," turning a real physical assault into a punchline about how short-tempered public life has become. Others focused less on the joke and more on the silence of the surrounding passengers, several of whom appear in the footage filming on their phones rather than stepping in.
