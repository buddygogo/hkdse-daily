Hong Kong Needs More AI Talent to Drive Growth, Financial Secretary Says

RTHK ·27 September 2026

Financial Secretary Paul Chan has stated that artificial intelligence is set to reshape the job marketto fundamentally change the nature of employment opportunities重塑就業市場 in Hong Kong, generating fresh employment opportunities while simultaneously fuelling economic growthdriving or accelerating economic expansion推動經濟增長. Speaking at the weekend, Mr Chan pointed to a striking year-on-year increase of 53 percent in exports of AI-related electronic products in August alone, a statistic he described as clear evidence that the sector is fast becoming a cornerstonea fundamental and essential part of something基石 of the local economy. This growth, he suggested, reflects surging global demand for hardware manufactured in the city and across the wider region.

It is widely argued that Hong Kong's long-term competitiveness hinges on its capacity to cultivate a workforce equipped with genuine AI literacythe knowledge and skills needed to understand and use AI effectively人工智能素養. Mr Chan stressed that the city urgently requires professionals who not only grasp the intricaciesto fully understand the complex details of something掌握箇中複雜細節 of AI systems but who are also willing to venture into uncharted territoryto attempt something new and unexplored踏足未知領域 by exploring novel applications across industries such as finance, logistics and healthcare. Nevertheless, he was careful to note that certain human capabilities remain irreplaceableimpossible to replace with something or someone else不可替代的, even by the most sophisticated algorithms, implying that critical judgement and interpersonal skills will continue to command a premium in the labour market.

By the same token, the government has pledged to nearly double its expenditure on innovation and technology, aiming to raise research spending to three percent of GDP after 2030. Critics contend that such ambitious targets will only be met if schools and universities respond swiftly, embedding digital and AI skills into mainstream curricula rather than treating them as an afterthought. For secondary students weighing up their future careers, the message is unmistakable: those who proactively upskillto learn new or advanced skills提升技能 themselves now are far more likely to secure a competitive edgean advantage over rivals競爭優勢 in an economy being reshaped by automation at an unprecedented pace.

New School Merger Scheme Aims to Pool Resources, Not Just Rescue Shrinking Schools

The Standard ·17 September 2026

Secretary for Education Christine Choi Yuk-lin has clarified that the newly announced school merger scheme should not be viewed merely as a lifeline for shrinking institutions, but rather as a deliberate push towards better-quality education overall. Under the proposal, known as the “Confluence and Elevation – School Excellence Project,” the Education Bureau will support and incentivise suitable publicly-funded primary and secondary schools to pool their resourcesto combine resources for shared benefit集中資源 and merge, with the scheme set to launch as early as the 2027/28 academic year.

It is widely acknowledged that Hong Kong's shrinking birth ratea declining number of births in a population出生率下降 has placed considerable strain on smaller schools, many of which now struggle to fill classrooms. Nevertheless, Ms Choi was at pains to emphasise that the scheme carries no fixed quotaa set maximum limit固定配額 and is not intended solely as a rescue measure for institutions on the brink of closurevery close to shutting down瀕臨結業. Rather, she argued, sponsoring bodies should regard mergers as a proactive strategy, enabling schools to consolidate teaching expertiseto strengthen and combine teaching skills and knowledge整合教學專業知識, share facilities and offer a broader curriculum than either institution could sustain alone.

By the same token, officials have promised attractive financial and administrative incentivesmonetary and procedural benefits offered to encourage action財政及行政誘因 to encourage early participation, including a reduction in bureaucratic red tapeexcessive rules and procedures that cause delay官僚繁文縟節. Critics contend, however, that mergers can be disruptive for students and teachers alike, particularly where two distinct school cultures must be reconciled. For families weighing up school choices in the years ahead, the debate over consolidation versus continuity is likely to remain a pressing concern, with far-reaching implicationsconsequences that extend widely深遠影響 for the shape of Hong Kong's education landscape.

Hong Kong's Changing Climate in 7 Charts: From Quadrupled 'Very Hot Days' to Record Rainfall

HKFP ·28 September 2026

A newly published analysis by Hong Kong Free Press has laid bare the extent to which the city's climate has shifted over the past two decades. Whereas Hong Kong recorded an average of just 13.4 “very hot days” per year in the 2000s, days on which the maximum temperature reaches 33 degrees or above, this figure has quadrupledincreased to four times the original amount增加至四倍 to more than 50 such days annually since 2020. The summer of 2026 alone brought a record-breaking heatwavea period of extreme heat that surpasses previous records破紀錄熱浪, underscoring a trend that meteorologists warn is unlikely to reverse.

It is widely argued that rainfall patterns have grown equally erraticirregular and unpredictable反覆無常. This year has seen 15 red rainstorm warningsan official alert for heavy rain in Hong Kong, second-highest tier紅色暴雨警告 and three black rainstorm warningsthe highest-tier official alert for extremely heavy rain in Hong Kong黑色暴雨警告, the second-highest tally on record for both categories. In June, the Observatory issued two black rainstorm warnings within a single day for the first time in the city's history, while July's rainfall total ranked as the second-highest ever recorded for that month. Such extremes, scientists contend, are consistent with broader predictions of a warming climate marked by intensified precipitation eventsinstances of rainfall that have become more severe加劇的降雨事件.

Nevertheless, there are grounds for cautious optimism: overall air quality has continued to improve, with concentrations of major pollutants steadily declining in recent years. By the same token, the government's pledge to achieve carbon neutralityachieving a net-zero balance of carbon dioxide emissions碳中和 before 2050 remains, critics argue, short on concrete implementation details. As extreme weather becomes an increasingly familiar feature of Hong Kong life, the pressure is mounting on policymakers to translate long-term targets into tangible, near-term action, particularly given the disproportionate impact of heatwaves and flooding on vulnerable communitiesgroups of people at greater risk of harm from adverse conditions弱勢社群 across the city.

Tai Hang's 147-Year-Old Fire Dragon Dance Lights Up Mid-Autumn Festival

Zolima CityMag ·24 September 2026

Hong Kong's Mid-Autumn Festival celebrations reached a spectacular climax this year as the Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, now in its 147th edition, once again wound its way through the narrow lanes of Causeway Bay. A 67-metre dragon, studded with more than 12,000 glowing incense sticks and borne aloft by roughly 300 performers, snaked along Wun Sha Street and Tung Lo Wan Road over three consecutive nights, drawing large crowds eager to witness a custom that has endured, largely unchanged, since 1880.

It is widely believed that the ritual, inscribed on the National List of Intangible Cultural Heritagetraditions or living expressions inherited from ancestors and passed to descendants非物質文化遺產, was originally performed to ward off misfortuneto prevent bad luck or disaster from occurring驅除厄運 following a plague that struck the village generations ago. Nevertheless, the tradition has evolved considerably over the decades, this year culminating in a grand finale processionthe climactic closing parade of an event壓軸巡遊 into Victoria Park, where the dragon joined an International Lantern Spectacular making its debut at the venue. Tickets for reserved seating along the finale route sold out well in advanceall tickets were purchased long before the event早已售罄, though organisers ensured that street-side viewing remained free and open to all.

By the same token, the festival highlights the delicate balancea fine equilibrium between competing considerations微妙的平衡 Hong Kong must strike between preserving age-old customs and accommodating modern tourism demands. Community elders argue that intergenerational involvementparticipation across different age groups, especially young and old跨代參與, with children carrying miniature LED dragons alongside veteran performers, is essential if such heritage practices are to be passed down rather than fade into obsolescenceto gradually become outdated and disappear逐漸式微並被淘汰. For students studying Hong Kong's cultural identity, the fire dragon dance offers a vivid case study in how tradition can be sustained through active community participation rather than passive nostalgia.

🥤 The Viral 'Hong Kong Sprite Iced Tea' Recipe Sweeping Social Media

Social Trend

A deceptively simple drink has taken TikTok and Instagram by storm: the ‘Hong Kong Sprite Iced Tea’, made by steeping black tea bags directly inside a bottle of Sprite before adding ice and a slice of fresh lemon. Searches for the recipe have surgedincreased suddenly and sharply急升 by over 200 percent, and the trend has proven so lucrative that a soft drinks manufacturer has since launched ‘Spritea’, a bottled version of the fizzy, tea-infused concoctiona mixture of various ingredients混合飲品, in both China and Hong Kong. Commentators note that the drink's viral appeal lies in its novelty and accessibilitythe qualities of being new/original and easy to obtain or do新穎性與易得性, requiring no specialist equipment and drawing on ingredients already found in most households. For many overseas creators, the trend has also sparked curiosity about Hong Kong's distinctive tea culture, transforming a humble local recipe into a piece of the city's soft-power exporta cultural product that enhances a place's international influence軟實力輸出.

HKDSE Writing Angles
  • Psychological appeal: The surprising, counter-intuitive combination of a fizzy soft drink and tea taps into curiosity and a desire for novel sensory experiences, encouraging viewers to try the recipe themselves.
  • Social media influence: Short-form video platforms reward visually satisfying, easy-to-replicate content, and the drink's vivid colour and simple preparation make it especially shareable.
  • Consumer culture: The trend illustrates how viral online content can directly shape purchasing behaviour, prompting manufacturers to commercialise grassroots trends into branded products.
