Hong Kong Tech Chief Warns AI Will Unleash 'Greatest Industrial Revolution'

South China Morning Post ·10 September 2026

Hong Kong's Secretary for Innovation, Technology and Industry, Sun Dong, has issued a stark warningto give an unusually serious warning about something發出嚴厲警告 that artificial intelligence will trigger a transformation exceeding all previous industrial revolutions. Speaking at the third GTI Forum on Digital Intelligence held in Hong Kong, Mr Sun told the South China Morning Post that "the impact of this AI wave on Hong Kong and its entire society will, I believe, exceed all previous industrial revolutions." It is widely argued that such remarks, coming from the city's top technology official, underscoreto emphasise or draw attention to the importance of something強調 the scale of disruption authorities now anticipate across every sector of the economy.

By the same token, the government insists it is not standing idle. Hong Kong has already rolled outto introduce a new product, scheme or policy officially推出 a HK$50 million universal AI education programme, established an AI supercomputing centre, and launched a HK$3 billion funding scheme intended to nurture homegrown large language models. Nevertheless, critics contend that training initiatives remain grossly insufficientseriously inadequate in scale or amount嚴重不足 given the pace of change, with some reports suggesting reskilling schemes currently reach barely one percent of the workforce. Mr Sun himself conceded that certain traditional sectors will "gradually decline," which he described as an inevitable outcomea result that cannot be avoided必然結果 of technological progress, even as new opportunities emerge elsewhere. For students weighing future careers, the debate poses a pressing questionto present an issue that urgently needs to be addressed提出迫切問題: how should Hong Kong prepare its next generation of workers for a labour market being reshaped faster than policy can keep pace?

HKTB to Host First International Lantern Event This Mid-Autumn

The Standard ·20 August 2026

Hong Kong is preparing its most ambitious Mid-Autumn Festival celebration to date, according to a report in The Standard. The Hong Kong Tourism Board will stage the city's first-ever International Lantern Spectacular at Victoria Park from 17 to 27 September, under the theme "Reunion". More than 3,000 lanterns from twenty countries and regions will line the newly created World Lantern Boulevard, including silk lanterns from Jinju in South Korea, patchwork designs from Brazil, and Polish lanterns made using traditional folk paper-cutting techniques.

It is widely argued that the initiative reflects a deliberate attempt to harnessto control and make use of something for a particular purpose善用；運用 Hong Kong's intangible cultural heritage as a tourism asset, rather than treating tradition and economic development as separate concerns. The centrepiece attraction, an eight-metre lantern named "Shadowlight" inspired by the city's iconic banyan trees, sits at the park's main entrance, its layered gauze panels intended to representto stand for or symbolise something象徵 the tree's growth in three-dimensional form.

By the same token, heritage advocates note that the festivities coincide with the 147th edition of the Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, running from 24 to 26 September. A 67-metre dragon studded withcovered or decorated with many small objects點綴著；鑲滿 roughly 12,000 burning incense sticks will be carried through Tai Hang's narrow streets by generations of local performers, a practice inscribed onofficially recorded or listed as part of an official register列入 China's National List of Intangible Cultural Heritage. Nevertheless, some commentators caution that as such events are increasingly packaged for visitorspresented or marketed in a way designed to appeal to tourists為遊客包裝, organisers must take care not to let commercial appeal come at the expense ofto cause harm or loss to something else in the process以……為代價 the ritual's original spiritual significance — a tension that, arguably, defines the challenge of preserving living heritage in a modern, tourism-driven city.

Hong Kong Embraces Workforce Shift Amid AI Revolution

China Daily (Hong Kong Edition) ·24 August 2026

Hong Kong's labour market is undergoing what China Daily describes as a fundamental shift, as artificial intelligence adoption accelerates across industries far faster than most employers anticipated. Roughly a quarter of local organisations now report widely deployingto put a resource, system or technology into active use部署 AI in their operations, three times the proportion recorded just a year earlier, while understanding and applying the technology has become, according to surveyed executives, the most crucial skill demanded of employees at every level of seniority.

It is widely argued that this shift carries a double edgehaving both a beneficial and a harmful aspect at the same time雙面刃. Merchandise exports of AI-related electronic products have grown sharply, and hundreds of companies have set up or expanded local operations this year alone, suggesting the technology is fuelling genuine economic momentum. Nevertheless, critics contend that the transition is unevenly distributednot spread equally or fairly across a group分布不均: nearly forty percent of firms remain confined to small-scale pilot projects, while full-time vacancies suitable for university graduates have fallen by roughly sixty percent since 2022, with entry-level administrative roles hit hardest of allaffected most severely compared to others受影響最深.

By the same token, employers appear notably more cautiouscareful to avoid risk or danger謹慎 about future hiring than in previous years, with a larger share now expecting headcount reductions than increases — the weakest outlook in recent memory. For secondary school students weighing their eventual career paths, the implication seems clear: technical literacy in AI is fast becoming a baseline expectationa minimum standard that is assumed or required基本要求 rather than a specialised advantage, and traditional entry routes into the workforce can no longer be taken for granted.

💃 "Feel Like Dance" Challenge Sweeps Hong Kong Feeds

Social Trend

A viral dance format known as the "Feel Like Dance Challenge" has taken over Hong Kong TikTok and Instagram feeds this September, encouraging users to recreateto make or do something again in the same way重現 retro choreography set against 1990s-style pop tracks, often filmed at Hong Kong Disneyland during its anniversary celebrations. Clips typically open with an ordinary, static pose before cutting to an exaggerated, synchronisedhappening or moving at exactly the same time or rate同步的 routine, a format that rewards both comic timing and genuine dance skill in equal measure.

The trend has proven especially popular among secondary school students filming with friends after school or during weekend visits to the park, with many adding their own captions poking fun atteasing or joking about something in a light-hearted way取笑 how differently the "before" and "after" versions of themselves appear on camera.

  • Psychological appeal: The dramatic before-and-after structure delivers a quick, satisfying payoff that rewards repeat viewing and imitation.

  • Consumer culture: Tying the trend to a specific attraction encourages theme-park visits, turning a nostalgic dance format into a driver of tourism and merchandise spending.
